# Business Acumen

## Definition

Business acumen is the ability to understand how an organization creates value, how decisions affect commercial or social outcomes, and how evidence should be connected to strategy. In market research, it includes understanding the client's operating context, identifying the decision behind a research request, prioritizing implications, and communicating findings in terms that support action.

## Evidence from the MRII study

The [[mrii-2026-market-research-study]] identifies a skills-gap mismatch between what insights professionals most want to learn and what senior leaders say drives impact. Professionals prioritize AI and digital skills, advanced analytics, and technical capabilities. Leaders place greater emphasis on business acumen, communication, influencing decisions, and connecting insights to outcomes.

The study's central argument is that AI will make answers faster, cheaper, and more accessible. As a result, the differentiating value of an insights professional moves upstream, toward shaping decisions, driving growth, building systems that leverage AI, and influencing strategy and action. Business acumen is the connective skill that allows technical analysis to become a business intervention rather than an isolated deliverable.

## Relationship to AI and human-intensive skills

Business acumen complements [[ai-skills]] rather than replacing them. A researcher may know how to use a language model, automate a survey analysis, or build a dashboard, yet still fail to identify the most consequential question or recommend an actionable response. The capability also overlaps with the [[value-chain-shift]] and with PwC's emphasis on judgment, leadership, and strategic decision-making in increasingly [[seniorised-roles|seniorised]] entry-level jobs.

## Development implications

Organizations can build business acumen through cross-functional project work, client exposure, decision-focused research briefs, post-project reviews, commercial case discussions, and mentoring by senior researchers and business stakeholders. Training should assess not only analytical correctness but also whether a professional can frame a decision, explain trade-offs, and demonstrate how insight changes action.
