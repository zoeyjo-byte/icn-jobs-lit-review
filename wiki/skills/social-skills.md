# [[social-skills]]

## 2026-08-26 Update

Human capacities related to forming meaningful relationships, understanding others' perspectives, and participating in communal life. Expanded with detailed analysis from Ferdman's structural perspective on capacity development, including embodiment requirements, AI threats in research contexts, and mitigation strategies as detailed in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]].

### Components
1. **Moral capacity**:
   - Recognizing others as moral agents
   - Paying moral attention to research participants
   - Understanding ethical dimensions of research situations
   - Making morally informed decisions about research design

2. **Relational capacity**:
   - Forming meaningful relationships with research participants
   - Understanding diverse perspectives and cultural contexts
   - Building trust and rapport in research interactions
   - Participating effectively in research teams and client relationships

### Embodiment Requirement
Social skills fundamentally require embodiment:
- "Embodied understanding" where perceived bodies are recognized as similar loci of subjectivity
- Spontaneous recognition of others as moral agents through embodied interaction
- The return of the gaze essential for moral attention in research contexts
- Participatory sense-making constitutive of social cognition
- Shared history created through physical presence in research settings

### AI Threats in Research Contexts
Ferdman identifies specific AI-related threats:
- **Disembodied research methods**: Digital platforms replacing face-to-face respondent interaction
- **Algorithmic matching**: Systems that match researchers with respondents without embodied engagement
- **Virtual collaboration tools**: Platforms reducing opportunities for spontaneous team interaction
- **Automated analysis**: Tools processing human responses without requiring direct researcher engagement

### Market Research Examples
- Online survey platforms eliminating opportunities to observe respondent body language and emotional cues
- AI-powered respondent matching reducing need for nuanced understanding through direct interaction
- Virtual focus groups lacking embodied presence necessary for deep rapport building
- Automated transcription tools processing interviews without requiring engagement with emotional dimensions

### Mitigation Strategies
- Preserve opportunities for face-to-face research methods where appropriate
- Design hybrid research approaches combining digital tools with embodied interaction
- Create organizational practices valuing embodied research experiences
- Develop training emphasizing physical presence importance in research quality
- Implement debriefing sessions where researchers share embodied fieldwork experiences

### Connection to Capacity Development
Social skills develop through:
- Embodied, intersubjective habituation process
- Shared valuing through mentorship and community
- Opportunities for spontaneous interaction with research participants
- Physical presence enabling participatory sense-making
- Return of the gaze facilitating moral recognition in research contexts

Ferdman's framework emphasizes that preserving social skills in market research requires structural solutions maintaining opportunities for embodied interaction. Organizations must recognize that some aspects of human understanding cannot be fully captured through disembodied digital methods. The value of market research insights depends not just on data but on the social skill exercise that enables researchers to understand human behavior in context. Capacity-conducive research environments intentionally preserve spaces for embodied research practice, recognizing that social skills are essential for producing genuinely human-centered insights. This requires balancing AI efficiency with the need for spaces where researchers can develop the nuanced understanding of human behavior that forms the foundation of quality market research.
