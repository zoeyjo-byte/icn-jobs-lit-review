# Social Capacities

**Social capacities** encompass the moral, caring, and relational abilities required to develop meaningful friendships, practice empathy, and engage in cooperative social practices. They are deeply intertwined with moral reasoning and the formation of shared values.

## Key Aspects
- **Moral Attention**: The ability to perceive others as embodied moral agents, requiring spontaneous recognition through gaze, voice, and physical presence.
- **Caring and Friendship**: The capacity to form deep, reciprocal relationships that provide reasons for action and contribute to human flourishing.
- **Intersubjective Valuing**: Learning to value skills and moral principles through shared experiences and communal practices, rather than through isolated or algorithmic mediation.

## AI and Social Deskilling
Disembodied interactions, such as those mediated by AI chatbots or social media platforms, make it harder to form meaningful friendships and practice moral attention. AI mediation can decrease the opportunity to develop capacities in concert with other humans, leading to a diminished experience of virtue friendship and shared valuing. This contributes to **[[capacity-hostile-environments]]** where social and moral capacities atrophy due to lack of embodied, intersubjective practice.
