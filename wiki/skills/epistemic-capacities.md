# Epistemic Capacities

**Epistemic capacities** refer to the human abilities involved in knowing, reasoning, forming beliefs, and exercising practical wisdom. They are foundational to competent decision-making, critical thinking, and the exercise of practical reason.

## Core Dimensions
- **Theoretical Rationality**: The ability to think, consider reasons to believe, form beliefs, and contemplate complex ideas. This capacity is essential for self-deliberation and evaluating the testimony of others.
- **Practical Rationality (Phronesis)**: Often translated as wisdom or common sense, this involves doing the right thing in the right way for the right reason. It requires an accurate understanding of one's situation, discernment of good and bad outcomes, and the ability to coordinate intentions with actions.

## AI and Epistemic Deskilling
Over-reliance on AI tools for information retrieval, decision-making, and analysis has been linked to the erosion of critical thinking, analytical reasoning, and activity awareness. When AI mediates epistemic activities, users may experience automation bias, declining retention of tacit knowledge, and weakened moral judgment. The loss of agential control over epistemic processes contributes to **[[capacity-hostile-environments]]** and **[[affordance-shrinkage]]**.
