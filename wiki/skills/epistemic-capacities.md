# Epistemic Capacities

**Definition:** Theoretical rationality (thinking, considering reasons to believe, forming beliefs) and practical rationality (phronesis/common sense or wisdom). These capacities are essential for competent exercise of practical reason, self-worth, and mutual respect.

## Theoretical Rationality
Involves the ability to think critically, analyze information, and form justified beliefs. In the context of AI, over-reliance on LLMs has been linked to the erosion of critical thinking, decision-making, and analytical reasoning. Theoretical rationality requires active engagement with information rather than passive acceptance of AI-generated outputs.

## Practical Rationality (Phronesis)
Practical wisdom involves doing the right thing in the right way for the right reason. It requires:
- Effective means-end reasoning.
- Accurate understanding of one's situation and circumstances.
- Discernment in assessing the testimony of others.
- Critical reflection on one's conceptions of a flourishing life to set appropriate goals.

Being well-motivated is insufficient for practical wisdom; it requires real-world experience and the ability to navigate unexpected events. Constantly turning to [[entities/artificial-personal-assistants|APAs]] for advice restricts real-world experience and undermines the development of practical wisdom.

## Role in Life-Planning
Epistemic capacities are central to [[concepts/life-planning|life-planning]], which is a self-constituting activity. Planning requires exercising theoretical rationality in self-deliberation on values and goals, and practical wisdom in coordinating intentions with actions. Outsourcing this process to AI tools risks atrophying these capacities, leading to a reliance on AI outputs as triggers rather than exercising agential control.
