# AI & Society

**First Observed:** 2026-07-16  
**Last Updated:** 2026-09-25

## Definition

Academic journal published by Springer focusing on the social, ethical, and philosophical implications of artificial intelligence and digital technologies, where [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]] was published. Provides a venue for critical reflection on AI's societal impacts.

## Relevance to Market Research

The journal's focus on the philosophical and ethical dimensions of AI has direct relevance to market research:

1. **Critical evaluation framework**: Offers theoretical foundations for evaluating how AI tools impact researcher capacities
2. **Structural perspective**: Ferdman's work provides a framework for understanding deskilling as a structural problem rather than individual failing
3. **Capacity-focused analysis**: Helps market researchers assess AI tools based on their conduciveness to capacity cultivation

## Key Publication

The journal published Avigail Ferdman's landmark paper "AI deskilling is a structural problem" (2026), which:

- Introduced the concept of "capacity-hostile environments"
- Developed a framework for understanding human capacities as skills
- Analyzed how AI tools like Artificial Personal Assistants create capacity-hostile environments
- Argued for evaluating AI applications based on their conduciveness to capacity cultivation

## Usage in Research Contexts

Market research organizations can leverage AI & Society publications to:

- Develop ethical guidelines for AI adoption
- Create evaluation frameworks for AI tools that consider capacity impacts
- Understand the structural dimensions of AI's impact on research quality
- Inform training programs that preserve critical human capacities

## Contributions to AI Evaluation Frameworks

Ferdman's work in AI & Society provides specific contributions to AI evaluation:

- The distinction between capacity-hostile and capacity-conducive environments
- Criteria for evaluating whether AI tools support agential control
- Framework for assessing how AI impacts habituation processes
- Guidance for designing AI systems that encourage rather than replace human capacities
