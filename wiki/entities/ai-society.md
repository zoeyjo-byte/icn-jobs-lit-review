# AI & Society

## Overview
Academic journal published by Springer focusing on the social, ethical, and philosophical implications of artificial intelligence and digital technologies, where [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]] was published. Provides a venue for critical reflection on AI's societal impacts.

## Publication Details
- **Publisher**: Springer
- **Scope**: Interdisciplinary research on AI's social, ethical, and philosophical dimensions
- **Relevance**: Publishes theoretical and empirical work examining how AI systems affect human capacities, social structures, and ethical frameworks
- **Key Contribution**: Provides a platform for structural analyses of AI's impact beyond individual responsibility frameworks

## Ferdman Paper Context
The 2026 Ferdman paper 'AI deskilling is a structural problem' (AI & Society, 41, 3001–3013) represents a significant contribution to the journal's mission by:
- Moving beyond individual responsibility frameworks to analyze systemic conditions
- Introducing the concepts of 'capacity-hostile environments' and 'capacity-conducive environments'
- Providing a philosophical framework for understanding AI's impact on human capacities
- Analyzing how AI mediation affects the habituation process necessary for skill development

## Journal's Role in Skills Gap Research
AI & Society contributes to market research skills gap analysis by:
- Publishing theoretical work that explains why the **entry-level squeeze** occurs
- Providing frameworks for understanding the **human-operating-model-gap** as a structural issue
- Offering critical perspectives on how AI adoption affects capacity development
- Highlighting the distinction between skill obsolescence and capacity deskilling

## Connection to Market Research Practice
The journal's theoretical contributions have practical implications:
- Explains why high-intensity AI adopters must intentionally design capacity-conducive environments
- Provides the foundation for understanding how AI tools can undermine **epistemic capacities** in research
- Offers frameworks for evaluating whether AI systems create **narrow fields of affordances**
- Helps distinguish between productive automation and capacity-hostile AI implementation

## Critical Perspective
Unlike industry reports that focus on technical skills gaps, AI & Society emphasizes:
- The structural dimensions of AI's impact on human capacities
- The philosophical foundations of human flourishing in the AI age
- The societal obligation to foster environments conducive to capacity development
- The limitations of individual responsibility frameworks for addressing deskilling

## Relevance to Research Firms
Market research organizations should engage with AI & Society to:
- Understand the theoretical foundations of capacity development
- Design research workflows that preserve opportunities for skill habituation
- Evaluate whether their AI tools create capacity-conducive or capacity-hostile environments
- Develop strategies for maintaining the human capacities essential to quality research
