## Update 2026-07-25

Expanded analysis based on Ferdman's research in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]]. Artificial Personal Assistants (APAs) are AI systems based on Large Language Models designed to assist with personal organization, life planning, and decision-making, with critical implications for capacity development.

Examples include:
- Scheduling tools like Sunsama that "display tasks synced from various software, alongside your calendar and unfinished tasks... [and] schedule tasks logically into your next available time slot"
- Life-planning assistants that help users "organize my life" and "find purpose"
- Social validation tools that provide "interpersonal guidance" and "self-regulation"

APAs can create [[capacity-hostile-environments]] when they:
- Encourage users to become plan-followers rather than plan-formers
- Reduce opportunities for embodied, intersubjective habituation of capacities
- Undermine the development of practical wisdom through real-world experience
- Create narrow fields of affordances that restrict opportunities for capacity development

In market research contexts, APA-like tools may threaten epistemic, social, creative, and volitional skills when researchers become overly reliant on AI for research design, insight generation, and client communication.

See [[plan-forming-vs-plan-following]], [[capacity-hostile-environments]], and [[ai-deskilling-structural-problem]] for related concepts.
