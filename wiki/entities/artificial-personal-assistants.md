## Update (2026-08-20)

Expanded with detailed analysis from [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]]:

Artificial Personal Assistants (APAs) are AI systems based on Large Language Models designed to assist with personal organization, life planning, and decision-making. Examples include ChatGPT, Claude.ai, and Gemini used as personal assistants.

Features:
- Adapt to individual user's needs and preferences
- Tailor advice based on user's responses, habits, and preferences
- Available constantly as an "always-on sounding board"
- Used for life-planning activities like organizing daily life and finding purpose
- Integrated with other AI tools for comprehensive assistance

Usage patterns:
- Second and third highest uses of LLMs are for 'organizing my life' and 'finding purpose'
- Users rely on APAs to determine values, get past roadblocks, and self-develop
- Heavy users utilize LLMs for social validation, self-regulation, and interpersonal guidance

Capacity impact:
- May create capacity-hostile environments by encouraging plan-following rather than plan-forming
- Reduces opportunities for embodied, intersubjective habituation of capacities
- May lead to reduced sense of personal competence through offloading small-scale decisions
- Restricts real-world experience necessary for developing practical wisdom

Market research implications:
- Research firms should evaluate whether APA-like tools in research workflows undermine capacity development
- Tools like Sunsama that automatically schedule tasks risk turning researchers into plan-followers
- APA integration in research should preserve opportunities for researchers to exercise agential control
- Research training should include awareness of APA impacts on capacity development
