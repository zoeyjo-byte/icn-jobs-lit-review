# Artificial Personal Assistants

**First Observed**: 2026-07-03 | **Last Updated**: 2026-07-22

Artificial Personal Assistants (APAs) are AI systems based on Large Language Models designed to assist with personal organization, life planning, and decision-making, with critical implications for capacity development as analyzed in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]]. These tools can create capacity-hostile environments when they encourage users to become plan-followers rather than plan-formers.

## Definition and Scope
APAs are AI systems that:
- Adapt to individual user's needs and preferences
- Provide subject-matter expertise across multiple domains
- Are constantly available as "always-on sounding boards"
- Function as virtual experts in different areas working together

## Key Features

### Personalization
- Tailor advice based on user's responses, habits, and preferences
- Configure interaction with preferred tone (strict taskmaster to encouraging cheerleader)
- Learn from user behavior to refine recommendations
- Example: ChatGPT adapting to user's communication style over time

### Integration Capabilities
- Combine with other AI tools for comprehensive assistance
- Leverage AI capabilities like dynamic reasoning and real-time adaptability
- Merge synergistically discrete uses to greatly magnify scope and power
- Example: Sunsama scheduling app integrating with calendar and task management systems

## Capacity Impact Analysis

### Plan-Forming vs Plan-Following
APAs threaten capacity development by:
- Encouraging plan-following rather than plan-forming agency
- Replacing the settling function (deciding between options) and coordination function of planning
- Diminishing opportunities for self-deliberation on values and goals
- Example: Users asking APAs to "help me plan my day" rather than developing their own planning skills

### Habituation Process Disruption
- Automating routine planning tasks deprives users of opportunities to practice judgment
- Reducing need for small-scale decision-making that builds broader life-planning capacities
- Creating environments with narrow fields of affordances for capacity development
- Example: APAs scheduling tasks into available time slots, removing opportunities for self-directed planning

### Embodiment and Intersubjectivity Erosion
- Decreasing embodied, intersubjective interaction involved in life-planning activities
- Replacing mentor-mentee relationships with AI guidance
- Diminishing opportunities for shared valuing of planning capacities
- Example: Preferring APA advice over that of a friend or colleague for life decisions

## Structural Implications
The APA phenomenon reveals why deskilling is a structural problem:
- APAs are designed to be constantly available and helpful, discouraging users from seeking new growth opportunities
- Long-term reliance limits agents' ability to transition to independent planning
- Unlike human mentors, APAs don't know "when to remove themselves as training wheels"
- Example: Sunsama app that prompts users to journal about accomplishments rather than developing self-reflection skills

## Contrast with Individual-Responsibility Approaches
Unlike frameworks treating APA impacts as individual choice, the structural perspective recognizes:
- The design of APAs actively encourages plan-following through constant availability
- Users cannot be expected to resist APA convenience when environments are designed to discourage plan-forming
- Capacity-hostile environments systematically undermine opportunities for agential control
- Example: Young people growing up with smartphones already struggle with essential skills like time management and problem-solving

## Preservation Strategies
Mitigating APA risks requires:
- Designing APAs as scaffolds rather than replacements for planning activities
- Creating environments that encourage rather than discourage initiating planning
- Preserving opportunities for embodied, intersubjective planning with human mentors
- Supporting public spaces that facilitate face-to-face planning and coordination
- Example: Using APAs for task management while preserving self-directed goal-setting
