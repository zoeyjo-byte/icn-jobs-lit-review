## Update: 2026-09-15

[[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]] provides a detailed analysis of how Artificial Personal Assistants (APAs) based on Large Language Models can create [[capacity-hostile-environments]] when they encourage users to become plan-followers rather than plan-formers.

The paper cites evidence that the second and third highest uses of LLMs are for life-planning: "organizing my life" and "finding purpose." Users rely on APAs to:
- Determine and define their values
- Get past roadblocks and self-develop
- Seek advice on what to do next
- Reframe problems and stay focused
- Provide social validation and self-regulation

Ferdman analyzes APAs through the lens of [[plan-forming-vs-plan-following]]:
- **Plan-forming** involves self-deliberation, requires exercising [[agential-control]], and engages multiple capacities ([[epistemic-capacities]], [[social-capacities]], [[creative-capacities]], [[volitional-capacity]])
- **Plan-following** involves responding to triggers in the environment (APA outputs) without initiating the planning process

A concrete example provided is Sunsama, a scheduling app that:
- Displays tasks synced from various software
- Prompts users to prioritize work and link tasks to objectives
- Schedules tasks into available time slots
- Reminds users when to go to meetings or take breaks
- Prompts journal entries about accomplishments

While convenient, over-reliance on such tools can narrow the field of affordances for capacity development by:
1. Reducing opportunities for small-scale decision-making that builds competence
2. Encouraging habitual responses to AI prompts rather than initiating plans
3. Decreasing embodied, intersubjective interaction necessary for capacity development
4. Undermining the development of practical wisdom through real-world experience

Ferdman concludes that while APAs aren't inherently problematic, their all-encompassing nature ("trained to do everything for everyone") combined with constant availability creates environments that may discourage users from seeking growth opportunities outside the APA framework.
