# Artificial Personal Assistants (APAs)

**Definition:** AI tools, primarily based on [[concepts/large-language-models|Large Language Models (LLMs)]] such as ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini, designed to adapt to individual user needs, tailor advice, and assist with life-planning, organization, and personal guidance.

## Key Features
- **Always-On Availability:** APAs act as constant sounding boards with configurable tones, from strict taskmasters to encouraging cheerleaders.
- **Adaptive Personalization:** They learn from user responses, habits, and preferences to provide highly tailored recommendations.
- **Multi-Role Functionality:** APAs can function as social companions, moral advisors, life coaches, and personal butlers simultaneously.

## Risks to Capacity Cultivation
While APAs offer convenience, heavy reliance on them poses significant risks to human capacity development:
- **Narrowing Affordance Space:** Constant availability and prompt-based interactions discourage users from seeking alternative avenues for growth, leading to [[concepts/affordance-shrinkage|affordance shrinkage]].
- **Plan-Following vs. Plan-Forming:** APAs invite users to respond to AI-generated plans rather than initiating planning themselves. This shifts the activity from agential control to habitual response, undermining the [[concepts/volitional-capacity|volitional capacity]] and other core capacities involved in planning.
- **Erosion of Practical Wisdom:** Relying on APAs for daily scheduling and moral reasoning (e.g., apps like [[entities/sunsama|Sunsama]]) deprives users of routine practice, leaving them unprepared for exceptions and diminishing their sense of personal competence.
- **Undermining Intersubjectivity:** Preferring APA advice over human interaction reduces opportunities for embodied, intersubjective habituation, which is crucial for developing shared valuing of skills and capacities.
