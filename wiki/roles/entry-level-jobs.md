Entry-level job postings in quartiles with lower AI exposure continue to grow, while those in the highest AI exposure quartile have flatlined since 2022.

## AI Adoption and Entry-Level Employment Growth

Contrary to predictions that AI would reduce entry-level opportunities, recent evidence from the [[ramp-revelio-2026-ai-jobs-impact-study]] shows that high-intensity AI adopters actually increased entry-level headcount by 12.0% over the first 24 months following AI adoption. This finding challenges simplistic narratives about AI's impact on junior positions.

### Key Findings

- High-intensity AI adopters (top tercile of AI spend per employee at $33.67/month) grew entry-level headcount by 12.0%
- Low-intensity adopters showed no statistically significant change in entry-level positions
- Entry-level share of total employment rose by 1.15 percentage points among high-intensity adopters
- Gains emerged gradually, following a learning curve as firms integrated AI tools

### Sector Variations

The employment gains were concentrated in the Information sector, where high-intensity adopters grew entry-level headcount by 13.4%. Other sectors showed minimal or non-significant effects, reflecting the uneven diffusion of AI capabilities across the economy.

### Implications for Market Research

For market research firms, these findings suggest that strategic AI investment may support growth in junior positions rather than necessitate reductions. The data indicates that AI is currently augmenting rather than replacing human capabilities in information-intensive work.

However, the findings also highlight the importance of distinguishing between low- and high-intensity AI adoption patterns. Firms making only minimal AI investments did not experience the same employment growth as those making substantial, sustained investments.
