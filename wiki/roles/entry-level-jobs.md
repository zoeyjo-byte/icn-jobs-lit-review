## Entry-Level Jobs

**First Observed:** 2026-06-24
**Last Updated:** 2026-07-03

### Definition
Entry-level positions in market research and insights organizations that typically serve as career entry points, requiring minimal prior professional experience but providing foundational training in research methodologies, client interaction, and data analysis.

### Current Trends

#### Growth Patterns
- Entry-level job postings in quartiles with lower AI exposure continue to grow
- Positions in highest AI exposure quartile have flatlined since 2022
- Contrary to displacement predictions, [[high-intensity-ai-adopters]] actually **increase entry-level headcount by 12.0%** (Ramp-Revelio 2026)
- Entry-level share rises by 1.15 percentage points among high-intensity AI adopters

#### The Entry-Level Squeeze
The [[entry-level-squeeze]] describes growing pressure on traditional entry points as AI automates foundational tasks that historically served as training grounds. This creates a paradox where:
- Firms adopting AI intensively expand their entry-level workforce
- But the nature of entry-level work is transforming, requiring different skill development pathways
- Traditional learning-through-task-automation is disrupted

### Skills Evolution
Entry-level roles increasingly demand:
- [[human-intensive-skills]] like empathy and face-to-face presence
- Ability to work alongside AI tools rather than perform automated tasks
- Stronger conceptual understanding to guide AI-assisted work
- Adaptability to rapidly changing toolsets and methodologies

### Strategic Implications
1. **Talent Pipeline Development**: Organizations must redesign onboarding to cultivate skills AI cannot replicate
2. **Mentorship Systems**: Structured guidance becomes more critical as organic learning from routine tasks diminishes
3. **Role Redefinition**: Entry positions should focus on higher-value activities from day one
4. **Measurement Shift**: Success metrics must evolve beyond task completion to value creation
