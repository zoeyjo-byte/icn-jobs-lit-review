# Embodied Habituation

**Embodied habituation** is the gradual, experiential process through which humans acquire skills and cultivate core capacities. It emphasizes that skill cannot be learned through testimony or abstract principles alone; it requires practice, feedback, and engagement with concrete cases.

## Key Components
1. **Intersubjectivity**: Skill development typically requires mentorship, shared intentions, and a communal understanding of the skill's value. Social practices and communities provide the mentoring environment necessary for cultivating proper motivational structures. Through shared valuing, both mentor and mentee come to view the skill as intrinsically valuable.
2. **Embodiment**: Successful habituation involves participatory sense-making and embodied interaction. Perceiving others as embodied moral agents and engaging in shared physical or social practices is constitutive of learning and valuing skills deeply. Disembodied interactions require more mental effort to attribute moral agency and form meaningful relationships.

## AI Implications
AI mediation risks undermining the quality of conditions for habituation by replacing embodied, intersubjective practice with disembodied, algorithmic responses. This can lead to **[[capacity-hostile-environments]]** where users interact primarily with non-human entities, limiting opportunities to develop capacities in concert with other humans. Preserving embodied habituation is essential for maintaining the shared valuing and deep experiential knowledge that underpin human flourishing.
