# [[epistemic-skills]]

## Update 2026-08-26

Human capacities related to knowledge acquisition, critical thinking, reasoning, and understanding, including theoretical rationality and practical rationality (phronesis). Expanded with detailed analysis from Ferdman's structural perspective on capacity development, including threats from AI in market research and preservation strategies.

### Components

1. **Theoretical Rationality**: Thinking, considering reasons to believe, forming beliefs, contemplating
2. **Practical Rationality (Phronesis)**: Doing the right thing in the right way for the right reason, common sense or wisdom
3. **Critical Evaluation**: Assessing information quality, identifying biases, recognizing limitations
4. **Contextual Understanding**: Interpreting information within relevant frameworks and situations
5. **Epistemic Humility**: Recognizing the limits of one's knowledge and the value of diverse perspectives

### Ferdman's Structural Perspective

Ferdman's analysis in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]] reveals how AI systems can undermine epistemic capacities through structural conditions:

- **Deskilling Mechanism**: When AI replaces critical thinking and decision-making, it creates capacity-hostile environments that restrict opportunities for developing epistemic capacities
- **Plan-Following Trap**: AI systems that provide ready-made answers encourage researchers to become plan-followers rather than plan-formers, diminishing practical rationality
- **Value Devaluation**: Without experiencing the gradual habituation process, researchers may fail to appreciate the value of deep epistemic work
- **Embodiment Requirement**: Epistemic capacities develop best through embodied interaction where researchers encounter diverse perspectives face-to-face

### Market Research Threats

1. **Automated Insights Generation**: AI tools that produce complete research reports without researcher input erode critical evaluation skills
2. **Black-Box Analytics**: Systems that provide predictions without transparent reasoning undermine practical rationality development
3. **Confirmation Bias Amplification**: AI that reinforces existing assumptions without challenging them diminishes critical thinking
4. **Reduced Contextual Understanding**: Over-reliance on quantitative AI analysis at the expense of qualitative understanding
5. **Epistemic Complacency**: Belief that AI-generated insights are sufficiently accurate, reducing motivation to develop deeper understanding

### Preservation Strategies

1. **AI as Sounding Board**: Use AI to generate initial hypotheses but require researchers to critically evaluate and refine them
2. **Transparent Methodologies**: Prioritize AI tools that explain their reasoning rather than providing opaque outputs
3. **Deliberate Disagreement**: Create team practices that intentionally surface and examine differing interpretations
4. **Embodied Sense-Making**: Maintain face-to-face client workshops and team discussions to develop contextual understanding
5. **Failure Analysis**: Implement regular practices for examining research missteps to strengthen epistemic resilience

As Ferdman argues, 'the competent exercise of the human capacities is the manifestation of the achievement of the intrinsically valuable goods, making the capacities intrinsically valuable too.' Market research organizations must preserve epistemic capacities as constitutive of high-quality insights rather than treating them as mere means to efficiency.
