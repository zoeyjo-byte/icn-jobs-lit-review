# [[intersubjectivity-in-skilling]]

## Definition
Intersubjectivity in skilling refers to the shared understanding dimension of skill development, where learning occurs through relationships with others who guide and share the value of the skill. As detailed in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]], becoming skilled typically requires a mentor to instill not only mechanical aspects but also the value of the skill, with critical implications for AI's impact on capacity development.

## Philosophical Foundation
Ferdman explains that skill habituation has a 'shared understanding' dimension:
- Becoming skilled typically requires a mentor to guide the novice into becoming an expert
- A good mentor is necessary to cultivate the proper motivational structure for becoming skilled
- In the mentor-mentee relationship, the mentor instills not only mechanical aspects but also the value of the skill
- Through habituation, the skilled agent eventually comes to view the value of the skill as their own
- Both mentor and mentee have shared intentions and come to have "shared valuing" of the skill

## Societal Dimension
The process of habituation is intersubjective also on a societal scale:
- Social practices performed by groups or whole societies provide mentoring environments
- The family, colleagues, and citizens collectively form a mentoring environment
- Social practices often generate moral reasons for action
- Learning is carried out through the practices of common life

## Implications for AI Systems
Ferdman argues that AI systems can undermine intersubjectivity in skilling through:
- Replacing human mentorship with algorithmic guidance
- Creating disembodied interaction environments that reduce shared valuing
- Automating routine tasks that would otherwise provide opportunities for mentorship
- Narrowing the field of affordances for developing shared understanding

## Professional Context
In market research, intersubjectivity in skilling is essential for:
- Developing practical wisdom through mentorship
- Learning the value of ethical research practices
- Cultivating shared understanding of research quality standards
- Transmitting tacit knowledge that cannot be codified

## Capacity Development
Intersubjectivity supports capacity development by:
- Providing opportunities to learn from others' experience
- Creating shared valuing of research excellence
- Transmitting not just techniques but the value of those techniques
- Building professional identity through shared practices
