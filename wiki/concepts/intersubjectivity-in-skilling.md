# Intersubjectivity in Skilling

**First Observed:** 2026-07-03  
**Last Updated:** 2026-09-25

## Definition

The shared understanding dimension of skill development, where learning occurs through relationships with others who guide and share the value of the skill. Becoming skilled typically requires a mentor to instill not only mechanical aspects but also the value of the skill, as detailed in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]]. Expanded with detailed analysis of characteristics, societal dimension, threats from AI, preservation strategies, and market research applications from Ferdman's research.

## Characteristics

Ferdman (2026) elaborates on the intersubjective nature of skill development:

> "Skill habituation has a 'shared understanding' dimension. First, becoming skilled typically requires a mentor to guide the novice into becoming an expert that engages in practice of the right kind. A good mentor is typically necessary in order to cultivate the proper motivational structure for becoming skilled."

Key characteristics:

- **Shared valuing**: Both mentor and mentee develop "shared valuing" of the skill, where the mentee eventually views the value of the skill as their own
- **Mentorship relationship**: Requires a relationship where the mentor instills both mechanical aspects and value of the skill
- **Societal dimension**: Social practices performed by groups or whole societies can provide a mentoring environment
- **Constitutive aim**: Social practices have a "telos, which is ultimately the aim of helping people fare well rather than badly"

## Threats from AI in Research Contexts

Ferdman identifies how AI mediation threatens intersubjective skilling:

> "APAs decrease embodied, intersubjective interaction involved in life-planning activities."

> "In embodied interaction, all the constant 'living together' which goes on in between and around physical, embodied activities with other persons better allow us to test and grow our human capacities, compared to virtual environments."

Specific threats to market research:
- AI tools that replace human mentorship in research methodology
- Digital platforms that reduce opportunities for shared understanding of research quality
- Automated analysis tools that bypass the intersubjective process of interpreting data
- Remote work environments that limit embodied interaction necessary for qualitative research skills

## Preservation Strategies

Ferdman suggests approaches to maintain intersubjective skilling:

1. **Structured mentorship**: Create formal mentorship programs that preserve opportunities for shared valuing
2. **Embodied collaboration**: Design workspaces that facilitate physical co-presence for research teams
3. **Reflection practices**: Build in regular opportunities for researchers to discuss the value of research skills
4. **AI as augmentation**: Use AI tools to enhance rather than replace intersubjective learning processes

## Market Research Applications

Intersubjectivity is particularly critical for:
- Developing qualitative interviewing skills that require embodied presence
- Building shared understanding of research quality standards
- Cultivating the ability to recognize emerging patterns through team discussion
- Preserving the value dimension of research skills that goes beyond mechanical execution
