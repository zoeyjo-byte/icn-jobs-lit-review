# [[intersubjectivity-in-skilling]]

## Definition

The **shared understanding dimension of skill development**, where learning occurs through relationships with others who guide and share the value of the skill, as established in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]].

## Key Characteristics

- Requires **mentor-mentee relationships** for proper skill development
- Involves **shared valuing** of both the skill's output and the skill itself
- Creates **shared intentions** between mentor and novice
- Develops through **participatory sense-making** regarding the skill
- Forms the basis for **social practices** that generate moral reasons for action

## Ferdman's Analysis

Ferdman emphasizes that skill habituation has an essential intersubjective dimension:

> "Skill habituation has a 'shared understanding' dimension. First, becoming skilled typically requires a mentor to guide the novice into becoming an expert that engages in practice of the right kind. A good mentor is typically necessary in order to cultivate the proper motivational structure for becoming skilled."

This relationship goes beyond mechanical instruction:

> "In the relationship between mentor and novice, the mentor instills not only the mechanical aspects of the skill, but, as importantly, the value of the skill, such that in the process of habituation, the skilled agent eventually comes to view the value of the skill as their own."

## Societal Dimension

The intersubjective process extends beyond individual mentorship to societal practices:

> "Social practices performed by groups or whole societies can provide a mentoring environment: the family, colleagues, citizens and so on. Indeed, as Kate Manne argues, social practices are often what generates moral reasons for action, as they have a constitutive aim or telos, which is ultimately the aim of helping people fare well rather than badly."

Alasdair MacIntyre similarly noted that learning occurs "through the practices of the common life" where agents learn responsiveness to skillful action requirements.

## AI's Threat to Intersubjectivity

AI systems threaten this intersubjective dimension by:

- **Replacing human mentors** with algorithmic guidance
- **Undermining shared valuing** of skills through automated outputs
- **Reducing opportunities** for developing shared intentions around skill development
- **Creating disembodied interactions** that make shared understanding more difficult

As Ferdman explains: "When enough persons do not value excellence or mastery of a human capacity (e.g., critical thinking), the capacity is in danger of atrophying."

## Implications for Market Research

- AI tools that **automate research training** undermine the mentor-mentee relationships essential for developing research expertise
- **Algorithmic guidance systems** cannot instill the shared understanding of research values that human mentors provide
- Market research firms need to preserve **intersubjective learning environments** even as they adopt AI tools
- The **devaluation of research skills** occurs when firms prioritize AI outputs over the development of human research capacities
