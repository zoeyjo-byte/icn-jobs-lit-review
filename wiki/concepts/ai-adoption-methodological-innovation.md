# AI Adoption Methodological Innovation

## Definition

Methodological advances in **measuring AI adoption**, particularly the shift from occupational exposure scores to direct firm-level spending data. The [[ramp-revelio-2026-ai-jobs-impact-study]] represents a breakthrough in this area by using observed AI vendor payments rather than theoretical exposure metrics.

## Evolution of Measurement Approaches

| Era | Primary Method | Key Limitations | Examples |
|------|----------------|-----------------|----------|
| **Pre-2023** | Occupational exposure scores | Cannot distinguish adopters from non-adopters; no intensity measure | Eloundou et al. (2024), Webb (2020) |
| **2023-2025** | Resume-based AI skills | Indirect proxy; measures capability not adoption | Babina et al. (2024) |
| **2026+** | Observed spending data | Direct adoption measure with timing and intensity | Ramp-Revelio study |

## Ramp-Revelio Methodology

The study introduces the **AI Spend Per Employee Methodology (PEPM)**:

1. **Adoption definition**: Three consecutive months of ≥$100 AI vendor spend
2. **Intensity measure**: PEPM = (sum of first 3 months' AI spend) / (3 × baseline headcount)
3. **Intensity stratification**: Low (bottom two PEPM terciles), High (top PEPM tercile)
4. **Comparison design**: Later adopters within same intensity group

This approach overcomes four critical limitations of prior methods:

1. **Direct observation**: Measures actual adoption behavior rather than theoretical exposure
2. **Timing precision**: Captures exact adoption month rather than imputed timing
3. **Intensity dimension**: Reveals differential effects by adoption intensity
4. **Firm-level specificity**: Avoids occupational aggregation that masks firm differences

## Key Innovations

### Observed Spending Threshold

The $100/month threshold excludes one-off experiments while capturing sustained adoption, addressing the "Is this really AI adoption?" question that plagues survey-based approaches.

### PEPM Metric

Using baseline headcount in the denominator makes intensity comparable across firm sizes and keeps employee scale predetermined relative to adoption timing.

### Staggered Treatment Design

The Callaway-Sant'Anna framework accounts for heterogeneous treatment timing, avoiding biases in traditional event-study approaches.

## Evidence of Impact

This methodological innovation revealed findings invisible to exposure-based studies:
- Only **high-intensity adopters** (+10.2% headcount growth) showed significant employment effects
- **Entry-level roles grew 12.0%** among high-intensity adopters, contradicting exposure-based predictions
- Effects concentrated in **Information sector** (+13.4% growth), not uniformly distributed

## Implications for Market Research

This methodological shift has critical implications:

- **Vendor evaluation**: Must measure actual usage intensity, not just adoption status
- **Impact assessment**: Requires tracking spending patterns over time, not binary adoption
- **Benchmarking**: Industry comparisons must account for intensity differences
- **ROI calculation**: Benefits appear only above critical intensity thresholds

## Practical Implementation

For market research organizations, adopting this methodological innovation means:
- Tracking actual AI spending rather than adoption claims
- Measuring intensity through normalized spending metrics
- Comparing against firms with similar pre-adoption characteristics
- Focusing on sustained usage patterns rather than initial adoption

Without these methodological improvements, organizations risk misattributing effects or missing the intensity threshold needed for positive returns—precisely what the [[ramp-revelio-2026-ai-jobs-impact-study]] demonstrates.
