# Agential Control

Agential control refers to the ability to adjust the execution of an action as the performance unfolds, and to initiate the activity through self-deliberation rather than environmental triggers. As a core property of skill, it distinguishes skilled agents from highly reliable automatons or habitual actors, as detailed in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]].

## Key Components
1. **Initiation Control:** Skilled agents have full control over starting a task. Habits, by contrast, are elicited in response to environmental cues.
2. **Real-Time Adjustment:** Skills involve flexible links between thought and action that continue to evolve even after expertise is achieved. Agents can tune and attune their responses as contexts change.
3. **Self-Deliberation:** Exercising agential control requires settling between options and coordinating intentions, which demands the competent exercise of epistemic, moral, and creative capacities.

## Threats from AI Systems
AI tools that automate decision-making or provide pre-determined plans can erode agential control by encouraging users to become plan-followers. When users respond to AI outputs as environmental triggers rather than initiating their own plans, they shift from skilled agents to habitual responders. This is particularly concerning in life-planning and strategic decision-making, where agential control is essential for developing practical wisdom and personal identity.

## Preservation Strategies
- **Design for Scaffolding, Not Replacement:** AI should assist with information retrieval and option generation, but leave the settling and coordinating functions to the human user.
- **Preserve Exception Handling:** Ensure humans remain involved in edge cases and system malfunctions to maintain judgment and cognitive musculature.
- **Encourage Plan-Forming:** Create workflows that require users to articulate their own goals, values, and strategies before leveraging AI for execution.
