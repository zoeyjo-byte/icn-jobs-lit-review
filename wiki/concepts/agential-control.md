## Update (2026-08-20)

Expanded with detailed analysis from [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]]:

Agential control is the ability to adjust the execution of an action as the performance unfolds. As Ferdman explains, this is a key component of being skilled that distinguishes skills from habits.

Key characteristics:
- Skills require control over initiating the task, whereas habits are merely triggered by environmental cues
- As agency develops, the balance between automaticity and control shifts through a process of tuning and attuning
- Cultivating a skill involves developing flexible links between thought and action that continue to evolve even after expertise has been achieved

Contrast with habitual action:
- Habitual activity is triggered by something in the environment, where the agent performs the activity in response to the trigger
- Skilled activity is one where the agent has full control over initiating the activity
- An agent who performs out of habit has less skill compared to an agent who initiated the activity with full agential control

Market research implications:
- AI tools that automate research processes may reduce researchers' agential control over analytical tasks
- When researchers become habitual users of AI-generated insights without questioning or adjusting them, they may lose critical thinking skills
- Research methodologies should preserve opportunities for researchers to exercise agential control over the research process
- Tools like Sunsama that "schedule tasks logically into your next available time slot" risk turning researchers into plan-followers rather than plan-formers
