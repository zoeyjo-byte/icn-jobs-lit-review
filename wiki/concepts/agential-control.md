# Agential Control

**Agential control** is a foundational property of skill, defined as the ability to adjust the execution of an action as it unfolds and to initiate tasks purposefully rather than reactively. It serves as a key differentiator between genuine skills and mere habits or automated responses.

## Skill vs. Habit
According to the philosophy of skill, habits are elicited in response to environmental triggers (e.g., swiping a phone, clicking notifications), whereas skills require the agent to have full control over initiating the activity. As an agent develops, the balance between automaticity and control shifts through a process of tuning and attuning, creating flexible links between thought and action that continue to evolve even after expertise is achieved.

## Role in Capacity Cultivation
Agential control is essential for **[[capacity-skilling|capacity cultivation]]**. Developing and exercising core human capacities requires the competent exercise of the **[[volitional-capacity]]**, which in turn demands agential control. When AI tools automate routine tasks or constantly prompt users with recommendations, they risk replacing agential initiation with reactive habituation. This shift deprives users of the opportunity to practice judgment, strengthen cognitive musculature, and develop the deep experiential knowledge that comes from initiating and coordinating complex activities.

## Implications for AI Design
Environments that encourage agential control are considered **[[capacity-conducive]]**, as they invite users to initiate valuable activities rather than merely responding to algorithmic triggers. Conversely, systems designed to maximize engagement through predictive prompts and automated suggestions contribute to **[[affordance-shrinkage]]**, narrowing the space for independent agency and fostering dependency on external mediation.
