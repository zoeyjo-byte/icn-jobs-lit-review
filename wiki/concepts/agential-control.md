# Agential Control

Agential control is the ability to adjust the execution of an action as the performance unfolds, representing a key component of skill development and exercise. This concept is central to understanding how AI systems may affect human capacities, as it distinguishes between skilled activity and mere habit or automatic response.

## Definition and Characteristics

To be skilled in something is to have agential control over it, which involves:

1. **Initiating the activity**: The agent has full control over starting the task, rather than responding to environmental triggers
2. **Adjusting execution**: The ability to modify performance in response to changing circumstances
3. **Flexible links between thought and action**: Developing connections that continue to evolve even after expertise has been achieved

As Nancy Sherman (1991) explains, "as agency develops, the balance between automaticity and control shifts through a process of tuning and attuning."

## Contrast with Habit

Skills differ from habits in an important respect:

- **Skills**: Require control over initiating the task; the agent has full control over starting the activity
- **Habits**: Are elicited in response to environmental triggers; the agent performs the activity in response to the trigger

For example, a habitual activity is one triggered by something in the environment (like checking notifications when a phone buzzes), whereas a skilled activity is one where the agent has full control over initiating the activity (like deliberately practicing a musical instrument).

## Importance for Capacity Development

Agential control is crucial for developing core human capacities because:

1. It enables self-criticism and improvement when plans fail
2. It supports the development of practical wisdom through real-world experience
3. It allows for creative adaptation to novel situations
4. It preserves responsibility for outcomes, fostering growth

## AI's Impact on Agential Control

AI systems can undermine agential control in several ways:

- **Over-automation**: When AI systems make decisions for users, they remove opportunities to practice judgment
- **Reactive interfaces**: Design patterns that encourage users to respond to algorithmic suggestions rather than initiate actions
- **Personalized recommendations**: Systems that anticipate user needs may reduce opportunities for independent decision-making

For instance, when users rely on [[artificial-personal-assistants]] to plan their lives, they may become "plan-followers rather than plan-formers," diminishing their capacity for practical reasoning.

## Implications for Market Research

For market research professionals, preserving agential control is essential for maintaining high-quality insights. Research tools that completely automate analysis may deprive researchers of opportunities to develop critical thinking skills. Instead, AI-augmented tools should preserve opportunities for researchers to exercise control over the analytical process.
