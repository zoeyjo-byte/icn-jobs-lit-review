# Agential Control

## Definition
The ability to adjust the execution of an action as the performance unfolds, a key component of being skilled that distinguishes skills from habits. Agential control represents the flexible links between thought and action that continue to evolve even after expertise has been achieved.

## Philosophical Foundation
As defined in Ferdman's [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]], agential control is central to understanding the difference between:
- **Skills**: Activities where the agent has full control over initiating the task
- **Habits**: Activities triggered by environmental triggers without initiation control

## Key Characteristics
- Requires **initiation control**: Ability to start the activity independently
- Involves **ongoing adjustment**: Capacity to modify execution during performance
- Develops through **habituation**: Gradual tuning and attuning process (Sherman 1991)
- Represents **flexible mastery**: Distinct from mere reliability or competence

## Role in Capacity Development
Agential control is essential for developing all **[[human-capacities]]**:
- **Epistemic capacities**: Adjusting reasoning processes during complex problem-solving
- **Social capacities**: Modifying interaction approaches based on social cues
- **Creative capacity**: Iterating on ideas during the creative process
- **Volitional capacity**: Exerting effort to overcome obstacles during challenging tasks

## Threats from AI Systems
Ferdman (2026) identifies how AI tools like **[[artificial-personal-assistants]]** undermine agential control:
- **Plan-following vs. plan-forming**: APAs encourage responding to AI-generated recommendations rather than initiating planning
- **Automation bias**: Users attribute excessive trust to AI tools over their own judgment
- **Reduced initiation opportunities**: Routine tasks automated away prevent "cognitive musculature" development
- **Triggered responses**: Users conditioned to respond to AI outputs rather than initiate actions

## Example: Life Planning
When using APAs for life planning:
- **With agential control**: Person initiates planning process, sets goals, adjusts based on circumstances
- **Without agential control**: Person responds to APA prompts, follows AI-generated plans, shifts responsibility for failures to the AI

## Organizational Implications
The erosion of agential control explains the **[[senior-skills]]** phenomenon where entry-level roles increasingly demand traditionally senior skills. When routine tasks are automated, junior researchers lose opportunities to develop foundational skills through habituation, creating a skills gap that manifests as "seniorized" entry requirements.
