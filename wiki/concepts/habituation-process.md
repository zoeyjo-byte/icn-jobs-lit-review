# [[habituation-process]]

## Definition
The **gradual process of becoming skilled through practice**, involving inculcation through continued attunement to demands of individual cases, with critical implications for AI's impact on skill development. As established in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]], skill development cannot be learned through testimony alone but requires practice, encountering and overcoming disappointment and failure.

## Core Characteristics

### Gradual Development Through Practice
Habituation is not instantaneous but occurs through:
- **Successive trials** and learning from mistakes
- **Refinement of action** through repeated engagement
- **Shifting balance** between automaticity and control
- **Stages of development** from novice to expert (Dreyfus and Dreyfus 1986)

As Ferdman explains: *'Habituation involves inculcation through continued attunement to the demands of individual cases; refinement of the action through successive trials and learning from mistakes.'*

### Two Essential Components
The habituation process comprises two interdependent dimensions:

1. **Intersubjectivity**:
   - Requires mentorship to instill both mechanical aspects and **value of the skill**
   - Creates 'shared valuing' between mentor and mentee
   - Enables social practices to generate moral reasons for action
   - As MacIntyre noted, learning occurs through 'the habits of a common life'

2. **Embodiment**:
   - Depends on physical presence and sensory experience
   - Enables 'participatory sense-making' with others
   - Critical for recognizing others as moral agents
   - Creates conditions where 'meaning is generated in the interplay between participants'

## Contrast with Simple Repetition
Habituation differs fundamentally from mechanical repetition:
- Involves **growth rather than mere repetition**
- Requires **encountering and overcoming disappointment**
- Entails **sensitivity to varying contexts**
- Develops **flexible links between thought and action**

As Ferdman notes: *'While becoming skilled in the capacities requires successive attempts at an activity, the process itself is one of growth rather than simple repetition.'*

## Threats from AI Systems
AI systems undermine the habituation process through:

### Artificial Personal Assistants
APAs disrupt habituation by:
- **Offloading routine decision-making** that strengthens 'cognitive musculature'
- Reducing opportunities for **small-scale decisions** that build broader life-planning skills
- Creating dependency that **diminishes sense of personal competence**
- Replacing **plan-forming** with **plan-following**

### Automation Design
Many AI implementations threaten habituation by:
- **Depriving users of routine opportunities** to practice judgment
- Creating **exception-handling scenarios** without foundational skill development
- **Narrowing the field of affordances** for capacity exercise
- Undermining the **volitional capacity** needed for skill development

## Implications for Market Research
In market research organizations, disrupted habituation manifests as:
- **[[entry-level-squeeze]]** where traditional training grounds disappear
- **Deskilling of junior researchers** who lack opportunities to develop through practice
- **Erosion of critical thinking** documented in industry studies
- **Weakened ethical sensitivity** as AI handles preliminary analysis

The **[[human-operating-model-gap]]** directly relates to disrupted habituation processes, as AI adoption outpaces adaptation of human development systems.

## Preserving Habituation Opportunities
Organizations can protect the habituation process by:
- Designing AI tools with **intentional learning pathways**
- Creating 'training wheels' approaches where AI support decreases over time
- Preserving **structured opportunities** for embodied mentorship
- Ensuring junior staff maintain **meaningful decision points**
- Building **failure-tolerant environments** where mistakes become learning opportunities

As Ferdman concludes, habituation is not merely an individual responsibility but requires capacity-conducive environments that society must actively foster in the age of AI.
