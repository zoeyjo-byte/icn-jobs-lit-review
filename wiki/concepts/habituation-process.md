# [[habituation-process]]

## Definition
The gradual process of becoming skilled through practice, involving inculcation through continued attunement to demands of individual cases, with critical implications for AI's impact on skill development. As detailed in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]], skill development cannot be learned through testimony alone but requires practice, encountering and overcoming disappointment and failure.

## Key Characteristics
- A slow, gradual process of becoming skilled through practice
- Involves inculcation through continued attunement to individual cases
- Requires refinement through successive trials and learning from mistakes
- Cannot be learned through testimony alone
- Includes encountering and overcoming disappointment and failure

## Two Essential Components

### Intersubjectivity
Skill habituation has a 'shared understanding' dimension:
- Requires mentors to guide novices into proper practice
- Mentors instill not only mechanical aspects but the value of the skill
- Involves shared intentions and 'shared valuing' of the skill
- Social practices at societal scale provide mentoring environments

### Embodiment
Successful habituation requires embodied component:
- 'Participatory sense-making' is constitutive of social cognition
- Embodied interaction enables recognizing others as moral agents
- Without embodiment, forming meaningful relationships becomes harder
- Physical presence is critical for intersubjective habituation

## AI Impact Analysis
AI systems can undermine habituation by:
- Automating routine tasks that provide essential practice opportunities
- Creating environments where users respond to AI outputs rather than initiating actions
- Reducing opportunities for embodied, intersubjective learning
- Preventing users from encountering and overcoming failure

## Market Research Implications
- Junior researchers need opportunities to practice foundational research skills
- Over-automation of data collection and analysis removes critical learning experiences
- Organizations should preserve opportunities for embodied interaction with research subjects
- Mentorship programs should emphasize shared valuing of research capacities
- Training should include safe spaces for encountering and learning from failure

## Development Stages
Dreyfus and Dreyfus (1986) characterize skill development through five stages:
1. **Novice**: Rule-following with context-free features
2. **Advanced beginner**: Situational perception with limited situational understanding
3. **Competent**: Hierarchical planning with conscious decision-making
4. **Proficient**: Holistic perspective with intuitive guidance
5. **Expert**: Fluid performance with intuitive understanding
