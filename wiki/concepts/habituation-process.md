# Habituation Process

Habituation is the gradual, slow process of becoming skilled through practice, inculcation, and attunement to the demands of individual cases. As outlined in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]], skill cannot be learned through testimony alone; it requires encountering and overcoming disappointment and failure. Habituation is the mechanism through which [[agential-control]] and [[human-capacities]] are cultivated.

## Two Critical Components
1. **Intersubjectivity:** Skill development is inherently social. It requires mentors, exemplars, and shared social practices that instill not only the mechanical 'know-how' but also a shared understanding of the skill's value. Social practices generate moral reasons for action and help individuals fare well.
2. **Embodiment:** Physical presence and sensory experience are constitutive of social cognition. Embodied interaction allows us to perceive others as moral agents, recognize their gaze and voice, and form meaningful relationships. Disembodied interaction requires significantly more mental effort to attribute moral agency and develop shared valuing.

## Stages of Skill Development
Drawing on the Dreyfus model, habituation progresses through stages: novice, advanced beginner, competent, proficient, and expert. Early stages rely heavily on rule-following, while later stages are characterized by rapid, fluid, involved engagement. Crucially, this process is one of growth rather than simple repetition.

## AI's Impact on Habituation
AI mediation risks undermining the quality of conditions for capacity habituation. By offloading routine tasks and providing instant answers, AI tools can shortcut the practice necessary for deep skill acquisition. Disembodied AI interactions further restrict the embodied, intersubjective engagement required for developing social and moral capacities. This leads to shallow skilling and capacity impoverishment.

## Mitigation & Preservation
- **Maintain Mentorship Structures:** Preserve human-to-human guidance and feedback loops in AI-augmented workflows.
- **Design for Practice:** Ensure AI tools require user engagement and decision-making rather than fully autonomous execution.
- **Foster Embodied Collaboration:** Prioritize in-person or richly mediated interactions for complex, value-laden tasks.
