# [[habituation-process]]

## Definition

The habituation process is the gradual process of becoming skilled through practice, involving inculcation through continued attunement to demands of individual cases, with critical implications for AI's impact on skill development. As detailed in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]], skill development cannot be learned through testimony alone but requires practice, encountering and overcoming disappointment and failure.

## Key Characteristics

1. **Gradual progression**: Moves from novice through advanced beginner, competent, proficient, to expert stages (Dreyfus and Dreyfus 1986)
2. **Case-based learning**: Involves attunement to the demands of individual cases rather than abstract principles
3. **Error-driven refinement**: Requires encountering and overcoming disappointment and failure
4. **Time-intensive**: "Skill generally cannot be learnt through testimony, and requires practice" and considerable living

Ferdman explains: "Habituation involves inculcation through continued attunement to the demands of individual cases; refinement of the action through successive trials and learning from mistakes. This includes encountering and overcoming disappointment and failure."

## Two Essential Components

### Intersubjectivity

The habituation process has a 'shared understanding' dimension requiring:
- Mentor guidance to instill both mechanical aspects and value of the skill
- Development of shared intentions between mentor and mentee
- Social practices that generate moral reasons for action
- "Shared valuing" of both the product and the skill itself

### Embodiment

Successful habituation requires an embodied component because:
- "Embodied 'participatory sense-making' is constitutive of social cognition"
- Recognition of others as moral agents depends on embodied interaction
- "Without being embodied in a social interaction... we would be hard-pressed to recognize them spontaneously as moral agents"
- Disembodied interactions make it harder to form meaningful relationships

## Threats from AI Automation

AI systems threaten the habituation process by:
- Automating routine tasks that provide essential practice opportunities
- Creating "capacity-hostile environments" that restrict habituation
- Replacing embodied mentorship with disembodied AI guidance
- Reducing opportunities for learning from mistakes through error correction

As Ferdman notes: "Automating routine tasks and leaving only exception-handling to the human user... 'deprives the user of the routine opportunities to practice their judgment and strengthen their cognitive musculature, leaving them atrophied and unprepared when the exceptions do arise' (Lee et al. 2025)."

## Implications for Market Research

The habituation process is critical for developing market research skills because:
- Qualitative analysis requires nuanced judgment developed through practice
- Ethical decision-making in research contexts depends on embodied experience
- Creative methodology development emerges from encountering research challenges
- Client relationship management depends on intersubjective habituation

## Preservation Strategies

To protect the habituation process in AI-augmented market research:
- Maintain opportunities for junior researchers to handle routine tasks
- Design AI tools that preserve space for human judgment
- Create hybrid mentorship models combining human and AI guidance
- Ensure researchers experience the full research cycle, including failures
- Foster embodied interaction through team-based research approaches
