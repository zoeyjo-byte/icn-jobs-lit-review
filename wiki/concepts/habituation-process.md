# [[habituation-process]]

## Definition

The gradual process of becoming skilled through practice, involving inculcation through continued attunement to demands of individual cases, with critical implications for AI's impact on skill development. As established in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]], skill development cannot be learned through testimony alone but requires practice, encountering and overcoming disappointment and failure.

## Key Characteristics

- **Gradual progression**: Moves through stages from novice to expert (Dreyfus and Dreyfus 1986)
- **Experience-dependent**: Requires concrete experience rather than abstract principles
- **Failure-embracing**: Involves encountering and overcoming disappointment and failure
- **Context-sensitive**: Develops through attunement to demands of individual cases
- **Time-intensive**: Requires considerable time and living to develop

## Stages of Skill Development

As described by Dreyfus and Dreyfus (1986):
1. **Novice**: Relies on context-free rules and features
2. **Advanced Beginner**: Recognizes situational aspects
3. **Competent**: Develops conceptual plans
4. **Proficient**: Sees situations holistically
5. **Expert**: Responds intuitively from deep understanding

## Role in Capacity Development

The habituation process is essential because:
- It enables the transition from rule-following to fluid, context-sensitive engagement
- It develops practical wisdom through real-world experience
- It creates the foundation for agential control in skilled activities
- It fosters the volitional capacity through overcoming difficulty
- It enables the development of intersubjective understanding of capacity value

## Threats from AI Systems

AI systems threaten the habituation process by:
- **Automation bias**: Offloading routine tasks that would strengthen "cognitive musculature"
- **Exception handling**: Leaving only unusual cases for humans, depriving them of routine practice opportunities
- **Shallow skilling**: Creating capacity-hostile environments that afford only superficial capacity development
- **Disembodied interaction**: Reducing opportunities for embodied habituation of social capacities
- **Plan-following orientation**: Transforming users from plan-formers to plan-followers

## Evidence in Market Research

Ferdman (2026) cites evidence that:
- Healthcare professionals experience declining clinical judgment from AI dependence
- Young people struggle with "everyday but essential" skills like empathy and time management (Halliday 2025)
- Overreliance on AI planning tools leads to reduced sense of personal competence (Gabriel et al. 2024)

## Preservation Strategies

1. **Structured practice pathways**: Design AI workflows that preserve critical habituation opportunities
2. **Deliberate difficulty**: Introduce appropriate challenges that require effort and perseverance
3. **Failure normalization**: Create environments where mistakes are expected and analyzed
4. **Embodied interaction requirements**: Ensure AI tools don't eliminate physical presence opportunities
5. **Mentorship integration**: Pair AI tools with human mentors who guide habituation processes
