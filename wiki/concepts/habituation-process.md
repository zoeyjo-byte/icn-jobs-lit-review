# [[habituation-process]]

**First observed:** 2026-07-03  
**Last updated:** 2026-07-18  

## Definition

The habituation process is the gradual process of becoming skilled through practice, involving inculcation through continued attunement to demands of individual cases, with critical implications for AI's impact on skill development. Skill development cannot be learned through testimony alone but requires practice, encountering and overcoming disappointment and failure, as detailed in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]].

## Key Characteristics

Based on [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]], the habituation process has several defining features:

1. **Gradual progression**: Skill development occurs through stages from novice to expert, with rule-following more dominant in early stages and increasing sophistication in later stages.

2. **Concrete experience**: Reliance on concrete experience rather than abstract principles, with refinement through successive trials.

3. **Learning from mistakes**: Involves encountering and overcoming disappointment and failure as essential components of growth.

4. **Time-intensive**: Requires considerable time and lived experience, not merely theoretical instruction.

## Two Critical Components

The habituation process consists of two interrelated components:

### 1. Intersubjectivity

- Requires mentorship where a good mentor instills both mechanical aspects and the value of the skill
- Involves shared intentions and "shared valuing" of the skill between mentor and mentee
- Occurs through social practices performed by groups or whole societies (family, colleagues, citizens)
- Learning is carried out through practices of common life with teachers and exemplars

### 2. Embodiment

- Requires embodied 'participatory sense-making' for social cognition
- Involves recognizing others as embodied beings similar to ourselves through voice and gaze
- Creates 'embodied understanding' by apprehending other perceived bodies as similar loci of subjectivity
- Makes spontaneous recognition of others as moral agents possible through embodied interaction

## Implications for AI Adoption

AI systems can undermine the habituation process in several ways:

- By automating routine tasks, depriving users of opportunities to practice judgment and strengthen "cognitive musculature"
- By reducing embodied interactions necessary for social and moral capacity development
- By creating environments where users respond to AI outputs rather than initiating their own actions
- By offloading small-scale decisions that collectively build practical wisdom
