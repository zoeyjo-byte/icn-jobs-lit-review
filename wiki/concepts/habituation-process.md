# [[habituation-process]]

## Definition
The gradual process of becoming skilled through practice, involving inculcation through continued attunement to demands of individual cases, with critical implications for AI's impact on skill development. As detailed in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]], skill development cannot be learned through testimony alone but requires practice, encountering and overcoming disappointment and failure.

## Key Characteristics
The habituation process features:
- **Gradual progression**: Movement through stages from novice to expert
- **Concrete experience**: Reliance on specific cases rather than abstract principles
- **Refinement through trial**: Learning from mistakes and successive attempts
- **Time-intensive nature**: Requires considerable time and lived experience
- **Incorporation of failure**: Encountering and overcoming disappointment is essential

## Philosophical Foundation
Ferdman draws on skill philosophy to establish that habituation:
- Cannot be achieved through testimony alone
- Involves tuning and attuning the balance between automaticity and control
- Creates flexible links between thought and action that evolve even after expertise
- Requires engagement with individual cases rather than rule application

As Ferdman notes: "Skill generally cannot be learnt through testimony, and requires practice. As we move through stages of skill development, from novice through to expert, we rely on concrete experience rather than abstract principles."

## Two Essential Components
1. **Intersubjectivity**: Skill habituation has a 'shared understanding' dimension requiring mentors who instill not only mechanical aspects but also the value of the skill. Social practices performed by groups provide mentoring environments where shared valuing develops.

2. **Embodiment**: Successful habituation requires embodied interaction, particularly for social and moral capacities. As Ferdman explains, "without being embodied in a social interaction... it would be much more difficult to achieve" meaningful skill development.

## AI's Threat to Habituation
AI systems threaten the habituation process through:
- **Automation of routine tasks**: Depriving users of "routine opportunities to practice their judgment and strengthen their cognitive musculature"
- **Reduced failure opportunities**: Creating environments where users encounter fewer challenges requiring skill application
- **Shallow engagement**: Interfaces that discourage deep, sustained practice necessary for expertise
- **Diminished intersubjectivity**: Replacing human mentors with algorithmic guidance that cannot provide shared valuing

## Case Study: Healthcare AI
Ferdman cites evidence that AI dependence in healthcare diminishes "diagnostic reasoning, declining clinical judgment, reduced retention of tacit knowledge, declining ethical sensitivity and weakened moral judgment" because it disrupts the habituation process necessary for medical expertise.
