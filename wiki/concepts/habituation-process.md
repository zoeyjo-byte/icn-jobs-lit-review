## Update: 2026-09-15

[[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]] elaborates on the habituation process as "a gradual and slow process" that cannot be learned through testimony alone but requires practice. The paper references Dreyfus and Dreyfus's (1986) 5-stage model of skill development:

1. Novice
2. Advanced beginner
3. Competent
4. Proficient
5. Expert

In the early stages, rule-following is more dominant, while in later stages, agents exercise more sophistication and sensitivity to varying contexts, characterized by "rapid, fluid, involved engagement."

Crucially, habituation involves "encountering and overcoming disappointment and failure." As Ferdman explains, "Skill development cannot be learned through testimony alone but requires practice, encountering and overcoming disappointment and failure."

The paper identifies two essential components of habituation:
1. **Intersubjectivity**: Skill habituation has a "shared understanding" dimension requiring mentors to instill not only mechanical aspects but also the value of the skill
2. **Embodiment**: Successful habituation must include an embodied component, as "embodied 'participatory sense-making' is constitutive of social cognition"

In market research contexts, AI tools that automate routine analysis tasks may deprive researchers of the habituation process necessary for developing expertise. Without opportunities to encounter and overcome research challenges through practice, researchers may develop only shallow expertise that cannot be adapted to novel research situations. This is particularly concerning for entry-level researchers who traditionally developed skills through handling routine research tasks.
