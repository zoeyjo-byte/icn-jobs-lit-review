# [[habituation-process]]

## Definition
The habituation process is the gradual process of becoming skilled through practice, involving inculcation through continued attunement to demands of individual cases, with critical implications for AI's impact on skill development. As detailed in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]], skill development cannot be learned through testimony alone but requires practice, encountering and overcoming disappointment and failure.

## Key Characteristics
According to Ferdman's analysis in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]], the habituation process has several critical features:

1. **Gradual Development**: Skills develop through a slow process of habituation rather than instant acquisition.

2. **Practice-Based Learning**: Skill generally cannot be learnt through testimony and requires concrete experience rather than abstract principles.

3. **Refinement Through Trial**: Habituation involves refinement of the action through successive trials and learning from mistakes.

4. **Encountering Failure**: The process includes encountering and overcoming disappointment and failure as essential components of skill development.

## Stages of Skill Development
Dreyfus and Dreyfus (1986) characterize the process of becoming skilled as a 5-stage progression:

1. **Novice**: Relies on context-free rules and features
2. **Advanced Beginner**: Recognizes aspects of situations
3. **Competent**: Develops conscious, linear plans
4. **Proficient**: Sees situations holistically
5. **Expert**: Has fluid, involved engagement with the domain

Rule-following is more dominant in early stages, while later stages are characterized by rapid, fluid, involved engagement. Crucially, while becoming skilled requires successive attempts at an activity, the process itself is one of growth rather than simple repetition.

## Components of Habituation
The habituation process has two essential components:

### Intersubjectivity
Skill habituation has a 'shared understanding' dimension:
- Requires mentors to guide novices into becoming experts
- Involves instilling not only mechanical aspects of the skill but also the value of the skill
- Creates shared intentions and "shared valuing" of the skill between mentor and mentee
- Occurs on societal scale through social practices that generate moral reasons for action

### Embodiment
Successful habituation must include an embodied component:
- Embodied 'participatory sense-making' is constitutive of social cognition
- Being embodied is critical for recognizing others as moral agents
- Embodied interaction creates 'embodied understanding' of others
- Without embodiment, it would be much harder to achieve meaningful relationships

## Impact of AI on Habituation
AI systems can undermine the habituation process in several ways:

1. **Automation of Routine Tasks**: "Automating routine tasks and leaving only exception-handling to the human user, 'deprives the user of the routine opportunities to practice their judgment and strengthen their cognitive musculature, leaving them atrophied and unprepared when the exceptions do arise.'"

2. **Reduced Practice Opportunities**: By handling small-scale decisions, AI systems reduce opportunities for the gradual habituation process necessary for developing practical wisdom.

3. **Shallow Skilling**: AI interfaces often afford only superficial engagement with tasks, preventing deeper habituation.

4. **Disembodied Interaction**: Digital interfaces limit the embodied component of habituation, particularly affecting social and moral capacities.

## Implications for Market Research
In the market research industry, preserving the habituation process is critical for:
- Developing expertise in research methodology through practice
- Building practical wisdom in interpreting complex human behaviors
- Creating meaningful client relationships through embodied interaction
- Developing moral judgment in ethical research practices

Organizations should evaluate AI implementations based on whether they preserve or undermine opportunities for the habituation process, as outlined in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]].
