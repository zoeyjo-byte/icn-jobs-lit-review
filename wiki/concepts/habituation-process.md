# [[habituation-process]]

## Definition
The gradual process of becoming skilled through practice, involving inculcation through continued attunement to demands of individual cases, with critical implications for AI's impact on skill development. As detailed in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]], skill development cannot be learned through testimony alone but requires practice, encountering and overcoming disappointment and failure.

## Key Characteristics
1. **Gradual progression**: Moves through stages from novice to expert (Dreyfus and Dreyfus 5-stage model)
2. **Experience-dependent**: Requires considerable time and living to gain experience
3. **Failure-oriented**: Involves encountering and overcoming disappointment and failure
4. **Context-sensitive**: Refinement through successive trials and learning from mistakes in specific contexts
5. **Non-repetitive**: A process of growth rather than simple repetition

## Intersubjective Dimension
Habituation has a 'shared understanding' dimension:
- Requires mentors to guide novices into becoming experts
- Mentors instill not only mechanical aspects but also the value of the skill
- Creates shared intentions and 'shared valuing' of the skill
- Social practices at societal scale provide mentoring environments

## Embodied Dimension
Successful habituation requires embodied interaction:
- 'Participatory sense-making' is constitutive of social cognition
- Embodied understanding is critical for recognizing others as moral agents
- Disembodied interactions make it harder to form meaningful relationships
- Physical presence enables 'shared history' and 'participatory sense-making'

## AI Impact on Habituation
AI systems can undermine habituation processes by:
- Automating routine tasks that provide necessary practice opportunities
- Offloading judgment to AI tools, depriving users of 'cognitive musculature' strengthening
- Creating environments where users respond to AI outputs rather than initiating activities
- Reducing opportunities for embodied, intersubjective learning experiences

## Market Research Context
In market research, habituation processes are critical for:
- Developing analytical reasoning through repeated data interpretation
- Building practical wisdom in research design and methodology selection
- Cultivating social capacities through participant interaction
- Developing creative capacities for innovative research approaches

AI adoption risks include:
- Automated survey analysis removing opportunities for junior researchers to develop analytical skills
- AI-generated research reports diminishing opportunities for habituation through failure
- Remote research tools reducing embodied interaction with participants
