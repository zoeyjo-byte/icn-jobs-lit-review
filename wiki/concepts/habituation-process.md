# Habituation Process

**First Observed:** 2026-07-03  
**Last Updated:** 2026-09-25

## Definition

The gradual process of becoming skilled through practice, involving inculcation through continued attunement to demands of individual cases, with critical implications for AI's impact on skill development. Skill development cannot be learned through testimony alone but requires practice, encountering and overcoming disappointment and failure, as detailed in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]].

## Components

Ferdman (2026) identifies two essential components of the habituation process:

### 1. Intersubjectivity

> "Skill habituation has a 'shared understanding' dimension. First, becoming skilled typically requires a mentor to guide the novice into becoming an expert that engages in practice of the right kind. A good mentor is typically necessary in order to cultivate the proper motivational structure for becoming skilled."

The mentor instills not only mechanical aspects of the skill but also the value of the skill, creating "shared valuing" where the skilled agent eventually views the value of the skill as their own.

### 2. Embodiment

> "Being embodied is critical for becoming skilled insofar as the process of 'shared valuing' of the skill requires some degree of doing something with the mentor(s), creating a shared history and a participatory sense-making regarding both the product of the skill and the skill itself."

Embodied interaction is essential for recognizing others as moral agents and forming the relationships necessary for skill transmission.

## Threats from AI

Ferdman identifies several ways AI threatens the habituation process:

> "Automating routine tasks and leaving only exception-handling to the human user, 'deprives the user of the routine opportunities to practice their judgment and strengthen their cognitive musculature, leaving them atrophied and unprepared when the exceptions do arise.'"

> "Without being embodied in a social interaction—without the return of the gaze of the other—we would be hard-pressed to recognize them spontaneously as moral agents."

Specific threats to market research skill development:
- AI tools that automate routine analysis tasks without preserving learning pathways
- Digital mediation that reduces embodied interaction necessary for qualitative research skills
- Over-reliance on AI-generated insights that bypass the habituation process

## Preservation Strategies

Ferdman suggests approaches to preserve habituation in AI-augmented environments:

1. **Design for practice**: Ensure AI tools preserve opportunities for researchers to engage in meaningful practice
2. **Maintain mentorship**: Create structured opportunities for intersubjective learning even with AI assistance
3. **Balance automation**: Automate drudgery while preserving critical learning pathways
4. **Create reflection points**: Build in moments for researchers to analyze their decision-making processes

## Market Research Applications

In market research, preserving habituation is critical for:
- Developing qualitative interviewing skills that require embodied presence
- Building judgment in interpreting ambiguous data
- Cultivating the ability to recognize emerging patterns in research
- Developing the capacity to adjust research approaches based on field experience
