# Epistemic Capacities

First observed: 2026-07-18
Last updated: 2026-07-21

## Definition

Epistemic capacities are the human capacities related to knowledge, reasoning, and understanding, including theoretical rationality and practical rationality (phronesis), which are essential for navigating truth and making informed decisions. As established in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]], these capacities are constitutive of human flourishing rather than merely instrumental.

## Characteristics

According to Ferdman's analysis, epistemic capacities have several critical features:

1. **Theoretical rationality**: Involves thinking, considering reasons to believe, forming beliefs, and contemplating.

2. **Practical rationality (phronesis)**: Manifests in common sense or wisdom - doing the right thing in the right way for the right reason.

3. **Constitutive value**: These capacities are not merely useful but constitutive of flourishing: "The competent exercise of the human capacities is the manifestation of the achievement of the intrinsically valuable goods."

4. **Development process**: Requires habituation through practice, encountering and overcoming disappointment, with intersubjective and embodied components.

## Examples in Market Research

Several research practices engage epistemic capacities:

- **Theoretical rationality**: Critical analysis of data, evaluating evidence quality, identifying logical fallacies in arguments

- **Practical rationality**: Making judgment calls about research design, determining appropriate analytical approaches, navigating ethical dilemmas

- **Wisdom development**: Learning when to apply standard methodologies versus developing novel approaches for unique research challenges

- **Truth navigation**: Distinguishing between meaningful insights and spurious patterns in data, particularly with AI-generated analyses

## Contrast with Information Processing

Epistemic capacities should be contrasted with mere information processing. As Ferdman argues, capacity-deskilling is harmful because it diminishes us as humans, not merely because it reduces efficiency. Epistemic capacities involve more than data analysis - they encompass the wisdom to determine what matters.
