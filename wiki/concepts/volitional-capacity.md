# Volitional Capacity

The human capacity to exert effort, overcome difficulty, persevere, and grow in order to achieve intrinsically valuable goals, treated as a meta-capacity necessary for developing all other capacities. As detailed in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]], this capacity is essential for initiating plan-forming and developing other human capacities.

## Definition and Characteristics

Volitional capacity encompasses:

- The ability to exert effort toward valuable goals
- Overcoming difficulty and persevering through challenges
- Growing through the process of capacity development
- The capacity to initiate activities rather than merely respond to triggers
- The meta-capacity required for developing all other human capacities

This capacity is fundamental because developing and exercising any of the other human capacities (epistemic, social, creative) is difficult, which therefore requires the competent exercise of volitional capacity.

## Key Components

### Effort and Perseverance

- The ability to exert sustained effort toward challenging goals
- Overcoming obstacles and persevering through difficulty
- Maintaining commitment to long-term development

### Initiation of Activity

- The capacity to initiate plan-forming rather than merely follow plans
- Taking control over starting activities rather than responding to environmental triggers
- The agential control necessary to begin the process of capacity development

## Why Volitional Capacity Matters

This capacity is fundamental because:

1. **It's a meta-capacity**: "Developing and exercising any of the human capacities is difficult, which therefore requires the competent exercise of the capacity to will. I will therefore treat the capacity to will as a meta-capacity of sorts."

2. **It enables capacity development**: Without volitional capacity, persons cannot initiate the habituation process necessary for developing other capacities

3. **It supports plan-forming**: Volitional capacity is required to initiate the planning activity itself, rather than merely following pre-constructed plans

4. **It's threatened by AI**: Capacity-hostile environments afford opportunities for not exercising volitional capacity

## Threats from AI Systems

AI systems threaten volitional capacity through:

- **Automation of effort**: Systems that eliminate the need to exert effort in routine tasks
- **Reduced initiation opportunities**: Interfaces that prompt users to respond rather than initiate activities
- **Plan-following environments**: Creating contexts where users respond to APA outputs rather than initiating planning
- **Shrinking affordance fields**: Environments that systematically reduce opportunities to exercise willpower

## Case Study: Artificial Personal Assistants

APAs threaten volitional capacity by:

- Replacing the need for users to initiate life-planning activities
- Providing ready-made plans that users follow without exerting effort
- Creating environments where users respond to APA prompts rather than initiating action
- Eliminating opportunities to develop perseverance through small-scale decision-making

As the paper states: "In capacity-hostile environments, the technology or tool will afford users the opportunity of not exercising their willpower, for example by replacing human activity with automated activity, or by offloading human activity... Insofar as the automation replaces human activity without encouraging users to seek alternative avenues to exercise their capacities, including the capacity to will, we may categorize these automation environments as capacity-hostile."

## Implications for AI Design

To protect volitional capacity, AI systems should:

- Preserve opportunities for users to exert effort in meaningful ways
- Create interfaces that encourage initiation of activities rather than mere response
- Avoid complete automation of routine tasks that provide opportunities for willpower development
- Design for environments that afford challenges requiring perseverance
