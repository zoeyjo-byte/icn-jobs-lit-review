# [[volitional-capacity]]

## Definition
The human capacity to **exert effort, overcome difficulty, persevere, and grow** in order to achieve intrinsically valuable goals, treated as a meta-capacity necessary for developing all other capacities. As established in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]], this capacity is essential for the development and exercise of all other human capacities.

## Philosophical Foundation
Ferdman identifies volitional capacity as:
- The ability to **exert effort** in pursuit of valuable goals
- The capacity to **overcome difficulty** and persevere through challenges
- The meta-capacity required for **growing** in competence and understanding
- Essential for achieving **intrinsically valuable goals**

As Ferdman explains: *'The capacity to will (volitional capacity): the ability to exert effort, overcome difficulty, persevere and grow, in order to achieve an intrinsically valuable goal (Bradford 2015). Importantly, developing and exercising any of the human capacities is difficult, which therefore requires the competent exercise of the capacity to will. I will therefore treat the capacity to will as a meta-capacity of sorts.'*

## Critical Role in Human Flourishing
Volitional capacity is constitutive of flourishing because:
- It is **necessary for developing all other capacities**
- It enables **persistence through the habituation process**
- It supports **overcoming disappointment and failure** in skill development
- It is essential for **achieving intrinsically valuable goods**

## Threats from AI Systems
AI systems undermine volitional capacity through:

### Artificial Personal Assistants
APAs disrupt volitional development by:
- **Offloading effort** required for routine decision-making
- Reducing opportunities to **overcome difficulty** in planning activities
- Creating dependency that **diminishes sense of personal competence**
- Undermining **plan-forming** which requires volitional initiation

### Automation Design
Many AI implementations threaten volitional capacity by:
- **Depriving users of routine opportunities** to exercise willpower
- Creating environments where **effort is minimized** through seamless automation
- **Narrowing the field of affordances** for challenging activities
- Encouraging **plan-following rather than plan-forming**

## Evidence of Erosion
Ferdman documents growing evidence of:
- **Reduced sense of personal competence** (Gabriel et al. 2024)
- **Erosion of critical thinking** (Bonicalzi et al. 2023; Gerlich 2025; Lee et al. 2025)
- **Decline in decision-making** and analytical reasoning (Zhai et al. 2024)
- **Diminished capacity** to respond to unexpected events

## Implications for Market Research
In market research organizations, threats to volitional capacity manifest as:
- **[[entry-level-squeeze]]** where AI automates foundational tasks
- **[[human-operating-model-gap]]** between AI capabilities and human systems
- **Erosion of perseverance** documented in industry studies
- **Reduced capacity** to overcome difficulty in complex research problems

## Preserving Volitional Capacity
Organizations can protect volitional capacity by:
- Designing AI tools that **preserve meaningful challenges**
- Creating environments with **appropriate friction points**
- Ensuring researchers maintain **final decision authority** over challenging tasks
- Providing opportunities for **embodied problem-solving** that cannot be fully automated
- Building **failure-tolerant environments** where overcoming difficulty becomes part of learning

As Ferdman concludes, volitional capacity is not merely instrumental but constitutive of human flourishing. Protecting it requires societal commitment to fostering capacity-conducive environments in the age of AI that encourage effort, perseverance, and growth rather than minimizing difficulty through seamless automation.
