# Embodiment in Skilling

The essential role of physical presence and sensory experience in the development of human capacities, particularly social and moral skills. Embodied interaction is critical for recognizing others as moral agents and forming meaningful relationships, as detailed in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]].

## Definition and Characteristics

Embodiment in skilling refers to:

- The necessity of physical presence for developing certain human capacities
- The role of sensory experience in moral attention to others
- The importance of voice, gaze, and physical cues in social cognition
- The concept of "embodied understanding" where persons apprehend others as similar loci of embodied subjectivity

This concept recognizes that some capacities cannot be fully developed without embodied interaction, as "the spontaneity of recognizing the other as moral agent is inherent in the embodied interaction."

## Key Insights from Research

### Embodied Social Cognition

- According to the Enactive Approach, embodied "participatory sense-making" is constitutive of social cognition
- Social interaction itself constitutes an essential part of the cognitive process between participants
- Meaning is generated and transformed in the interplay between unfolding interaction and individuals engaged

### Moral Recognition

- Without embodied interaction, recognizing others as moral agents requires more mental effort
- The "return of the gaze of the other" is crucial for spontaneous recognition of moral agency
- Disembodied interactions make it harder to form meaningful friendships because they require more discipline for diminished experience

## Why Embodiment Matters for Capacity Development

Embodiment is essential because:

1. **It enables moral attention**: Physical presence allows for perceiving others as embodied beings similar to ourselves

2. **It supports intersubjective habituation**: Becoming skilled in social capacities requires embodied interaction with mentors

3. **It creates shared history**: Physical co-presence enables the "shared history and participatory sense-making" necessary for shared valuing

4. **It triggers capacities**: Environments must afford embodied interaction to trigger the development of social and moral capacities

## Threats from AI Systems

AI systems threaten embodiment in skilling through:

- **Disembodied interfaces**: Digital platforms that replace face-to-face interaction with screen-based communication

- **Reduced physical presence**: Systems that encourage users to interact primarily through disembodied mediums

- **Narrowing affordance fields**: Creating environments with limited opportunities for embodied interaction

- **Automation bias**: Offloading tasks to machines reduces opportunities for embodied practice of capacities

## Case Study: Artificial Personal Assistants

APAs threaten embodiment in skilling by replacing human interaction with digital interfaces. As the paper states: "APAs decrease embodied, intersubjective interaction involved in life-planning activities." When users prefer APA advice over that of friends or colleagues, they undermine the shared experiences necessary for testing and growing human capacities.

## Implications for AI Design

To preserve embodiment in capacity development:

- Design systems that complement rather than replace face-to-face interaction
- Create interfaces that encourage physical presence when developing social capacities
- Avoid systems that systematically reduce opportunities for embodied interaction
- Support rather than undermine environments that afford embodied social practices
