# [[embodiment-in-skilling]]

## 2026-08-26 Update

The essential role of physical presence and sensory experience in the development of human capacities, particularly social and moral skills. Embodied interaction is critical for recognizing others as moral agents and forming meaningful relationships, as analyzed in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]].

### Characteristics
- Involves "embodied understanding" where perceived bodies are recognized as similar loci of embodied subjectivity
- Creates "participatory sense-making" constitutive of social cognition
- Enables spontaneous recognition of others as moral agents through embodied interaction
- Provides the return of the gaze essential for moral attention
- Facilitates the shared history necessary for shared valuing of capacities

### Contrast with Disembodied Interaction
| **Embodied Interaction** | **Disembodied Interaction** |
|-------------------------|----------------------------|
| Enables spontaneous moral recognition | Requires more mental effort for moral attribution |
| Facilitates participatory sense-making | Limits shared understanding development |
| Supports spontaneous relationship formation | Requires more discipline for diminished experience |
| Provides return of the gaze | Lacks embodied feedback cues |
| Creates shared history through physical presence | Limits opportunities for shared valuing |

### Threats from AI in Market Research
Ferdman identifies several AI-related threats to embodiment in skilling:
- **Remote research methods**: Digital platforms that replace face-to-face respondent interaction
- **Algorithmic matching**: Systems that match researchers with respondents without embodied engagement
- **Virtual collaboration tools**: Platforms that reduce opportunities for spontaneous interaction among research teams
- **Automated analysis**: Tools that process human responses without requiring researchers to engage directly with respondents

### Market Research Examples
- Online survey platforms that eliminate opportunities for researchers to observe respondent body language and emotional cues
- AI-powered respondent matching that reduces the need for researchers to develop nuanced understanding through direct interaction
- Virtual focus groups that lack the embodied presence necessary for deep rapport building
- Automated transcription tools that process interviews without requiring researchers to fully engage with the emotional dimensions of responses

### Preservation Strategies
- Preserve opportunities for face-to-face research methods where appropriate
- Design hybrid research approaches that combine digital tools with embodied interaction
- Create organizational practices that value embodied research experiences
- Develop training programs emphasizing the importance of physical presence in research quality
- Implement debriefing sessions where researchers share embodied experiences from fieldwork

### Connection to Capacity Development
Embodiment is essential for developing social and moral capacities in market research. Without opportunities for embodied interaction, researchers cannot develop the nuanced understanding of human behavior that forms the foundation of quality insights. Ferdman argues that preserving embodiment in skilling requires structural solutions at the organizational level, particularly in designing research workflows that maintain opportunities for physical presence and sensory engagement. In market research specifically, this means recognizing that some aspects of human understanding cannot be fully captured through disembodied digital methods, and intentionally preserving spaces for embodied research practice.
