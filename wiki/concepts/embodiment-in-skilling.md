# Embodiment in Skilling

## Definition
The **essential role of physical presence and sensory experience** in the development of human capacities, particularly social and moral skills. Embodied interaction is critical for recognizing others as moral agents and forming meaningful relationships, as detailed in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]].

## Philosophical Foundation
Drawing on the Enactive Approach in cognition, Ferdman explains:
- Embodied 'participatory sense-making' is constitutive of social cognition
- Moral attention requires perceiving others as embodied beings through voice and gaze
- One apprehends other perceived bodies as similar loci of embodied subjectivity
- Social interaction itself constitutes an essential part of the cognitive process

Crucially, without embodied interaction:
- Recognizing others as moral agents becomes more difficult
- Forming meaningful friendships requires more discipline for diminished experience
- Developing social capacities is significantly impaired

## Impact on Market Research
Embodiment is particularly relevant to insights work:
- **Human-intensive-skills** like empathy and cultural understanding require embodied interaction
- Client relationships depend on physical presence and non-verbal communication
- Team collaboration benefits from shared physical spaces
- Ethnographic research relies fundamentally on embodied presence

## Threat from AI and Digital Mediation
AI systems can undermine embodied skilling by:
- Creating disembodied interaction environments
- Reducing opportunities for face-to-face engagement
- Fragmenting user attention through disconnected digital fields
- Encouraging shallow engagement rather than deep interaction

As Ferdman states: "Without being embodied in a social interaction—without the return of the gaze of the other—we would be hard-pressed to recognize them spontaneously as moral agents."

## Maintaining Embodiment in AI Adoption
Organizations can preserve embodied skilling by:
- Designing hybrid work models that maintain physical interaction
- Preserving opportunities for face-to-face client engagement
- Creating spaces for unstructured embodied interaction
- Recognizing that some research methods cannot be fully virtualized
