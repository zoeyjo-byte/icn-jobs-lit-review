# [[embodiment-in-skilling]]

## Definition

The essential role of physical presence and sensory experience in the development of human capacities, particularly social and moral skills, as analyzed in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]]. Embodiment represents the physical dimension of capacity cultivation that is systematically undermined in disembodied digital environments.

## Philosophical Foundation

Ferdman draws on the Enactive Approach in cognition to explain embodiment's critical role:

> "Successful habituation must include an embodied component. According to the Enactive Approach in cognition, embodied 'participatory sense-making' (De Jaegher and Di Paolo 2007) is constitutive of social cognition (Di Paolo and Thompson 2014), for example in paying moral attention to the other, because the process of habituation of moral attention requires perceiving the other as an embodied being, who is similar to ourselves, through their voice and their gaze. One comes to apprehend other perceived bodies as being similar loci of embodied subjectivity, creating 'embodied understanding' (Gallagher and Zahavi 2013)."

The paper emphasizes that embodiment is not merely helpful but constitutive of certain capacities:

> "Crucially, without being embodied in a social interaction—without the return of the gaze of the other—we would be hard-pressed to recognize them spontaneously as moral agents. The spontaneity of recognizing the other as moral agent is inherent in the embodied interaction, whereas disembodied interaction requires the agent to exercise more mental efforts in attributing moral agency to the other."

## Critical Functions of Embodiment

Embodiment serves three essential functions in capacity development:

1. **Spontaneous recognition**: Enables immediate recognition of others as moral agents through embodied cues
2. **Participatory sense-making**: Creates shared understanding through physical co-presence
3. **Shared valuing foundation**: Provides the basis for developing shared understanding of skill importance

As Ferdman explains:

> "Being embodied is critical for becoming skilled insofar as the process of 'shared valuing' of the skill requires some degree of doing something with the mentor(s), creating a shared history and a participatory sense-making regarding both the product of the skill and the skill itself."

## Threats from Digital Environments

Disembodied digital environments threaten capacity development through:

- **Reduced spontaneity**: Requiring "more mental efforts in attributing moral agency to the other"
- **Diminished participatory sense-making**: Limiting opportunities for co-created understanding
- **Shallow shared valuing**: Making it harder to develop deep appreciation for capacities
- **Fragmented attention**: Digital interfaces often encourage "determinate, separate, and repeatable tasks" that fracture users' affordance space

Ferdman specifically notes:

> "Similarly, for the good of friendship, disembodied interactions make it harder to form meaningful friendship because technologically mediated virtue friendships require more discipline for a diminished experience (Grasso 2025)."

## Market Research Implications

Embodiment is particularly critical in market research contexts where:

- **Client relationships** depend on embodied cues to build trust and understanding
- **Ethnographic research** requires physical presence to observe contextual behaviors
- **Team collaboration** benefits from embodied interaction for nuanced communication
- **Emotional intelligence** develops through face-to-face interaction with research participants

The shift toward remote work and AI-mediated interactions creates significant challenges for developing these embodied capacities, contributing to the **entry-level squeeze** where traditional training grounds disappear.

## Preserving Embodied Skilling

Strategies to maintain embodied capacity development in digital environments include:

- Designing hybrid research workflows that preserve critical embodied interactions
- Creating intentional opportunities for face-to-face mentorship and team collaboration
- Developing reflection practices that help researchers extract embodied learning from digital interactions
- Using AI tools that enhance rather than replace embodied research methods
- Structuring client interactions to maintain physical co-presence where essential
