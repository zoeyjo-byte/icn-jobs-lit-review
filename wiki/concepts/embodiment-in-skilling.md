# [[embodiment-in-skilling]]

## Definition
Embodiment in skilling refers to the essential role of physical presence and sensory experience in the development of human capacities, particularly social and moral skills. Embodied interaction is critical for recognizing others as moral agents and forming meaningful relationships, as detailed in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]].

## Philosophical Foundation
Ferdman's analysis draws on the Enactive Approach in cognition, which posits that:

- "Embodied 'participatory sense-making' is constitutive of social cognition"
- Social interaction itself and its emergent dynamics constitute an essential part of the cognitive process
- "Meaning is generated and transformed in the interplay between the unfolding interaction process and the individuals engaged in it"

## Critical Role in Capacity Development
Embodiment plays several indispensable roles in human capacity development:

1. **Moral Recognition**: "Without being embodied in a social interaction—without the return of the gaze of the other—we would be hard-pressed to recognize them spontaneously as moral agents"

2. **Shared Valuing**: "The process of 'shared valuing' of the skill requires some degree of doing something with the mentor(s), creating a shared history and a participatory sense-making regarding both the product of the skill and the skill itself"

3. **Friendship Development**: Disembodied interactions make it "harder to form meaningful friendship because technologically mediated virtue friendships require more discipline for a diminished experience"

4. **Capacity Triggering**: Environments must not only afford but "encourage embodied interaction" to ensure "the activation of the capacities"

## Threats from Digital Environments
Ferdman identifies how disembodied digital environments undermine capacity development:

- **Reduced Moral Recognition**: "Disembodied interaction requires the agent to exercise more mental efforts in attributing moral agency to the other"

- **Shallow Relationships**: Without embodied interaction, it becomes "much more difficult to achieve" meaningful relationships compared to interactions with significant embodiment

- **Narrow Affordance Fields**: Digital environments often provide "disconnected fields" of interaction that "fracture the user's affordance space" rather than offering opportunities for mutually informing and enriching capacities

- **Value Transmission Loss**: The absence of embodied interaction limits opportunities for developing "shared valuing" of capacities

## Implications for Market Research
For the market research industry, embodiment in skilling has critical implications:

- Qualitative research methods depend on embodied interaction for deep understanding
- Client relationship building requires physical presence for trust development
- Team collaboration benefits from embodied interaction for shared understanding
- The shift to remote work and AI tools creates risks for capacity deskilling through reduced embodied interaction

## Contrast with Disembodied Interaction
Ferdman emphasizes that while shared valuing "might be achieved in the absence of any embodied interaction," it would be "much more difficult to achieve, compared to interactions that involve a significant level of embodiment."
