# [[embodiment-in-skilling]]

## Definition
Embodiment in skilling refers to the essential role of physical presence and sensory experience in the development of human capacities, particularly social and moral skills. As established in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]], embodied interaction is critical for recognizing others as moral agents and forming meaningful relationships, with significant implications for AI's impact on capacity development.

## Philosophical Foundation
Ferdman draws on the Enactive Approach in cognition to explain that:
- Embodied 'participatory sense-making' is constitutive of social cognition
- The process of habituation of moral attention requires perceiving others as embodied beings
- One comes to apprehend other perceived bodies as similar loci of embodied subjectivity
- Social interaction itself constitutes an essential part of the cognitive process
- Meaning is generated in the interplay between unfolding interaction and individuals

## Critical Role in Capacity Development
Embodiment is critical for:
- Recognizing others as moral agents spontaneously through embodied interaction
- Forming meaningful friendships that require shared physical presence
- Developing moral capacities through embodied social practices
- Creating shared history and participatory sense-making regarding skills

## Implications for AI Systems
Ferdman argues that disembodied AI interaction environments can undermine capacity development through:
- Making it harder to recognize others as moral agents without the return of the gaze
- Requiring more mental effort to attribute moral agency to others
- Creating technologically mediated virtue friendships that require more discipline for diminished experience
- Narrowing the affordance space into disconnected fields that lead to alienation

## Professional Context
In market research, embodiment in skilling is essential for:
- Developing moral sensitivity in handling sensitive data
- Building rapport with research participants
- Interpreting non-verbal cues in qualitative research
- Cultivating practical wisdom through embodied professional practice

## Capacity-Conducive Design
Ferdman suggests that capacity-conducive environments should include:
- Public goods like community centers and quality public spaces
- Opportunities for structured and unstructured embodied interactions
- Settings that encourage shared experiences necessary for shared valuing
- Physical environments that trigger multiple capacities simultaneously
