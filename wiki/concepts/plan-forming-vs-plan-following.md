# Plan-Forming vs. Plan-Following

The distinction between plan-forming and plan-following highlights a critical shift in human agency induced by AI systems, particularly Artificial Personal Assistants (APAs). As analyzed in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]], plan-forming requires self-deliberation and agential control, while plan-following represents a habitual mode of action triggered by environmental cues (i.e., AI outputs).

## Plan-Forming
- **Definition:** Actively creating plans through self-deliberation, involving the settling of options and coordination of intentions.
- **Capacities Exercised:** Requires epistemic capacities (theoretical & practical rationality), social/moral capacities (aligning plans with relationships and values), creative capacities (adapting to surprises), and volitional capacity (initiating the effort).
- **Agential Control:** The user initiates the activity, maintains control over execution, and takes responsibility for outcomes. This fosters practical wisdom and personal identity.
- **Value:** Life-planning is self-constituting; it shapes character and dispositions rather than merely achieving instrumental goals.

## Plan-Following
- **Definition:** Executing pre-determined plans generated by external entities (e.g., APAs, algorithms) without exercising agential control over the planning process.
- **Mechanism:** The user responds to a trigger in the environment (the AI's output) rather than initiating self-deliberation. This shifts the agent from a plan-former to a plan-follower.
- **Capacity Erosion:** Offloading planning to AI reduces the need for routine decision-making, undermining the habituation of capacities necessary for trusting one's own judgment. Responsibility for failure can be shifted to the tool, forgoing opportunities for self-growth.
- **Risk:** Encourages shallow skilling and capacity impoverishment, as users bypass the difficult but necessary process of developing practical wisdom through real-world experience and failure.

## Implications for Market Research
Research workflows that rely heavily on AI-generated insights, automated reporting, or algorithmic survey design risk pushing practitioners into plan-following modes. To preserve professional expertise, organizations must design systems that require researchers to formulate hypotheses, design methodologies, and interpret findings (plan-forming), using AI only as a supportive tool for data processing and pattern recognition.
