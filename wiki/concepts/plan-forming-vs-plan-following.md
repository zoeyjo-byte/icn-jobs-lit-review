# Plan-Forming vs Plan-Following

**First Observed**: 2026-07-17 | **Last Updated**: 2026-07-22

The plan-forming vs plan-following distinction refers to the critical difference between actively creating plans through self-deliberation (requiring agential control) versus executing pre-determined plans (more habitual), with critical implications for AI's impact on capacity development as analyzed in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]]. This distinction reveals how AI systems can undermine human capacities by encouraging plan-following rather than plan-forming.

## Philosophical Foundation
Drawing on Bratman's theory of agency, planning is a manifestation of practical rationality where:
- **Plan-forming** involves future-directed intentions that play two agential roles:
  * Settling function: Managing finite cognitive resources when facing choices
  * Coordination function: Forming and organizing different types of plans and intentions
- **Plan-following** involves responding to triggers in the environment without initiating the planning process

## Key Differences

| Characteristic | Plan-Forming | Plan-Following |
|----------------|--------------|----------------|
| **Agency** | Requires agential control | Triggered by environmental cues |
| **Initiation** | Agent-controlled | Environment-initiated |
| **Responsibility** | Taking ownership of outcomes | Shifting responsibility to external source |
| **Capacity Development** | Builds epistemic and volitional capacities | Diminishes capacity through habituation |
| **Error Response** | Learning from failures | Avoiding responsibility for failures |

## AI Impact Analysis
Artificial Personal Assistants (APAs) exemplify how AI systems encourage plan-following:

### APA Design Features
- "Always-on sounding boards" configured with preferred interaction tones
- Constant availability that discourages users from initiating planning activities
- Integration with multiple AI tools that can "do everything for everyone" (Gebru and Torres 2023)
- Example: Sunsama scheduling app plans users' days, removing opportunities for self-directed planning

### Capacity Deskilling Mechanisms
- **Reduced self-deliberation**: APAs provide ready-made plans, diminishing opportunities for theoretical rationality
- **Diminished practical wisdom**: Users don't develop "effective means to one's ends" through real-world experience
- **Weakened volitional capacity**: Less need to exert effort in planning activities reduces perseverance development
- **Erosion of responsibility**: Failed AI-generated plans allow users to shift responsibility, avoiding self-criticism

## Structural Implications
The plan-forming vs plan-following distinction reveals why deskilling is a structural problem:
- Environments that encourage plan-following create narrow fields of affordances for capacity development
- Over-reliance on APAs acclimates users to responding to environmental triggers rather than exercising agency
- Long-term reliance limits agents' ability to transition to independent planning
- Unlike human mentors, APAs don't know "when to remove themselves as training wheels" (Liu et al. 2022)

## Contrast with Individual-Responsibility Approaches
Unlike frameworks treating deskilling as individual failing, this distinction recognizes:
- The structural design of AI systems actively encourages plan-following
- Users cannot be expected to resist plan-following when environments are designed to discourage plan-forming
- Capacity-hostile environments systematically undermine opportunities for agential control
- Example: Students using AI writing assistants may struggle to develop independent planning skills

## Preservation Strategies
Protecting plan-forming capacity requires:
- Designing AI tools as scaffolds rather than replacements for planning activities
- Creating environments that encourage rather than discourage initiating planning
- Preserving opportunities for embodied, intersubjective planning with mentors
- Supporting public spaces that facilitate face-to-face planning and coordination
