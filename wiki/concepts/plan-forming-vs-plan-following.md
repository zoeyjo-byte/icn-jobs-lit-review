# Plan-Forming vs Plan-Following

## Definition

Plan-forming refers to the active process of creating plans through self-deliberation, requiring the exercise of [[agential-control]] and multiple human capacities. Plan-following refers to executing pre-determined plans, often in response to environmental triggers, with less exercise of agential control.

## Key Distinctions

| Plan-Forming | Plan-Following |
|--------------|----------------|
| Requires initiating the planning activity | Responds to triggers in the environment |
| Involves settling function (deciding between options) | Follows predetermined options |
| Involves coordination function (organizing different plans) | Executes a single predetermined plan |
| Requires exercising epistemic, social, moral and creative capacities | Requires shallower exercise of capacities |
| Involves taking responsibility for outcomes | May shift responsibility to the plan creator |
| Develops practical wisdom through experience | Limits development of practical wisdom |

## Implications for AI

As analyzed in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]], AI systems, particularly [[artificial-personal-assistants]], risk creating environments where users primarily engage in plan-following rather than plan-forming. When users rely on AI to create their daily schedules, life plans, or decision pathways, they become habituated to responding to AI-generated prompts rather than developing their own planning capacities.

The paper argues that "forming a plan by self-deliberation requires exercising control over all the steps of the activity of forming the plan, including over initiating the two functions of planning: settling (deciding between options) and coordinating." In contrast, "relying on an APA to form our plans for us is more like a habit that is developed in response to a trigger in the environment."

## Research Findings

Ferdman's research indicates that over-reliance on AI for planning can lead to:
- Reduced sense of personal competence
- Diminished practical wisdom development
- Limited real-world experience in handling unexpected events
- Weakened capacity to will oneself into initiating planning activities
