# Capacity-Hostile Environments

**Capacity-hostile environments** are socio-technical contexts that restrict, limit, or create a narrow field of affordances for the development and exercise of core human capacities. These environments encourage shallow skilling while systematically discouraging users from compensating for the loss of deeper competencies.

## Defining Characteristics
An environment is classified as capacity-hostile when it exhibits one or more of the following traits:
1. **Narrow Field of Affordances**: The environment offers limited action possibilities, often replacing complex human activities with automated or simplified alternatives.
2. **Disembodied Interaction**: It prioritizes digital or algorithmic mediation over face-to-face, embodied engagement, making it harder to develop moral and social capacities.
3. **Reduced Intersubjectivity**: It limits opportunities for shared valuing, mentorship, and communal practices that are essential for deep habituation.

## Contrast with Capacity-Conducive Environments
**Capacity-conducive environments** do the opposite: they afford opportunities for embodied, intersubjective habituation, encourage the exercise of the **[[volitional-capacity]]**, and support the competent development of **[[epistemic-capacities]]**, **[[social-capacities]]**, and **[[creative-capacity]]**. They include public goods like community centers, libraries, and unstructured social spaces that invite structured and unstructured embodied interactions.

## Structural Implications
The concept shifts the focus of deskilling from individual virtue ethics to structural design. Rather than expecting users to become "virtuous superheroes" who will themselves to resist AI dependency, capacity-hostile environments highlight how socio-technical systems can inadvertently erode human flourishing. AI automation that offloads moral reasoning, life-planning, or routine judgment without encouraging alternative capacity exercise is a primary driver of this phenomenon.
