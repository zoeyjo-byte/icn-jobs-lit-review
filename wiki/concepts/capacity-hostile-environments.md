# Capacity-Hostile Environments

**Definition:** Environments that restrict, limit, or create a narrow field of [[concepts/affordances|affordances]] for capacity development and exercise. These environments afford opportunities for shallow skilling but restrict opportunities for fuller, well-rounded development.

## Theoretical Framework
Capacity-hostile environments are contrasted with [[concepts/capacity-conducive-environments|capacity-conducive environments]]. The structural dimension of capacity-deskilling is captured by the concept of affordances: the environment offers action possibilities that invite agents to exercise specific capacities. When AI tools narrow this field systematically, it leads to [[concepts/affordance-shrinkage|affordance shrinkage]], discouraging users from compensating for the loss of action possibilities.

## AI and Affordance Shrinkage
AI automation and mediation can create capacity-hostile environments through several mechanisms:
- **Offloading and Automation:** Replacing routine human activity without encouraging compensation deprives users of the habituation process necessary for developing capacities. This leads to atrophied skills and unpreparedness for exceptions.
- **Disembodied Interaction:** AI mediation often occurs in disembodied environments, which require more mental effort to attribute moral agency and form meaningful relationships. This hinders the [[concepts/embodied-habituation|embodied, intersubjective habituation]] crucial for developing moral and social capacities.
- **Trigger-Based Responses:** APAs are designed to constantly prompt users, inviting them to become "plan-followers" rather than "plan-formers." This shifts the balance from agential control (initiating tasks) to habitual response to environmental triggers.

## Impact on Life-Planning
Life-planning requires the exercise of [[skills/epistemic-capacities|epistemic capacities]], [[skills/social-capacities|social capacities]], [[skills/creative-capacity|creative capacity]], and [[concepts/volitional-capacity|volitional capacity]]. When APAs are used to organize life and find purpose, they risk undermining the shared valuing and intersubjective mentorship that normally accompany life-planning. Users may shift responsibility for failed plans to the AI, forgoing opportunities for self-criticism and growth.
