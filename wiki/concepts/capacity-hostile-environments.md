# [[capacity-hostile-environments]]

## Definition

Environments that **restrict, limit, or create a narrow field of affordances** for the development and exercise of core human capacities, as analyzed in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]]. These environments afford opportunities for shallow skilling in capacities while restricting fuller development and exercise.

## Characteristics

- Provide **limited action possibilities** that discourage the development of human capacities
- Create conditions where users become **"hard-pressed to recognize others spontaneously as moral agents"** due to disembodiment
- **Systematically reduce opportunities** for meaningful capacity cultivation through automation
- Undermine the **habituation process** necessary for skill development
- Encourage **plan-following behavior** rather than plan-forming activities

## Examples in Market Research

- AI tools that **automate routine analytical tasks** without providing alternative pathways for developing analytical reasoning skills
- Research platforms that **eliminate embodied interaction** with respondents, reducing opportunities to develop empathy and social capacities
- **Automated reporting systems** that replace the process of crafting research narratives, undermining development of creative and epistemic capacities
- **AI-powered personal assistants** that take over life-planning activities essential for developing practical wisdom

## Mechanisms of Harm

As Ferdman explains, capacity-hostile environments harm human development through:

> "Indiscriminately automating human activity may create capacity-hostile environments, insofar as users will be deprived of the habituation process necessary for properly developing the capacities involved in routine activities."

These environments also undermine the **intersubjective dimension** of skill development by reducing opportunities for shared valuing of capacities and embodied learning from mentors.

## Contrast with [[capacity-conducive-environments]]

While capacity-hostile environments restrict capacity development, capacity-conducive environments actively encourage it through rich opportunities for embodied interaction and shared valuing. The key distinction lies in whether the environment **affords** versus **encourages** valuable activities.
