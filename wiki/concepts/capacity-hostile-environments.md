## Update (2026-08-20)

Expanded with detailed analysis from [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]]:

Capacity-hostile environments restrict, limit, or create a narrow field of affordances for the development and exercise of human capacities. As Ferdman explains, these environments afford opportunities for shallow skilling while restricting fuller development of capacities.

Key characteristics:
- Restrict opportunities for [[agential-control]] over activities
- Limit [[intersubjectivity-in-skilling]] from mentors and exemplars
- Reduce [[embodiment-in-skilling]] necessary for capacity development
- Create environments where persons respond to triggers rather than initiating activities
- May lead to "affordance shrinkage" - systematic reduction in action possibilities

Examples from the paper:
- AI automation that offloads routine tasks, depriving users of opportunities to "practice their judgment and strengthen their cognitive musculature"
- Disembodied digital environments that make it harder to form meaningful relationships
- [[artificial-personal-assistants]] that encourage "plan-following" rather than "plan-forming"

Market research implications:
- AI tools used in market research could create capacity-hostile environments for researchers if they automate critical thinking processes
- Research firms should evaluate their AI tools based on whether they afford opportunities for researchers to develop and exercise [[epistemic-capacities]]
- Capacity-hostile environments in research could lead to diminished critical analysis of data and reduced creativity in interpreting findings
