# [[capacity-hostile-environments]]

## Definition

Capacity-hostile environments are socio-technical contexts that restrict, limit, or create a narrow field of affordances for the development and exercise of core human capacities. As established in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]], these environments afford opportunities for shallow skilling while restricting fuller development of capacities, with significant implications for AI's impact on human flourishing.

## Characteristics

Capacity-hostile environments exhibit three key characteristics:

1. **Narrow field of affordances**: They systematically reduce action possibilities for valuable activities, creating what Dokumacı (2023) and Krueger (2023) term "affordance shrinkage" - a reduction that is systematic, enduring, and deeply entrenched

2. **Diminished embodiment**: They restrict embodied interaction necessary for social and moral capacities, as Ferdman explains: "Without being embodied in a social interaction—without the return of the gaze of the other—we would be hard-pressed to recognize them spontaneously as moral agents"

3. **Reduced intersubjectivity**: They limit opportunities for shared valuing of capacities through mentor-mentee relationships, as social practices that generate moral reasons for action are diminished

## Examples in Market Research

- **AI-powered analysis tools** that automate data interpretation, depriving researchers of routine opportunities to practice judgment and strengthen their cognitive musculature
- **Fully automated survey platforms** that eliminate the need for researchers to design questions thoughtfully, reducing opportunities to develop **creative capacities**
- **Virtual-only research environments** that minimize face-to-face interaction, undermining the **embodiment in skilling** necessary for nuanced qualitative analysis
- **AI writing assistants** that generate research reports, diminishing opportunities to develop writing skills through practice and feedback

## Consequences

Capacity-hostile environments lead to:
- **Shallow skilling**: Limited development of capacities that cannot support mastery
- **Devaluation of capacities**: When persons don't experience the full maturation of capacities, they cannot learn to value excellence in those capacities
- **Structural deskilling**: Systemic erosion of capacities across organizations rather than individual skill loss

## Contrast with Capacity-Conducive Environments

Unlike [[capacity-conducive-environments]], which actively encourage capacity development through rich opportunities for embodied interaction and shared valuing, capacity-hostile environments create conditions where individuals cannot be expected to "properly cultivate their human capacities if their social environment does not value the full maturation of these capacities."

## Mitigation Strategies

To counter capacity-hostile environments in market research:
- Design AI tools that preserve opportunities for **agential control**
- Maintain structured and unstructured embodied interactions through hybrid research approaches
- Create mentorship programs that foster **intersubjectivity in skilling**
- Implement deliberate practice opportunities for capacities at risk of deskilling
