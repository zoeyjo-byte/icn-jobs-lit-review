## Update 2026-07-25

Expanded definition based on Ferdman's structural analysis in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]]. Capacity-hostile environments restrict, limit, or create a narrow field of affordances for the development and exercise of core human capacities. These environments afford opportunities for shallow skilling while restricting fuller development of capacities, with significant implications for AI's impact on human flourishing.

Key characteristics:
- Restrict opportunities for embodied interaction necessary for social and moral skill development
- Create environments where users respond to triggers rather than exercising agential control
- Undermine the intersubjective dimension of skill development by removing human mentors
- Reduce opportunities for habituation through encountering and overcoming disappointment and failure
- Encourage plan-following rather than plan-forming activities

In market research contexts, capacity-hostile environments may emerge when:
- AI tools automate routine analytical tasks without preserving space for judgment development
- Research processes become disembodied through excessive digital mediation
- Researchers become overly reliant on AI for insight generation rather than developing their own interpretive skills

See [[narrow-field-of-affordances]], [[ai-deskilling-structural-problem]], and [[plan-following]] for related concepts.
