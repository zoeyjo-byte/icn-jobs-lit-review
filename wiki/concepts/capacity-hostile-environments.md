# Capacity-Hostile Environments

First observed: 2026-07-03
Last updated: 2026-07-21

## Definition

Capacity-hostile environments are settings that restrict, limit, or create a narrow field of affordances for the development and exercise of core human capacities. As established in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]], these environments afford opportunities for shallow skilling while restricting fuller development of capacities, with significant implications for AI's impact on human flourishing.

## Characteristics

According to Ferdman's structural analysis, capacity-hostile environments exhibit several key characteristics:

1. **Narrow field of affordances**: They provide limited action possibilities, restricting opportunities for capacity development. This becomes particularly problematic when it leads to "affordance shrinkage" - a systematic, enduring reduction in action possibilities that discourages users from finding compensatory avenues.

2. **Discouragement of embodied interaction**: These environments minimize opportunities for physical presence and sensory experience, which are critical for developing social and moral capacities. As Ferdman notes, "without being embodied in a social interaction... we would be hard-pressed to recognize [others] spontaneously as moral agents."

3. **Reduction of intersubjective learning**: They limit opportunities for learning from mentors who can instill both the mechanical aspects of skills and the shared understanding of their value.

4. **Promotion of plan-following over plan-forming**: They encourage users to respond to environmental triggers (like AI outputs) rather than exercising [[agential-control]] to initiate activities.

## Examples in Market Research

Several contemporary practices may constitute capacity-hostile environments:

- **Fully automated insight generation**: Systems that deliver final insights without requiring researchers to engage in analytical reasoning, depriving them of opportunities to develop [[epistemic-capacities]].

- **Disembodied research methods**: Overreliance on digital-only research that eliminates face-to-face interaction, making it harder to develop the embodied understanding necessary for recognizing participants as moral agents.

- **AI-driven research execution**: Platforms that automate the entire research process from question formulation to reporting, reducing researchers to plan-followers rather than [[plan-forming-vs-plan-following]].

## Contrast with Capacity-Conducive Environments

Capacity-hostile environments should be contrasted with [[capacity-conducive-environments]], which actively encourage capacity development through:

- Rich opportunities for embodied interaction
- Structured and unstructured learning from mentors
- Sufficient challenge that requires exercising [[volitional-capacity]]
- Affordances that encourage plan-forming rather than mere plan-following

## Implications for Skills Gap Research

Understanding capacity-hostile environments is critical for addressing the skills gap:

- Identifies how AI adoption might inadvertently undermine essential human capacities
- Highlights that deskilling is not merely an individual failing but a structural problem requiring organizational solutions
- Suggests that AI implementation strategies should evaluate tools based on their conduciveness to capacity cultivation
- Directly relates to the [[human-operating-model-gap]] between AI capabilities and human systems
