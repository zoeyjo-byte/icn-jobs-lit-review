# Capacity-Hostile Environments

## Definition

Capacity-hostile environments are settings that restrict, limit, or create a narrow field of affordances for the development and exercise of core human capacities, as analyzed in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]].

## Key Characteristics

Capacity-hostile environments:

1. **Restrict action possibilities**: Provide a narrow field of affordances that limit opportunities to engage in activities requiring competent exercise of human capacities
2. **Discourage embodied interaction**: Reduce opportunities for physical presence and sensory experience in skill development
3. **Limit intersubjective habituation**: Restrict opportunities to learn from others and develop shared understanding of skill value
4. **Promote shallow skilling**: Afford opportunities for shallow development of capacities rather than well-rounded mastery
5. **Devalue capacities**: Create conditions where inadequate development of capacities becomes normalized

## AI-Related Examples

As detailed in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]], AI systems can create capacity-hostile environments through:

- **Healthcare AI**: Systems that encourage offloading diagnostic reasoning to machines, leading to "automation bias" where users attribute excessive trust to AI tools over human advice
- **Smartphone interfaces**: Providing "possibilities for interaction that are disconnected from other persons, such as swiping, clicking and scrolling" which "fractures the user's affordance space into disconnected fields, ultimately leading to alienation"
- **Artificial Personal Assistants**: Creating environments where users primarily engage in plan-following rather than plan-forming, reducing opportunities to develop practical wisdom

## Structural Dimensions

The paper identifies three key axes for analyzing whether AI tools create capacity-hostile environments:

1. **AI Automation and the Field of Affordances**: When automation replaces routine human activities without providing alternative avenues for capacity development
2. **Embodied User Engagement**: When interfaces reduce opportunities for physical presence and sensory experience in interactions
3. **Level of Intersubjectivity**: When systems limit opportunities to develop capacities in concert with other humans

## Contrast with Capacity-Conducive Environments

Unlike [[capacity-conducive-environments]], which actively encourage capacity development through rich opportunities for embodied interaction and shared valuing, capacity-hostile environments systematically undermine the conditions necessary for cultivating human capacities.
