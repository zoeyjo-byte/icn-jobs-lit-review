# Capacity-Hostile Environments

Capacity-hostile environments are socio-technical contexts that restrict, limit, or create a narrow field of affordances for the development and exercise of core human capacities. This concept, introduced in Ferdman's 2026 paper "AI Deskilling is a Structural Problem," provides a framework for evaluating how AI systems may undermine human flourishing.

## Definition and Characteristics

A capacity-hostile environment:

1. **Restricts action possibilities**: Limits opportunities to engage in activities requiring competent exercise of human capacities
2. **Encourages shallow skilling**: Affords opportunities for only superficial development of capacities
3. **Undermines habituation**: Disrupts the gradual process of becoming skilled through practice
4. **Discourages shared valuing**: Fails to provide intersubjective contexts where the value of capacities is collectively recognized

These environments are particularly problematic when they lead to "affordance shrinkage" - a systematic, enduring reduction in action possibilities that discourages users from finding compensatory pathways.

## Examples in AI Contexts

Several AI applications create capacity-hostile environments:

- **Over-automated workflows**: When AI systems replace routine tasks without providing opportunities for users to practice judgment, they deprive users of "the routine opportunities to practice their judgment and strengthen their cognitive musculature" (Lee et al. 2025)
- **Disembodied interactions**: Environments that lack physical presence make it harder to form meaningful relationships and develop moral capacities
- **Algorithmic decision-making**: Systems that offload moral reasoning to non-human agents undermine the development of practical wisdom

## Contrast with Capacity-Conducive Environments

Capacity-hostile environments stand in contrast to [[capacity-conducive-environments]], which actively encourage the development and exercise of human capacities through:

- Rich opportunities for embodied interaction
- Intersubjective contexts for shared valuing
- Gradual challenges that support habituation
- Affordances that encourage exercising the capacity to will

## Implications for Market Research

For market research professionals, recognizing capacity-hostile environments is crucial when designing AI-augmented research tools. Systems that automate data collection or analysis without preserving opportunities for researchers to develop critical thinking skills could ultimately undermine the quality of insights produced.

The concept also has implications for understanding how AI affects consumer behavior and decision-making - environments that are hostile to capacity development may produce less thoughtful consumer responses.
