# Capacity-Hostile Environments

## Definition
Environments that restrict, limit, or create a narrow field of affordances for the development and exercise of core human capacities, as analyzed in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]]. These environments afford opportunities for shallow skilling while restricting fuller development of capacities, with significant implications for AI's impact on human flourishing.

## Characteristics

### Narrow Field of Affordances
Capacity-hostile environments provide limited action possibilities that systematically restrict opportunities for capacity development. As Ferdman explains, these environments:
- Create 'affordance shrinkage' that is systematic, enduring, and deeply entrenched
- Fail to encourage (not just afford) valuable activities that trigger capacity development
- Discourage users from finding compensatory pathways due to the cumulative effect of automation

### Automation Bias
These environments often encourage over-reliance on AI systems through:
- Offloading critical tasks to machines (e.g., diagnostic reasoning in healthcare)
- Replacing routine activities that are essential for habituation ('depriving users of routine opportunities to practice their judgment and strengthen their cognitive musculature')
- Creating contexts where users respond to AI outputs as environmental triggers rather than exercising agential control

### Disembodiment
Many capacity-hostile environments feature:
- Reduced embodied interaction that undermines moral attention
- Diminished opportunities for recognizing others as moral agents
- Limited shared experiences necessary for intersubjective habituation

## Examples in Market Research
- AI tools that automate foundational research tasks without providing scaffolding for skill development
- Platforms that replace human interaction in client relationships with chatbots
- Systems that offload analytical reasoning to AI without requiring researchers to justify conclusions
- Tools that generate research reports without requiring researchers to engage in the planning process

## Contrast with Capacity-Conducive Environments
Unlike [[capacity-conducive-environments]], which actively encourage embodied interaction and shared valuing, capacity-hostile environments:
- Restrict opportunities for developing **agential control**
- Undermine the **habituation process** through automation
- Diminish **intersubjectivity in skilling** by reducing human interaction
- Create conditions where users become **plan-followers** rather than **plan-formers**

## Implications
Understanding capacity-hostile environments explains the structural nature of the [[human-operating-model-gap]] and why the [[entry-level-squeeze]] occurs - traditional entry-level positions increasingly fail to provide the affordances necessary for developing professional capacities through habituation.
