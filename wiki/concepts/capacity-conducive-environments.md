# [[capacity-conducive-environments]]

## Definition
Capacity-conducive environments are socio-technical contexts that actively encourage the development and exercise of human capacities through rich opportunities for embodied interaction and shared valuing. As established in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]], these environments provide structured and unstructured embodied interactions necessary for the intersubjective habituation of capacities.

## Characteristics
According to Ferdman's analysis, capacity-conducive environments typically exhibit these features:
- They afford a broad field of action possibilities that trigger multiple human capacities simultaneously
- They encourage embodied interaction, which is essential for recognizing others as moral agents
- They facilitate intersubjective learning processes where mentors instill both mechanical aspects and the value of capacities
- They provide opportunities for developing agential control through self-initiated activities
- They support the cultivation of the [[volitional-capacity]] by presenting appropriately challenging activities

## Examples
Ferdman identifies several examples of capacity-conducive environments:
- Community centers and public spaces that encourage structured and unstructured embodied interactions
- Quality public libraries that afford opportunities for reading and social engagement
- Professional settings with strong mentorship cultures where junior staff learn through guided practice
- Educational environments that balance automation with opportunities for hands-on skill development

## Contrast with Capacity-Hostile Environments
Unlike [[capacity-hostile-environments]], which systematically narrow the field of affordances, capacity-conducive environments provide invitations for valuable activities that trigger the development of multiple capacities. This distinction is critical for designing AI systems that complement rather than undermine human flourishing.

## Structural Design Principles
Ferdman argues that creating capacity-conducive environments requires:
- Ensuring AI systems afford but also encourage embodied interaction
- Designing systems that support rather than replace the habituation process
- Creating opportunities for users to exercise agential control over AI tools
- Preserving spaces for intersubjective learning and shared valuing of capacities
