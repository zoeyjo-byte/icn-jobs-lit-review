## Update: 2026-09-15

According to [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]], capacity-conducive environments should include specific public goods that encourage embodied interaction:

- Community centers
- Quality public spaces
- Public libraries

These environments encourage both structured and unstructured embodied interactions, which are necessary for creating shared experiences. As Ferdman explains: "Embodied engagement in these settings would be more conducive to creating shared experiences, which are necessary for shared valuing of the social capacities."

The paper emphasizes that merely affording opportunities for embodied interaction is insufficient - environments must actively encourage such interaction. As Ferdman states: "To ensure the activation of the capacities, the environment has to encourage the activity of φ-ing, otherwise the capacities might not be triggered."

Capacity-conducive environments support the intersubjective dimension of skill development by providing contexts where mentors and novices can develop "shared valuing" of capacities. They also support the [[habituation-process]] by offering opportunities to encounter and overcome disappointment and failure in a supportive context.

In market research contexts, capacity-conducive environments might include:
- In-person focus groups and ethnographic research settings
- Collaborative workshops where researchers develop skills together
- Mentorship programs that emphasize embodied interaction and shared valuing
- Research settings that balance AI tool usage with opportunities for human judgment and creativity
