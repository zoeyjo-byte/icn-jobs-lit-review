# Capacity-Conducive Environments

Environments that actively encourage the development and exercise of human capacities through rich opportunities for embodied interaction and shared valuing, providing alternatives to capacity-hostile conditions. These environments afford structured and unstructured embodied interactions necessary for intersubjective habituation of capacities.

## Definition and Characteristics

A capacity-conducive environment is one that:

1. Provides a broad field of affordances (rich action possibilities)
2. Encourages rather than merely affords embodied interaction
3. Supports intersubjective learning through mentorship and shared valuing
4. Preserves opportunities for agential control over capacity development
5. Facilitates the habituation process through practice and feedback

Unlike merely providing opportunities, capacity-conducive environments actively encourage engagement with activities that trigger human capacities, recognizing that "merely having the opportunity to φ does not guarantee that the agent will actually φ and engage the capacities involved in φ-ing."

## Examples of Capacity-Conducive Environments

- **Community centers**: Provide spaces for structured and unstructured embodied interactions that foster social capacities

- **Public libraries**: Afford opportunities for reading and learning while encouraging embodied engagement with knowledge

- **Quality public spaces**: Create environments where people can interact face-to-face, developing social and moral capacities

- **Mentorship programs**: Facilitate intersubjective habituation where mentors guide novices in developing capacities

- **Educational settings with embodied interaction**: Classrooms that prioritize face-to-face discussion and collaborative work

## Importance for Capacity Development

Capacity-conducive environments are essential because:

1. **They support habituation**: The gradual process of becoming skilled requires continued attunement to demands of individual cases, refinement through successive trials, and learning from mistakes.

2. **They foster intersubjectivity**: Becoming skilled typically requires a mentor to instill not only mechanical aspects but also the value of the skill, creating "shared valuing" of capacities.

3. **They encourage embodiment**: Physical presence and sensory experience are critical for recognizing others as moral agents and forming meaningful relationships.

4. **They preserve agential control**: Allow users to initiate activities rather than merely responding to environmental triggers.

## Contrast with Capacity-Hostile Environments

While capacity-hostile environments restrict opportunities for capacity development, capacity-conducive environments actively encourage the development and exercise of capacities. The key difference lies in whether the environment merely affords or actively encourages engagement with activities that trigger human capacities.

## Design Principles for AI Systems

To create capacity-conducive AI systems, designers should:

- Preserve opportunities for users to initiate activities (agential control)
- Support rather than replace embodied interaction
- Facilitate rather than undermine intersubjective learning
- Provide feedback that encourages habituation through practice
- Avoid creating environments with a narrow field of affordances
