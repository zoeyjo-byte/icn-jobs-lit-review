# Capacity-Conducive Environments

## Definition
Capacity-conducive environments are socio-technical environments that actively encourage the development and exercise of human capacities through rich opportunities for embodied interaction and shared valuing. These environments provide broad fields of affordances that invite valuable activities triggering associated capacities.

## Key Characteristics
- Afford and **encourage** embodied interaction
- Support **intersubjectivity in skilling** through mentorship and shared valuing
- Provide opportunities for **agential control** over initiating activities
- Facilitate the **habituation process** through gradual, case-by-case attunement
- Encourage **willpower exercise** necessary for capacity development

## Structural Components

### Embodied Interaction Spaces
Environments that afford:
- Physical co-presence for 'participatory sense-making' (De Jaegher & Di Paolo 2007)
- Spontaneous recognition of others as moral agents through gaze and voice
- Shared experiences that build 'embodied understanding' (Gallagher & Zahavi 2013)
- Community centers, public spaces, and libraries that encourage structured/unstructured interaction

### Intersubjective Learning Frameworks
- Mentor-mentee relationships where mentors instill both mechanical aspects and value of skills
- Social practices that generate moral reasons for action (Manne 2013)
- Common life practices that teach 'responsiveness to what skillful action requires' (MacIntyre 2017)

### Agential Control Opportunities
- Environments where users initiate activities rather than respond to triggers
- Spaces that require settling and coordinating functions of planning
- Contexts where responsibility for outcomes is clearly user-owned

## Market Research Applications

### Design Principles
- Preserve embodied interaction in research settings (e.g., in-person focus groups)
- Create mentorship structures that transmit both technical skills and research values
- Design AI tools that maintain researcher agential control over judgment
- Ensure research workflows include opportunities for responsibility-taking

### Implementation Examples
- Hybrid research methodologies combining AI analysis with human interpretation
- Structured debrief sessions after research projects to reinforce learning
- Research communities of practice that share valuing of core capacities
- Training programs emphasizing gradual skill development through case studies

## Contrast with Capacity-Hostile Environments
Unlike [[capacity-hostile-environments]], capacity-conducive environments:
- Provide broad affordance fields rather than narrow constraints
- Encourage skill development rather than habit formation
- Foster shared valuing of capacities
- Support willpower exercise necessary for capacity development

## Connection to Deskilling
This concept provides the positive counterpart to [[capacity-hostile-environments]], showing how environments can be designed to counter the [[ai-deskilling-structural-problem]]. It operationalizes solutions to the [[human-operating-model-gap]] by specifying environmental conditions for capacity cultivation.
