# [[capacity-conducive-environments]]

## Definition

Capacity-conducive environments are socio-technical contexts that actively encourage the development and exercise of human capacities through rich opportunities for embodied interaction and shared valuing. As established in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]], these environments afford structured and unstructured embodied interactions necessary for intersubjective habituation of capacities, providing alternatives to capacity-hostile conditions.

## Characteristics

Capacity-conducive environments exhibit three key characteristics:

1. **Rich affordance fields**: They provide diverse action possibilities that trigger multiple human capacities simultaneously, avoiding the "narrow field of affordances" that characterizes capacity-hostile environments

2. **Embodied interaction**: They facilitate physical presence and sensory experience critical for social and moral capacities, as Ferdman explains: "Being embodied is critical for becoming skilled insofar as the process of 'shared valuing' of the skill requires some degree of doing something with the mentor(s), creating a shared history and a participatory sense-making"

3. **Intersubjective habituation**: They support the gradual process of skill development through relationships with others who guide and share the value of the skill

## Examples in Market Research

- **Hybrid research approaches** that combine AI-powered data collection with human-led interpretation sessions
- **Mentorship programs** where senior researchers guide juniors through complex analysis, sharing not just techniques but the value of deep analytical thinking
- **Community spaces** like research labs or co-working environments that encourage spontaneous collaboration and knowledge sharing
- **Deliberate practice frameworks** that provide structured opportunities to develop capacities with feedback loops

## Benefits

Capacity-conducive environments foster:
- **Deep skilling**: Gradual development of capacities through habituation and practice
- **Shared valuing**: Understanding of why capacities matter, not just how to perform them
- **Agential control**: Development of the ability to adjust execution of activities as performance unfolds
- **Resilience**: Capacity to handle unexpected scenarios when AI systems fail or encounter novel situations

## Contrast with Capacity-Hostile Environments

Unlike [[capacity-hostile-environments]], which restrict opportunities for capacity development, capacity-conducive environments recognize that "learning to value a capacity is part of the process of its habituation" and design contexts that support this development.

## Implementation Strategies

To create capacity-conducive environments in market research:
- Design AI tools that preserve opportunities for **plan-forming** rather than encouraging **plan-following**
- Ensure AI adoption maintains sufficient routine tasks for **habituation process**
- Create spaces for embodied interaction that support **intersubjectivity in skilling**
- Develop evaluation metrics that value capacity development alongside efficiency gains

## Philosophical Foundation

The concept draws from Developmental Perfectionism, where "flourishing is understood as a complex, unified, and well-rounded life" (Kauppinen 2025). Capacity-conducive environments support this flourishing by nurturing the core human capacities that constitute a good life.
