# Capacity-Conducive Environments

**First Observed:** 2026-07-03  
**Last Updated:** 2026-09-25

## Definition

Environments that actively encourage the development and exercise of human capacities through rich opportunities for embodied interaction and shared valuing, providing alternatives to capacity-hostile conditions. These environments afford structured and unstructured embodied interactions necessary for intersubjective habituation of capacities, as detailed in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]].

## Characteristics

Ferdman (2026) identifies key characteristics of capacity-conducive environments:

- **Embodied interaction opportunities**: Spaces that facilitate physical presence and sensory experience essential for skill development
- **Intersubjective learning**: Environments that support mentorship relationships where both mechanical aspects and value of skills are transmitted
- **Agential control**: Settings that allow individuals to initiate activities rather than merely respond to environmental triggers
- **Shared valuing**: Contexts where the value of capacities is collectively understood and reinforced
- **Field of affordances**: Environments that provide sufficient action possibilities for meaningful capacity development

## Examples in Market Research

- Research labs designed for collaborative analysis with physical whiteboards and shared workspaces
- Mentorship programs that pair junior researchers with experienced professionals for qualitative analysis
- Workshops where researchers practice interviewing techniques with embodied role-playing
- Team debrief sessions that encourage reflection on research decisions and outcomes
- Physical co-working spaces that facilitate spontaneous knowledge sharing

## Implementation Strategies

Ferdman emphasizes that capacity-conducive environments must actively encourage rather than merely afford embodied interaction:

> "To ensure the activation of the capacities, the environment has to encourage the activity of φ-ing, otherwise the capacities might not be triggered. This is why the environment conducive to the capacities has to include public goods like community centers, quality public spaces, public libraries, etc. that encourage structured and unstructured embodied interactions."

Specific implementation strategies for market research:

1. **Design collaborative workspaces**: Create physical environments that encourage embodied interaction among researchers
2. **Preserve mentorship pathways**: Ensure AI tools don't replace opportunities for intersubjective learning
3. **Balance automation**: Automate drudgery while preserving opportunities for capacity development
4. **Build reflection practices**: Incorporate regular debriefs where researchers analyze their decision-making processes

## Threats from AI Adoption

Ferdman warns that AI adoption can undermine capacity-conducive environments:

> "The smartphone: it provides possibilities for interaction that are disconnected from other persons, such as swiping, clicking and scrolling. This in turn invites determinate, separate, and repeatable tasks. This fractures the user's affordance space into disconnected fields, ultimately leading to alienation, rather than offering opportunities for the development of new cognitive and bodily skills that are mutually informing and enriching."
