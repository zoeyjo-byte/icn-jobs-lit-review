# [[capacity-conducive-environments]]

## Definition
Environments that actively encourage the development and exercise of human capacities through rich opportunities for embodied interaction and shared valuing, providing alternatives to capacity-hostile conditions. These environments afford structured and unstructured embodied interactions necessary for intersubjective habituation of capacities, as detailed in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]].

## Key Characteristics
- Provide a broad field of affordances that invite valuable activities requiring human capacities
- Encourage (not merely afford) embodied interaction and shared experiences
- Support the development of **agential control** over capacities
- Facilitate **intersubjective habituation** through mentor-mentee relationships
- Cultivate **shared valuing** of capacities through social practices
- Afford opportunities for developing the **volitional capacity** to overcome difficulty

## Essential Components

### Embodied Interaction Spaces
Public goods like community centers, quality public spaces, and public libraries that encourage structured and unstructured embodied interactions (Sypnowich 2016). These settings create shared experiences necessary for shared valuing of social capacities.

### Intersubjective Learning Frameworks
Social practices that generate moral reasons for action through constitutive aims or telos (Manne 2013). As MacIntyre noted, learning occurs 'through the practices of the common life' with teachers who 'know how to communicate responsiveness to others through the habits of a common life' (MacIntyre 2017).

### Agential Control Opportunities
Environments that require users to initiate activities rather than merely respond to environmental triggers, preserving opportunities for plan-forming rather than plan-following.

## Market Research Applications
- Research designs incorporating embodied respondent interaction rather than fully automated surveys
- Training programs with mentorship components emphasizing shared valuing of research capacities
- Workspaces designed to encourage embodied collaboration among researchers
- AI implementation strategies that preserve opportunities for critical thinking and creative problem-solving

## Creating Capacity-Conducive Environments
Organizations can foster these environments by:
- Designing AI tools that augment rather than replace human capacities
- Preserving opportunities for embodied interaction with research subjects
- Creating mentorship structures that emphasize shared valuing of research capacities
- Implementing feedback systems that encourage agential control over research processes
- Ensuring AI tools don't narrow the field of affordances for capacity development
