# Capacity-Conducive Environments

First observed: 2026-07-03
Last updated: 2026-07-21

## Definition

Capacity-conducive environments are settings that actively encourage the development and exercise of human capacities through rich opportunities for embodied interaction and shared valuing. As detailed in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]], these environments afford structured and unstructured embodied interactions necessary for intersubjective habituation of capacities, providing alternatives to [[capacity-hostile-environments]].

## Characteristics

According to Ferdman's structural analysis, capacity-conducive environments exhibit several key characteristics:

1. **Rich field of affordances**: They provide diverse action possibilities that trigger multiple human capacities simultaneously, avoiding the "affordance shrinkage" seen in capacity-hostile environments.

2. **Encouragement of embodied interaction**: These environments facilitate physical presence and sensory experience, which are critical for developing social and moral capacities. As Ferdman states, "embodied engagement... would be more conducive to creating shared experiences, which are necessary for shared valuing of the social capacities."

3. **Support for intersubjective learning**: They create opportunities for learning from mentors who can instill both the mechanical aspects of skills and the shared understanding of their value.

4. **Promotion of plan-forming over plan-following**: They encourage users to exercise [[agential-control]] to initiate activities rather than merely responding to environmental triggers.

## Examples in Market Research

Several practices constitute capacity-conducive environments:

- **Hybrid research methods**: Combining digital tools with face-to-face interaction preserves opportunities for embodied understanding while leveraging AI efficiency.

- **Mentorship programs**: Structured relationships where experienced researchers guide novices through the habituation process of research skills.

- **Community spaces**: Physical environments like research labs or co-working spaces that encourage spontaneous interaction and shared valuing of research capacities.

- **Deliberate skill-building exercises**: Activities designed to develop specific capacities through graduated challenges that require exercising [[volitional-capacity]].

## Contrast with Capacity-Hostile Environments

Capacity-conducive environments should be contrasted with [[capacity-hostile-environments]], which restrict development through narrow fields of affordances. Crucially, capacity-conducive environments don't merely afford but actively encourage valuable activities: "merely having the opportunity to φ does not guarantee that the agent will actually φ and engage the capacities involved in φ-ing."

## Implications for Skills Gap Research

Understanding capacity-conducive environments is critical for addressing the skills gap:

- Provides a framework for designing AI implementation strategies that preserve capacity development
- Highlights the importance of maintaining embodied interaction in research practices
- Suggests organizational solutions to the [[human-operating-model-gap]]
- Offers alternatives to the [[entry-level-squeeze]] by creating environments where junior researchers can develop capacities
