# [[capacity-conducive-environments]]

## Definition
Capacity-conducive environments are environments that actively encourage the development and exercise of human capacities through rich opportunities for embodied interaction and shared valuing. As established in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]], these environments provide structured and unstructured embodied interactions necessary for intersubjective habituation of capacities, serving as alternatives to capacity-hostile conditions.

## Characteristics
According to Ferdman's analysis in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]], capacity-conducive environments have several key characteristics:

1. **Rich Affordance Space**: They provide abundant action possibilities that trigger and support the development of multiple human capacities simultaneously.

2. **Embodied Interaction**: They encourage physical presence and sensory experience in skill development, particularly critical for social and moral capacities.

3. **Intersubjective Learning**: They facilitate mentor-mentee relationships and shared understanding about both the mechanical aspects of skills and their intrinsic value.

4. **Agential Control Development**: They afford opportunities for users to initiate activities and adjust their execution, rather than merely responding to environmental triggers.

5. **Volitional Capacity Support**: They encourage the development of willpower by presenting appropriately challenging activities that require effort and perseverance.

## Examples
1. **Community Centers**: Provide spaces for structured and unstructured embodied interactions that foster shared experiences necessary for social capacity development.

2. **Public Libraries**: Afford opportunities for reading, research, and community engagement that trigger epistemic and social capacities.

3. **Quality Public Spaces**: Encourage spontaneous social interactions and civic engagement that develop moral and relational capacities.

4. **Apprenticeship Models**: Create environments where novices learn from mentors through embodied practice, shared valuing, and gradual habituation of skills.

5. **Collaborative Research Environments**: In market research settings, teams that engage in co-creation with clients and participants, rather than relying solely on AI tools, create capacity-conducive environments for developing critical thinking and creativity.

## Contrast with Capacity-Hostile Environments
Unlike [[capacity-hostile-environments]], which restrict opportunities for capacity development through narrow fields of affordances, capacity-conducive environments actively encourage the cultivation of human capacities. Crucially, they don't merely provide opportunities for capacity exercise but actively encourage such exercise, recognizing that "merely having the opportunity to φ does not guarantee that the agent will actually φ and engage the capacities involved in φ-ing."

## Implementation in Organizations
Ferdman suggests that organizations can foster capacity-conducive environments by:
- Creating public goods like community centers and quality public spaces
- Designing workspaces that encourage embodied interaction
- Implementing mentorship programs that emphasize both skill mechanics and value
- Avoiding indiscriminate automation of routine tasks that deprive users of habituation opportunities
- Evaluating AI applications based on their conduciveness to capacity cultivation

## Implications for Market Research
In the market research industry, capacity-conducive environments would:
- Encourage researchers to engage directly with research participants
- Support embodied interactions that develop empathy and social understanding
- Create opportunities for mentorship in research design and interpretation
- Balance AI tool usage with human judgment development
- Foster environments where researchers can develop practical wisdom through real-world experience

Organizations should evaluate their AI adoption strategies based on whether they create capacity-conducive or capacity-hostile environments for their researchers, as outlined in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]].
