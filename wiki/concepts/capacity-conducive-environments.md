# Capacity-Conducive Environments

## Definition
Environments that actively encourage the development and exercise of human capacities through rich opportunities for embodied interaction and shared valuing, providing alternatives to capacity-hostile conditions. These environments afford structured and unstructured embodied interactions necessary for intersubjective habituation of capacities, as detailed in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]].

## Characteristics

### Rich Affordance Space
Capacity-conducive environments provide diverse action possibilities that trigger multiple human capacities simultaneously. As Ferdman explains, these environments:
- "Afford the opportunity for reading books, thereby triggering the epistemic and social capacities involved in reading"
- Encourage rather than merely afford valuable activities
- Provide sufficient invitations for activities that develop capacities

### Support for Agential Control
These environments preserve opportunities for individuals to initiate activities and adjust their execution. Ferdman emphasizes that "to cultivate a skill is to develop flexible links between thought and action, such that continue to evolve even after expertise has been achieved."

### Embodied Interaction Opportunities
Capacity-conducive environments facilitate physical presence and sensory experience essential for social capacity development. As Ferdman notes, "being embodied is critical for becoming skilled insofar as the process of 'shared valuing' of the skill requires some degree of doing something with the mentor(s)."

## AI Integration Principles

### Strategic Automation
Automation should target drudgery and repetition while preserving opportunities for meaningful decision-making. As Ferdman acknowledges, "not all automation constitutes a capacity-hostile environment. Some automation may free agents from drudgery and repetition."

### Human-AI Collaboration Design
AI tools should be designed to augment rather than replace human capacities. The ideal configuration maintains the user's "agential control over initiating the activity" rather than creating habitual responses to AI outputs.

### Intersubjective Valuing Preservation
AI systems should support rather than replace the mentor-mentee relationship essential for capacity development. As Ferdman explains, "a good mentor is typically necessary in order to cultivate the proper motivational structure for becoming skilled."

## Market Research Applications

### Research Workflow Design
Capacity-conducive research environments:
- Preserve opportunities for researchers to formulate research questions rather than merely execute AI-suggested approaches
- Maintain spaces for embodied interaction during complex analysis sessions
- Create structured opportunities for researchers to articulate their reasoning before and after AI assistance

### Training Programs
Effective capacity-conducive training:
- Uses AI tools as supplements rather than replacements for foundational skill development
- Incorporates deliberate practice with feedback on judgment calls
- Maintains mentorship relationships where "both mentor and mentee have shared intentions and they come to have 'shared valuing' of the skill"

### Tool Evaluation Framework
Research organizations should evaluate AI tools based on:
- Whether they preserve opportunities for agential control
- How they support embodied interaction
- Whether they encourage rather than discourage capacity development

## Implementation Strategies

### Public Goods Integration
Ferdman suggests environments should include "public goods like community centers, quality public spaces, public libraries" that encourage structured and unstructured embodied interactions.

### Volitional Capacity Support
Since the volitional capacity is a "meta-capacity" necessary for developing all other capacities, capacity-conducive environments should provide appropriate challenges that require effort without overwhelming users.

### Affordance Expansion
Rather than merely avoiding capacity-hostile conditions, organizations should actively design for affordance expansion - creating new opportunities for capacity development that didn't previously exist.
