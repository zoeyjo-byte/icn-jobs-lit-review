# [[capacity-conducive-environments]]

## Definition
Capacity-conducive environments are environments that "actively encourage the development and exercise of human capacities through rich opportunities for embodied interaction and shared valuing," as defined in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]]. These environments afford structured and unstructured embodied interactions necessary for intersubjective habituation of capacities.

## Characteristics
According to Ferdman's analysis, capacity-conducive environments typically:
- Provide rich opportunities for embodied interaction
- Encourage the development of [[agential-control]] over capacities
- Support the [[habituation-process]] through meaningful practice
- Foster [[intersubjectivity-in-skilling]] through mentorship and shared valuing
- Create conditions where users can spontaneously recognize others as moral agents
- Afford sufficient action possibilities for developing and exercising [[human-capacities]]

## Examples in Practice
Ferdman identifies several features of capacity-conducive environments:

1. **Public Goods Infrastructure**: "Community centers, quality public spaces, public libraries, etc. that encourage structured and unstructured embodied interactions" (Sypnowich 2016).

2. **Embodied Interaction Spaces**: Environments that facilitate "the return of the gaze of the other" which is "inherent in the embodied interaction" and critical for recognizing others as moral agents.

3. **Intersubjective Learning Contexts**: Settings where "learning to value a capacity is part of the process of its habituation," allowing for the development of shared understanding regarding both the outputs and the capacities themselves.

4. **Volitional Encouragement**: Environments that encourage the [[volitional-capacity]] by providing challenges that require effort and perseverance, rather than expecting individuals to be "virtuous superheroes."

## Structural Design Principles
Ferdman argues that capacity-conducive environments should be designed with these principles:
- **Embodiment Priority**: Prioritizing physical presence and sensory experience in interactions
- **Intersubjective Valuing**: Creating spaces for shared understanding of capacity value
- **Agential Encouragement**: Designing environments that encourage rather than merely afford action
- **Affordance Richness**: Providing diverse action possibilities that trigger multiple capacities simultaneously

## Implications for Market Research
For the market research industry, capacity-conducive environments could include:
- Research settings that combine AI tools with human interaction to maintain capacity development
- Training programs that preserve embodied learning experiences alongside digital tools
- Workspaces designed to encourage meaningful collaboration rather than isolated tool usage
- Evaluation frameworks that measure not just output efficiency but also capacity cultivation

## Contrast with Capacity-Hostile Environments
While [[capacity-hostile-environments]] restrict opportunities for capacity development, capacity-conducive environments actively foster them. The key difference lies in whether the environment merely provides opportunities or actively encourages engagement with those opportunities.
