# Capacity-Conducive Environments

Capacity-conducive environments are socio-technical contexts that actively encourage the development and exercise of human capacities through rich opportunities for embodied interaction and shared valuing. These environments provide the necessary conditions for [[habituation-process]] and the cultivation of [[agential-control]], as detailed in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]].

## Characteristics
- **Broad Field of Affordances:** Offers diverse action possibilities that trigger the competent exercise of epistemic, social, creative, and volitional capacities.
- **Embodied & Intersubjective Engagement:** Prioritizes physical presence and sensory experience, which are critical for recognizing others as moral agents, forming meaningful relationships, and developing shared valuing of skills.
- **Encouragement of Volitional Capacity:** Actively supports the capacity to will by encouraging agents to develop and exercise other capacities, recognizing that capacity development is difficult and requires effort.
- **Mentorship & Social Practices:** Provides structured and unstructured opportunities for learning from exemplars, instilling both the 'know-how' and the intrinsic value of skills.

## Examples in AI Contexts
- **Collaborative AI Workflows:** Tools designed to augment human judgment rather than replace it, requiring users to deliberate, settle, and coordinate plans (plan-forming).
- **Public Libraries & Community Centers:** Physical spaces that foster shared experiences and unstructured interactions, essential for the intersubjective habituation of social and moral capacities.
- **AI Evaluation Frameworks:** Systems that assess AI applications based on their conduciveness to capacity cultivation, prioritizing tools that preserve agential control and encourage real-world experience.

## Implementation in Market Research
For insights organizations, fostering capacity-conducive environments means designing research workflows that preserve researcher autonomy, encourage deep engagement with data, and maintain human-to-human collaboration. AI should be positioned as a tool that frees time for higher-order strategic thinking and creative synthesis, rather than a replacement for foundational analytical and empathetic skills.
