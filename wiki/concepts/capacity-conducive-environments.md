## Update 2026-07-25

Expanded definition based on Ferdman's analysis in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]]. Capacity-conducive environments actively encourage the development and exercise of human capacities through rich opportunities for embodied interaction and shared valuing. These environments afford structured and unstructured embodied interactions necessary for intersubjective habituation of capacities.

Key characteristics:
- Provide opportunities for embodied interaction essential for social and moral skill development
- Encourage agents to exercise agential control over initiating activities
- Support intersubjective learning through mentorship and shared valuing of capacities
- Create space for the gradual habituation process involving encountering and overcoming challenges
- Balance automation with opportunities for plan-forming activities

In market research contexts, capacity-conducive environments include:
- Team-based research design sessions that blend AI tools with human creativity
- Client workshops that prioritize face-to-face interaction for complex problem-solving
- Mentorship programs that guide junior researchers through the habituation process
- Research processes that maintain appropriate challenges requiring human judgment

See [[embodiment-in-skilling]], [[intersubjectivity-in-skilling]], and [[agential-control]] for related concepts.
