# Plan-Following

Plan-following refers to the execution of pre-determined plans without exercising agential control over the planning process itself. As analyzed in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]], this represents a habitual mode of action where individuals respond to environmental triggers rather than initiating and adjusting plans through self-deliberation.

## Characteristics

According to Ferdman's analysis, plan-following exhibits these key characteristics:

- **Environmental triggers**: Initiated by something in the environment (e.g., an AI recommendation)
- **Limited control**: Responding to pre-determined plans rather than initiating them
- **Reduced responsibility**: Ability to shift responsibility to the plan creator when things go wrong
- **Less adaptability**: Following a predetermined path with less flexibility for novel situations
- **Shallow skilling**: Requires a shallower exercise of human capacities

## Contrast with Plan-Forming

Ferdman makes a critical distinction between plan-following and plan-forming:

| **Characteristic** | **Plan-Following** | **Plan-Forming** |
|------------------|-------------------|------------------|
| **Initiation** | Triggered by environment | Initiated through self-deliberation |
| **Control** | Limited over execution | Full agential control |
| **Responsibility** | Can shift responsibility | Takes ownership of outcomes |
| **Adaptability** | Less flexible | Adapts to novel situations |
| **Capacity development** | Shallow skilling | Develops multiple capacities |

## AI's Role in Encouraging Plan-Following

According to Ferdman, AI systems like Artificial Personal Assistants (APAs) actively encourage plan-following:

- **Constant availability**: "Always-on" presence that provides immediate recommendations
- **Personalization**: Tailoring advice based on user's past behavior, making recommendations feel personally relevant
- **Settling function**: Making decisions between options for the user
- **Coordination function**: Organizing different types of plans and policies for the user
- **Convenience**: Reducing the cognitive effort required for planning

## Market Research Implications

In market research contexts, plan-following can manifest as:

- Researchers following AI-generated research templates rather than developing original approaches
- Clients accepting AI-recommended solutions without critical evaluation
- Teams implementing predetermined analysis frameworks rather than adapting to data insights
- Decision-makers following algorithmic recommendations without considering contextual factors

## Mitigation Strategies

To counter plan-following tendencies in AI-augmented research:

1. **Design for initiation**: Create workflows that require researchers to articulate their own plans before consulting AI
2. **Build reflection points**: Incorporate opportunities to evaluate and adjust plans during execution
3. **Preserve error exposure**: Ensure researchers encounter and learn from planning mistakes
4. **Foster practical wisdom**: Develop researchers' capacity for situational judgment beyond algorithmic recommendations
