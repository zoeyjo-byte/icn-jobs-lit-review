# [[plan-following]]

## 2026-08-26 Update

Plan-following refers to the execution of pre-determined plans without exercising agential control over the planning process itself. As analyzed in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]], this represents a habitual mode of action where individuals respond to environmental triggers rather than initiating and adjusting plans through self-deliberation.

### Characteristics
- Execution of pre-determined plans without initiating the planning process
- Response to triggers in the environment rather than self-initiated action
- Limited opportunity for adjustment during execution
- Reduced responsibility for plan outcomes
- Requires shallower exercise of human capacities
- More habitual than skill-based in nature

### Contrast with Plan-Forming
| **Plan-Following** | **Plan-Forming** |
|--------------------|-----------------|
| Execution of pre-determined plans | Creation of plans through self-deliberation |
| Response to environmental triggers | Initiation of planning activity |
| Limited adjustment during execution | Flexible adjustment as performance unfolds |
| Reduced responsibility for outcomes | Full responsibility for plan outcomes |
| Shallow exercise of capacities | Full exercise of epistemic, social, creative capacities |
| Habitual mode of action | Skill-based mode of action |

### AI's Role in Encouraging Plan-Following
Ferdman identifies how AI systems promote plan-following:
- **Constant availability**: APAs that are "always-on sounding boards" discouraging independent planning
- **Prescriptive recommendations**: Outputs that users follow without self-deliberation
- **Automated planning**: Systems that generate complete plans without user initiation
- **Responsibility displacement**: Ability to shift accountability for failures to the AI system
- **Convenience factor**: APA's ease of use that limits motivation to develop independent planning skills

### Market Research Examples
- Researchers following AI-generated research methodologies without self-deliberation on research goals
- Junior analysts executing survey analysis based on AI templates without developing their own interpretation frameworks
- Teams implementing AI-recommended reporting structures without considering alternative presentation approaches
- Project managers scheduling research activities based on AI recommendations without adjusting for unique project needs

### Capacity Deskilling Effects
Plan-following undermines capacity development by:
- Reducing opportunities to exercise practical rationality (phronesis)
- Limiting development of creative capacity through novel problem-solving
- Discouraging volitional capacity through reduced need for perseverance
- Undermining social capacity development through reduced shared valuing
- Creating dependency on external systems for decision-making

### Mitigation Strategies
- Design AI tools with initiation points requiring user self-deliberation
- Create reflection stages before accepting AI recommendations
- Implement training emphasizing the value of independent planning
- Develop organizational practices that reward plan-forming behavior
- Balance AI assistance with opportunities for self-initiated planning

### Connection to Capacity Development
Plan-following represents a key mechanism of capacity deskilling because it converts skill-based planning into habitual action. Without opportunities to initiate and adjust research plans, market researchers cannot develop the practical rationality that distinguishes expert researchers. Ferdman argues that preserving plan-forming opportunities requires structural solutions that prevent AI systems from completely taking over the planning process. Capacity-conducive research environments maintain spaces where researchers must initiate and adjust their own research plans, recognizing that the ability to form plans is essential for developing the full range of human capacities required for high-quality research.
