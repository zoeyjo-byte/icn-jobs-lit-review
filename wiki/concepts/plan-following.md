# Plan-Following

## Definition

Plan-following refers to the execution of pre-determined plans without exercising agential control over the planning process itself. As analyzed in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]], this represents a habitual mode of action where individuals respond to environmental triggers rather than initiating and adjusting plans through self-deliberation.

## Key Characteristics

- **Triggered by external prompts**: Actions are initiated in response to environmental cues rather than internal deliberation
- **Limited agential control**: Minimal ability to adjust execution as performance unfolds
- **Shallow skill development**: Does not cultivate the full range of human capacities required for competent planning
- **Reduced responsibility**: When plans fail, responsibility can be shifted to the planning tool rather than prompting self-critique and growth

## Relationship to AI Systems

Artificial Personal Assistants (APAs) often encourage plan-following behavior by:
- Providing ready-made schedules and action steps
- Prompting users with "what to do next" suggestions
- Reducing the need for small-scale decision-making that builds planning competence
- Creating dependency through constant availability and helpfulness

As noted in the Ferdman study, "When much of the planning process is offloaded to the APA, it arguably requires a shallower exercise of the capacities that are ordinarily involved in making coherent plans and executing them."

## Contrast with Plan-Forming

Plan-following differs fundamentally from [[plan-forming]], which involves:
- Exercising control over initiating planning activities
- Engaging in self-deliberation on values and goals
- Developing practical wisdom through trial and error
- Taking responsibility for planning outcomes
- Cultivating the full range of human capacities through the planning process

## Implications for Market Research

In market research contexts, plan-following tendencies encouraged by AI tools could:
- Reduce researchers' ability to adapt methodologies to novel research questions
- Diminish capacity for creative problem-solving when encountering unexpected data
- Undermine development of practical wisdom in research design
- Create dependency on AI-generated research plans rather than cultivating independent research judgment
