# Social Capacities

## Definition
The human capacities related to forming meaningful relationships, understanding others' perspectives, and participating in communal life, including moral capacity and relational capacity. As detailed in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]], these capacities are constitutive of human flourishing and can be undermined by AI systems.

## Two Core Components

### Moral Capacity
- Recognizing others as moral agents
- Making ethical judgments and understanding moral reasons for action
- Developing moral attention through embodied interaction
- Understanding the constitutive aim or telos of social practices

### Relational Capacity
- Forming meaningful relationships of friendship and love
- Understanding others' perspectives and building trust
- Participating in communal life and shared valuing of capacities
- Developing the ability to coordinate with others toward complex goals

## Role in Planning and Decision-Making
Social capacities are essential for effective planning:
- Planning involves coordinating with others toward achieving complex goals
- Social relationships provide the context for understanding reasons for action
- 'Our life goals (e.g., being a good parent, help combat climate change) are... drawn from our social relationships'
- Shared experiences create the foundation for developing practical wisdom

## AI's Threat to Social Capacities
Ferdman identifies how AI systems undermine social capacities:
- Creating disembodied environments that make it 'harder to form meaningful friendship because technologically mediated virtue friendships require more discipline for a diminished experience'
- Reducing opportunities for 'embodied understanding' of others as similar loci of subjectivity
- Undermining the spontaneity of recognizing others as moral agents
- Diminishing shared experiences necessary for shared valuing of capacities
- 'AI mediation might come at the expense of human interaction, decreasing the opportunity to develop our capacities in concert with other humans'

## Market Research Implications

### Client Relationships
- AI tools that replace face-to-face client interaction with chatbots
- Platforms that automate client reporting without opportunities for embodied relationship building
- Systems that reduce researchers' opportunities to develop trust with clients

### Team Collaboration
- Remote work tools that diminish embodied interaction among research teams
- AI systems that replace human mentorship with algorithmic guidance
- Workflows that reduce opportunities for shared reflection on research quality

## Preserving Social Capacities
Strategies to protect social capacities in AI-augmented research:
- Design research workflows that preserve opportunities for face-to-face client interaction
- Create hybrid research models that combine digital tools with embodied engagement
- Structure team collaboration to include regular in-person interaction
- Develop mentorship programs that explicitly cultivate shared valuing of research capacities
- Ensure AI tools enhance rather than replace the relational aspects of research

## Connection to Embodiment
Social capacities depend critically on **embodiment in skilling**:
- 'Without being embodied in a social interaction—without the return of the gaze of the other—we would be hard-pressed to recognize them spontaneously as moral agents'
- Embodied interaction enables 'participatory sense-making' where meaning is generated through the interplay between participants
- Physical presence creates opportunities for developing moral attention and relational skills

## Contrast with Technical Skills
Unlike technical skills that may become obsolete, social capacities are fundamental to human flourishing. Their impoverishment leads to diminished lives, explaining why capacity-deskilling should always be a cause for worry. In market research, preserving social capacities is essential for maintaining the human element that distinguishes high-quality insights from automated data analysis.
