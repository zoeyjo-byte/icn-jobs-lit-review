# Social Capacities

**First Observed**: 2026-07-18
**Last Updated**: 2026-07-19

## Definition

The human capacities related to forming meaningful relationships, understanding others' perspectives, and participating in communal life, including moral capacity and relational capacity. These capacities are constitutive of human flourishing and essential for the 'arts of personhood' (Vallor 2015).

## Philosophical Framework

Based on Ferdman's (2026) analysis in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]], social capacities encompass:

### Moral Capacity
- Recognizing others as moral agents
- Making ethical judgments in complex situations
- Understanding moral reasons for action
- Developing shared valuing of ethical principles

### Relational Capacity
- Building and maintaining meaningful friendships and professional relationships
- Understanding others' perspectives and needs
- Engaging in participatory sense-making
- Creating shared experiences and histories

As Ferdman explains: "The social capacities include the moral capacity as well as the social and caring capacities for developing meaningful relationships of friendship and love."

## Embodied Foundation

Social capacities fundamentally depend on embodied interaction:
- "Without being embodied in a social interaction... we would be hard-pressed to recognize them spontaneously as moral agents"
- "The spontaneity of recognizing the other as moral agent is inherent in the embodied interaction"
- Disembodied interactions require "more mental efforts in attributing moral agency to the other"

Ferdman notes that technologically mediated virtue friendships "require more discipline for a diminished experience" compared to embodied relationships.

## Role in Life-Planning

Social capacities are essential for life-planning because:
- Planning involves coordinating with others toward complex goals
- Our life goals (e.g., being a good parent, helping combat climate change) are drawn from social relationships
- Planning requires the moral and social capacities to navigate relationships and social contexts

## AI Threats to Social Capacities

AI systems threaten social capacities through:

1. **Reduced embodied interaction**: Digital interfaces that afford "swiping, clicking and scrolling" fracture social affordance spaces

2. **Undermining intersubjective habituation**: APAs decrease opportunities for "embodied, intersubjective interaction involved in life-planning activities"

3. **Replacing human mentorship**: "Preferring the advice of an APA over that of a friend or colleague may contribute to undermining the shared experience between persons"

4. **Diminished moral attention**: In healthcare, AI dependence has been found to lead to "declining ethical sensitivity and weakened moral judgment" (NHS England 2023)

## Market Research Implications

Social capacities are critical for market research work:

- **Qualitative research** requires deep understanding of participants' perspectives
- **Client relationships** depend on trust and shared understanding
- **Team collaboration** necessitates effective communication and coordination
- **Ethical research practices** demand strong moral capacity

The increasing use of AI in research threatens these capacities through:
- Reduced face-to-face client and participant interactions
- Algorithmic recommendations replacing human judgment in research design
- Automated analysis diminishing opportunities for team discussion and interpretation
- Narrowing of the field of affordances for developing social capacities

## Preservation Strategies

To maintain social capacities in AI-augmented research environments:
- Preserve space for embodied client and participant interactions
- Maintain team-based interpretation sessions alongside AI analysis
- Create structured opportunities for ethical deliberation in research design
- Design workflows that require researchers to explain findings to clients rather than relying on automated reports

As Ferdman (2026) emphasizes: "Embodied engagement... would be more conducive to creating shared experiences, which are necessary for shared valuing of the social capacities." This principle is equally applicable to research relationships and team dynamics.
