# Life-Planning

**Life-planning** refers to the cognitive and agential process of structuring one's future actions, goals, and values. In the context of AI-mediated environments, life-planning is increasingly being offloaded to **[[artificial-personal-assistants|APAs]]**, raising concerns about its impact on human capacity cultivation.

## Theoretical Perspectives
Scholars distinguish between two primary views of life-planning:

1. **Instrumentalist View**: Life-planning is treated as a mere tool to help an agent achieve pre-existing goals and aims. Under this view, outsourcing planning to an AI is seen as efficient, provided the goals remain the user's own.
2. **Non-Instrumentalist View**: Life-planning is a **self-constituting activity**. As argued by Heyd and Miller (2010), a person's identity is partly constituted by their life plans. Planning is not about maximizing an independently identifiable good, but about developing a particular character or disposition. It relates to the kind of person one decides to become, rather than just the aims one wants to maximize.

## Required Capacities
From a **[[capacity-skilling|capacity-as-skill]]** perspective, effective life-planning requires the integrated exercise of multiple core human capacities:
- **[[epistemic-capacities]]**: Exercising theoretical rationality for self-deliberation on values, and practical wisdom (**[[phronesis]])** to coordinate goals with actions and assess the testimony of others.
- **[[social-capacities]]**: Coordinating with others toward complex shared goals, and drawing moral/social reasons for action from relationships.
- **[[creative-capacity]]**: Responding to novel situations, surprises, and unexpected events, since plans are always incomplete.
- **[[volitional-capacity]]**: The meta-capacity required to initiate planning, resist the temptation to outsource it, and persevere through difficulty.

## Risks of AI Mediation
When APAs replace the user's planning activities, they transform the user from a **plan-former** into a **plan-follower**. This shift reduces **[[agential-control]]**, as the user responds to AI triggers rather than initiating settling and coordinating functions. Furthermore, relying on an APA for life-planning diminishes opportunities for **[[embodied-habituation]]** and shared valuing, potentially entrenching a **[[capacity-hostile-environment]]** that narrows the **[[affordance-shrinkage|field of affordances]]** for personal growth.
