# Human Capacities

## Definition
Core abilities that constitute human flourishing, including epistemic, social, creative, and volitional capacities, which are fundamental to being human and can be undermined by AI systems, as detailed in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]].

## Four Categories of Human Capacities

### 1. Epistemic Capacities
The abilities required for knowing and understanding:

- **Theoretical rationality**: Thinking, considering reasons to believe, forming beliefs, contemplating
- **Practical rationality (phronesis/wisdom)**: Doing the right thing in the right way for the right reason
  - Involves practical wisdom: knowing effective means to ends, accurate understanding of situations, discernment of how good and bad apply in circumstances
  - Essential for critically reflecting on conceptions of a flourishing life

### 2. Social Capacities
The abilities required for meaningful relationships:

- **Moral capacity**: The ability to make ethical judgments and act accordingly
- **Social and caring capacities**: For developing meaningful relationships of friendship and love
  - Requires embodied interaction for full development
  - Depends on intersubjective learning through relationships

### 3. Creative Capacity
The ability to innovate and transform:

- Making unfamiliar combinations of familiar ideas
- Transforming conceptual spaces without relying merely on luck, accidents, or mechanical procedures
- Essential for responding to novel situations and unexpected events in life planning

### 4. Volitional Capacity
The meta-capacity required for developing all other capacities:

- The ability to exert effort, overcome difficulty, persevere and grow
- Required to achieve intrinsically valuable goals
- Particularly important because developing any capacity is difficult
- Enables resistance to outsourcing capacity development to external tools

## Why Capacities Matter
Human capacities are constitutive of flourishing:

- They enable us to achieve competence in practical reason, self-worth, and mutual respect
- They are necessary for honesty, justice, and ethical sensitivity
- They allow us to engage in activities that yield intrinsically valuable outcomes
- As Richard Kraut notes, capacities rarely operate in isolation; for example, "the capacity to know involves theoretical rationality as well as practical rationality"

## Threats from AI Systems
AI systems can undermine human capacities through:

- **Automation of routine tasks**: Depriving users of opportunities to practice and strengthen capacities
- **Reduced embodied interaction**: Limiting opportunities for social and moral capacity development
- **Diminished intersubjectivity**: Replacing human mentorship with algorithmic guidance
- **Plan-following environments**: Shifting users from plan-forming (requiring capacity exercise) to plan-following (habitual response)

As Ferdman explains, when AI systems replace valuable human activity, they risk "deskilling humans of their human capacities" through systemic conditions rather than individual failings.

## Structural Perspective
The structural perspective reveals that capacity development depends on environmental conditions:

- We "develop and exercise our epistemic, moral, social, and creative capacities in response to our social and material environment"
- A deficient environment can lead to deskilling of core human capacities
- When capacities weaken, they may be devalued, creating a vicious cycle

## Contrast with Domain-Specific Skills
Unlike domain-specific skills (hunting, basket-weaving, coding), where deskilling may be beneficial (freeing time for other pursuits), capacity-deskilling is always problematic:

> "The deskilling of our core human capacities—to know, create, form meaningful relationships, and use our willpower—is a different matter. In this sense, capacities are more analogous to virtues. The deskilling of our core human capacities is bad because inadequate development of these capacities can lead to 'capacity impoverishment', which, in turn, diminishes us as humans and undermines our flourishing."

## Implications for Market Research
In AI-augmented market research, preserving human capacities is critical for:

- Maintaining researchers' practical wisdom in interpreting complex human behaviors
- Ensuring ethical research practices through developed moral capacities
- Supporting creative approaches to research design and analysis
- Preserving the volitional capacity needed to overcome research challenges
