# Human Capacities

## Definition
Human capacities are the core abilities that constitute human flourishing, including epistemic, social, creative, and volitional capacities. Unlike skills that may become obsolete, these capacities are fundamental to being human.

## Characteristics
- Constitutive of human flourishing rather than merely instrumental
- Develop through habituation involving agential control
- Require intersubjective learning and embodied experience
- Can be impoverished through capacity-hostile environments
- Are intrinsically valuable as manifestations of flourishing

## From Ferdman (2026)
In "AI Deskilling is a Structural Problem," Ferdman identifies four key human capacities:

1. **Epistemic capacities**:
   - Theoretical rationality: thinking, considering reasons to believe, forming beliefs, contemplating
   - Practical rationality: manifesting in phronesis (common sense or wisdom)

2. **Social capacities**:
   - Moral capacity
   - Social and caring capacities for developing meaningful relationships

3. **Creative capacity**: Making unfamiliar combinations of familiar ideas, transforming conceptual spaces

4. **Volitional capacity**: The ability to exert effort, overcome difficulty, persevere and grow to achieve intrinsically valuable goals

Ferdman emphasizes that "developing and exercising any of the human capacities is difficult, which therefore requires the competent exercise of the capacity to will. I will therefore treat the capacity to will as a meta-capacity of sorts."

## Distinction from Skills
Ferdman distinguishes between skill and capacity:
- Skills may become obsolete through technological development (hunting, basket-weaving)
- Capacity-deskilling is always problematic because "inadequate development of these capacities can lead to 'capacity impoverishment', which, in turn, diminishes us as humans and undermines our flourishing."

## Implications for Market Research
- Research practices should support the development of researchers' full capacities
- AI tools should be evaluated based on their impact on researchers' capacities
- Organizations should create environments conducive to capacity development
- Training should focus on developing capacities rather than just technical skills
- The industry should recognize that capacity-deskilling leads to diminished professional lives
