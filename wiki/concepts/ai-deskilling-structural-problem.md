# AI Deskilling as a Structural Problem

AI deskilling is understood not merely as an individual failing to resist automation, but as a systemic, structural phenomenon where AI socio-technical environments actively undermine the cultivation of human capacities. As detailed in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]], this perspective shifts the focus from individual responsibility to the design and affordances of the environments in which agents operate.

## Core Thesis
Traditional virtue ethics frames deskilling as a personal vice: the individual has a duty to cultivate the 'arts of personhood' and fails if they do not. However, this overlooks how our social and material environments shape capacity development. A deficient environment can systematically erode core human capacities, leading to 'capacity impoverishment' and diminished flourishing. The structural lens argues that AI's influence creates systemic conditions that inhibit the long, gradual process of capacity cultivation by undermining habituation and agential control.

## Mechanisms of Structural Deskilling
1. **Automation Bias & Offloading:** Indiscriminate automation of routine tasks deprives users of the practice necessary to strengthen cognitive musculature, leaving them unprepared for exceptions and atrophying judgment.
2. **Narrow Field of Affordances:** AI tools often restrict action possibilities to shallow skilling, discouraging users from seeking alternative avenues to exercise deeper capacities.
3. **Erosion of Intersubjectivity & Embodiment:** AI mediation replaces embodied, intersubjective interactions with disembodied, algorithmic ones. This makes it harder to recognize others as moral agents, form meaningful relationships, and develop shared valuing of skills.
4. **Plan-Following over Plan-Forming:** APAs and automated systems invite users to respond to environmental triggers (tool outputs) rather than initiating self-deliberation, shifting agents from plan-formers to plan-followers.

## Implications for Organizational Design
Recognizing deskilling as a structural problem necessitates societal and organizational solutions. Organizations must evaluate AI implementations based on whether they create capacity-hostile or capacity-conducive environments. This involves designing workflows that preserve [[agential-control]], encourage [[habituation-process]] through mentorship and embodied collaboration, and maintain a broad field of affordances for professional growth.
