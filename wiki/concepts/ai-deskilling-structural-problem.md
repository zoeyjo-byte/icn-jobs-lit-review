## Update (2026-08-20)

Expanded with detailed analysis from [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]]:

The AI deskilling structural problem refers to how AI systems undermine human capacities through systemic conditions that create environments hostile to capacity cultivation. This should be understood as a structural problem requiring societal solutions, not merely an individual responsibility issue.

Key mechanisms:
- Creation of narrow fields of affordances that restrict capacity development opportunities
- Promotion of plan-following rather than plan-forming activities
- Reduction of embodied, intersubjective habituation necessary for capacity development
- Systematic affordance shrinkage across multiple human spheres

Examples:
- Healthcare AI that diminishes diagnostic reasoning and clinical judgment
- Writing tools that erode critical thinking and analytical reasoning
- Personal assistants that replace life-planning activities

Societal implications:
- Deskilling diminishes us as human beings, undermining our flourishing
- Capacity impoverishment leads to impoverished lives
- We cannot expect agents to be 'virtuous superheroes' who resist deskilling

Market research relevance:
- Research firms must evaluate whether their AI tools create capacity-hostile environments
- The structural approach shifts responsibility from individual researchers to organizational design
- Research methodologies should be designed to preserve capacity development opportunities
- Firms should create capacity-conducive environments that encourage embodied, intersubjective habituation
