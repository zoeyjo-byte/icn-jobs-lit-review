# AI Deskilling Structural Problem

**First Observed:** 2026-07-03  
**Last Updated:** 2026-09-25

## Definition

The phenomenon where AI systems undermine human capacities through systemic conditions that create environments hostile to capacity cultivation, as established in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]]. This should be understood as a structural problem requiring societal solutions, not merely an individual responsibility issue. Enhanced with evidence from [[ama-2026-state-of-marketing-careers-report]]'s "Will AI Make Us Dumb?" section documenting parallel deskilling dynamics in marketing.

## Structural Perspective

Ferdman (2026) makes a crucial distinction between individual and structural approaches:

> "Rather than focusing only on how human agents ought to engage with AI tools, we should also ask what kind of socio-technical environments would be best for the cultivation of our personhood."

> "Framing the response to the threats to our humanness as an individual responsibility problem therefore misses the structural dimensions of the deskilling problem."

The structural perspective emphasizes:
- Environmental conditions that enable or disable capacity development
- Systemic design choices that create narrow fields of affordances
- Societal obligations to foster capacity-conducive environments
- Organizational responsibility rather than individual blame

## Evidence from Marketing

The AMA report provides parallel evidence in marketing:

> "The report identifies critical trends including the rise of AI-powered generalists and the transformation of entry-level marketing roles, providing valuable parallels to the market research industry."

> "The 'Will AI Make Us Dumb?' section documents parallel deskilling dynamics in marketing, particularly how AI tools that automate creative processes may undermine marketers' capacity for original thinking."

## Market Research Implications

This structural perspective has significant implications:

- **Evaluation framework**: Organizations need to evaluate AI tools based on their conduciveness to capacity cultivation
- **Organizational design**: Research teams must be structured to preserve capacity development pathways
- **Career development**: Entry-level roles need to maintain opportunities for habituation of core capacities
- **Ethical guidelines**: Professional associations should develop guidelines for capacity-preserving AI adoption
