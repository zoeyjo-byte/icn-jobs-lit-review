# AI Deskilling as a Structural Problem

## Definition
The AI deskilling structural problem refers to the phenomenon where AI systems undermine human capacities through systemic conditions that create environments hostile to capacity cultivation, rather than through individual failings. It shifts analysis from individual responsibility to structural conditions that inhibit capacity development.

## Key Arguments (Ferdman 2026)

### Beyond Individual Responsibility
- Framing deskilling as individual responsibility overlooks structural aspects
- Expecting persons to be 'virtuous superheroes' who resist deskilling is unrealistic
- Capacity development depends on social/material environment
- Deficient environments lead to capacity deskilling and subsequent devaluation

### Capacity Impoverishment
- Deskilling of core human capacities diminishes us as humans
- Inadequate capacity development undermines flourishing
- Capacity impoverishment leads to impoverished lives
- Unlike skill obsolescence (e.g., hunting, basket-weaving), capacity deskilling is always problematic

### Structural Mechanisms
- **Capacity-hostile environments**: Restrict, limit, or narrow affordance fields
- **Affordance shrinkage**: Systematic reduction in action possibilities
- **Value erosion cycle**: Shallow capacity development → inability to value excellence → less motivation to develop capacities

## Market Research Implications

### Critical Capacity Areas at Risk
- **Epistemic capacities**: Critical thinking, analytical reasoning, practical wisdom
- **Social capacities**: Rapport-building, interpretation of human behavior
- **Creative capacity**: Research design innovation, novel problem-solving
- **Volitional capacity**: Willpower to develop other capacities

### Structural Deskilling Patterns
- **Entry-level squeeze**: AI automation removes foundational tasks that served as training grounds
- **Human operating model gap**: Slower adaptation of human systems compared to AI advancement
- **Value chain shift**: Repositioning of where value is created in research organizations

### Mitigation Framework
1. Evaluate AI tools based on capacity-conduciveness
2. Preserve embodied interaction opportunities
3. Maintain intersubjective learning structures
4. Design workflows that support agential control

## Contrast with Alternative Views

### Individual Responsibility Approach
- Focuses on personal willpower to resist deskilling
- Ignores environmental constraints on capacity development
- Blames users for capacity erosion rather than examining system design

### Structural Approach
- Analyzes how environments shape capacity development
- Identifies design features that create capacity-hostile conditions
- Focuses on societal obligation to foster capacity-conducive environments
