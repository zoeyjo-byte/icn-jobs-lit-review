# Developmental Perfectionism

Neo-Aristotelian approach to human flourishing where flourishing is understood as a complex, unified, and well-rounded life, providing the philosophical foundation for understanding why capacity-deskilling is harmful. As established in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]], this framework explains why impoverished capacities lead to impoverished lives.

## Definition and Characteristics

Developmental Perfectionism is characterized by:

- Viewing human flourishing as a complex, unified, and well-rounded life
- Recognizing that humans exercise capacities in activities yielding intrinsically valuable outcomes
- Understanding that competent exercise of capacities manifests achievement of intrinsically valuable goods
- Treating capacities as intrinsically valuable because they constitute flourishing

As the paper explains: "Humans exercise their capacities in activities that yield outcomes that have intrinsic value like knowledge, beauty, morality, friendship... The competent exercise of the human capacities is the manifestation of the achievement of the intrinsically valuable goods, making the capacities intrinsically valuable too."

## Core Principles

### 1. Capacities as Constitutive of Flourishing

- Human capacities are not merely instrumental but constitutive of flourishing
- Impoverished capacities (capacity-deskilling) lead directly to impoverished lives
- The value of activities comes from competent exercise of capacities, not merely outcomes

### 2. Intrinsic Value of Capacity Exercise

- The value of being on the summit of Mt. Everest comes from the competent exercise of climbing capacities, not merely from being there
- As Hurka and Bradford argue, "the value of being on the summit of Mt. Everest is gained from the competent exercise of a combination of capacities involved in climbing it, and not from simply being there (say, if one arrives there by helicopter)"

### 3. Developmental Perspective

- Capacity development is a gradual process requiring habituation
- Flourishing involves the competent exercise of capacities developed over time
- Deskilling represents a failure in the developmental process toward flourishing

## Why It Matters for AI Ethics

This framework is crucial for understanding AI's impact because:

1. **It explains why deskilling is harmful**: Capacity-deskilling diminishes us as humans by undermining our flourishing

2. **It distinguishes capacity deskilling from skill obsolescence**: While domain-specific skill obsolescence may be innocuous, capacity-deskilling is always problematic

3. **It provides normative foundation**: Gives us reason to ensure socio-technical environments are conducive to capacity cultivation

4. **It shifts focus from individual to structural**: Highlights that environments deficient for capacity development lead to deskilling, regardless of individual will

## Contrast with Instrumentalist Views

Unlike instrumentalist views that treat capacities as mere tools for achieving goals, Developmental Perfectionism recognizes that:

- The competent exercise of capacities has intrinsic value
- Capacity development is an end in itself, not merely a means
- Flourishing consists in the exercise of capacities, not just their outcomes

This explains why life-planning should not be outsourced to tools: "planning one's life is about developing a particular character or dispositions, rather than to the aims or goals one wants to maximize. As such, planning one's life is about... rather than following an overall program of predetermined activities."

## Implications for AI Design

The Developmental Perfectionism framework implies that AI systems should:

- Support rather than undermine the competent exercise of human capacities
- Create environments conducive to capacity development
- Recognize that some capacities cannot be outsourced without diminishing flourishing
- Prioritize designs that encourage rather than replace capacity exercise
