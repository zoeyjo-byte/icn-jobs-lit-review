# Developmental Perfectionism

First observed: 2026-07-15
Last updated: 2026-07-21

## Definition

Developmental Perfectionism is a neo-Aristotelian approach to human flourishing where flourishing is understood as a complex, unified, and well-rounded life, providing the philosophical foundation for understanding why capacity-deskilling is harmful. As established in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]], this framework explains why impoverished capacities lead to impoverished lives.

## Characteristics

According to Ferdman's analysis, Developmental Perfectionism has several critical features:

1. **Flourishing as unified life**: "Flourishing is understood as a complex, unified, and well-rounded life" rather than a collection of isolated achievements.

2. **Capacities as constitutive**: Human capacities are not merely instrumental but constitutive of flourishing: "The competent exercise of the human capacities is the manifestation of the achievement of the intrinsically valuable goods, making the capacities intrinsically valuable too."

3. **Value of competent exercise**: The value of achievements comes from the competent exercise of capacities involved, not merely from the outcome: "the value of being on the summit of Mt. Everest is gained from the competent exercise of a combination of capacities involved in climbing it, and not from simply being there."

4. **Capacity-deskilling as harmful**: Impoverished capacities (capacity-deskilling) lead to impoverished lives, making capacity cultivation normatively important.

## Examples in Market Research

Several industry practices relate to Developmental Perfectionism:

- **Research quality assessment**: Evaluating research based on the competent exercise of capacities (methodological rigor, ethical consideration) rather than just the final deliverable.

- **Career development**: Structuring career paths to provide opportunities for developing the full range of research capacities rather than narrow technical skills.

- **AI implementation**: Designing AI tools that preserve opportunities for competent exercise of research capacities rather than merely optimizing for efficiency.

- **Professional standards**: Establishing industry-wide standards that recognize the intrinsic value of research capacities beyond their instrumental utility.

## Contrast with Instrumental Views

Developmental Perfectionism should be contrasted with purely instrumental views of capacities. As Ferdman argues, capacity-deskilling is bad because "our human capacities are constitutive of our flourishing," not merely because they're useful for getting work done.

## Implications for Skills Gap Research

Understanding Developmental Perfectionism is critical for addressing the skills gap:

- Provides a normative foundation for why capacity cultivation matters beyond economic utility
- Explains why the [[human-operating-model-gap]] represents more than just a productivity issue
- Highlights why AI adoption strategies should evaluate tools based on their impact on human flourishing
- Offers a framework for distinguishing between benign skill obsolescence and harmful capacity deskilling
