# Developmental Perfectionism

First observed: 2026-07-15 | Last updated: 2026-07-18

## Definition
Developmental Perfectionism is a neo-Aristotelian approach to human flourishing where flourishing is understood as a complex, unified, and well-rounded life. It provides the philosophical foundation for understanding why capacity-deskilling is harmful.

## Key Principles
- Human flourishing involves the competent exercise of core human capacities
- Capacities are intrinsically valuable because their exercise yields intrinsically valuable goods
- The competent exercise of capacities is the manifestation of achievement of intrinsically valuable goods
- Flourishing cannot be achieved by shortcuts; it requires the competent exercise of capacities
- Capacity-deskilling is bad because impoverished capacities lead to impoverished lives

## Evidence from Research
As detailed in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]]:
- "Developmental Perfectionism neatly captures why deskilling is bad: capacity-deskilling is bad because our human capacities are constitutive of our flourishing"
- "The competent exercise of the human capacities is the manifestation of the achievement of the intrinsically valuable goods, making the capacities intrinsically valuable too"
- "The value of being on the summit of [ADDRESS] is gained from the competent exercise of a combination of capacities involved in climbing it, and not from simply being there"

## Implications for Market Research
In market research, Developmental Perfectionism explains why:
- Shortcuts through AI automation may undermine the value of research insights
- The process of developing research capacities is as important as the outcomes
- Junior researchers need opportunities to develop capacities through habituation
- AI tools should enhance rather than replace the competent exercise of research capacities

AI adoption challenges Developmental Perfectionism when it:
- Creates shortcuts that bypass the habituation process necessary for capacity development
- Undermines the value of research capacities by making them seem obsolete
- Separates research outcomes from the process of capacity development
- Reduces opportunities for researchers to experience the intrinsic value of their capacities
