# Developmental Perfectionism

## Definition
Developmental Perfectionism is a neo-Aristotelian approach to human flourishing where flourishing is understood as a complex, unified, and well-rounded life. It provides the philosophical foundation for understanding why capacity-deskilling is harmful.

## Characteristics
- Views human flourishing as the competent exercise of core human capacities
- Understands capacities as constitutive of flourishing rather than merely instrumental
- Emphasizes that the value of activities comes from the competent exercise of capacities
- Recognizes that impoverished capacities lead to impoverished lives

## From Ferdman (2026)
In "AI Deskilling is a Structural Problem," Ferdman uses Developmental Perfectionism to frame the problem of AI-induced deskilling:

1. **Core argument**: "Developmental Perfectionism neatly captures why deskilling is bad: capacity-deskilling is bad because our human capacities are constitutive of our flourishing. Impoverished capacities (i.e., capacity-deskilling), therefore, lead to impoverished lives."

2. **Intrinsic value of capacities**: "The competent exercise of the human capacities is the manifestation of the achievement of the intrinsically valuable goods, making the capacities intrinsically valuable too."

3. **Example**: "The value of being on the summit of Mt. Everest is gained from the competent exercise of a combination of capacities involved in climbing it, and not from simply being there (say, if one arrives there by helicopter)."

4. **Core human capacities**: Ferdman identifies four key capacities:
   - Epistemic capacities (theoretical and practical rationality)
   - Social capacities (moral capacity and capacities for relationships)
   - Creative capacity
   - Volitional capacity (capacity to will)

## Implications for Market Research
- Research practices should be evaluated based on how they support the flourishing of researchers
- AI tools should enhance rather than diminish researchers' capacities
- Organizations should design work environments that support the development of researchers' full capacities
- The industry should recognize that capacity-deskilling leads to diminished professional lives and poorer research outcomes
- Training should focus on developing researchers' capacities rather than just technical skills
