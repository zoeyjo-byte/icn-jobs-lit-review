# Developmental Perfectionism

## Definition
A neo-Aristotelian approach to human flourishing where flourishing is understood as a complex, unified, and well-rounded life, providing the philosophical foundation for understanding why capacity-deskilling is harmful, as detailed in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]].

## Philosophical Foundation
Developmental Perfectionism, as articulated by Kauppinen (2025), conceptualizes human flourishing through several key principles:

- **Complex unity**: Flourishing is not merely the sum of individual goods but a unified, well-rounded life (Hurka 1993)
- **Intrinsic value**: Humans exercise capacities in activities that yield outcomes with intrinsic value like knowledge, beauty, morality, and friendship (Kraut 2013; Bradford 2016, 2021; Fletcher 2016)
- **Competent exercise**: The competent exercise of human capacities manifests the achievement of intrinsically valuable goods, making the capacities themselves intrinsically valuable
- **Developmental process**: Flourishing emerges through a developmental process where capacities are cultivated over time

As Ferdman explains, Developmental Perfectionism "neatly captures why deskilling is bad: capacity-deskilling is bad because our human capacities are constitutive of our flourishing. Impoverished capacities (i.e., capacity-deskilling), therefore, lead to impoverished lives."

## Four Core Human Capacities
Developmental Perfectionism identifies four categories of capacities essential to human flourishing:

1. **Epistemic capacities**:
   - Theoretical rationality: thinking, considering reasons, forming beliefs, contemplating
   - Practical rationality (phronesis/wisdom): doing the right thing in the right way for the right reason

2. **Social capacities**:
   - Moral capacity
   - Social and caring capacities for developing meaningful relationships

3. **Creative capacity**:
   - Making unfamiliar combinations of familiar ideas
   - Transforming conceptual spaces without relying on luck or mechanical procedures

4. **Volitional capacity**:
   - The ability to exert effort, overcome difficulty, persevere and grow
   - Treated as a "meta-capacity" since developing any capacity requires willpower

## Why Deskilling is Harmful
From a Developmental Perfectionist perspective, deskilling is harmful because:

- It diminishes us as human beings by undermining our abilities in the "arts of personhood"
- It prevents us from achieving competence in practical reason, self-worth, and mutual respect
- It weakens our capacity for honesty, justice, and ethical sensitivity
- It leads to "capacity impoverishment," which directly undermines human flourishing

As Ferdman argues, "the deskilling of our core human capacities—to know, create, form meaningful relationships, and use our willpower—is a different matter" than the deskilling of domain-specific skills like hunting or coding. Capacity-deskilling "diminishes us as humans and undermines our flourishing."

## Structural Implications
Developmental Perfectionism has important structural implications:

- It moves beyond individual responsibility to recognize that "we develop and exercise our epistemic, moral, social, and creative capacities in response to our social and material environment"
- It explains why we cannot expect persons to be 'virtuous superheroes' who resist deskilling through willpower alone
- It provides the normative foundation for evaluating socio-technical environments based on their conduciveness to capacity cultivation

## Contrast with Skill Obsolescence
Unlike domain-specific skills where obsolescence can be beneficial (freeing time for other pursuits), capacity-deskilling is always problematic:

> "Whereas deskilling of activities like hunting, basket-weaving or coding may be innocuous, capacity-deskilling should always be a cause for worry. As such, we have reason to ensure that socio-technical environments we inhabit are conducive, rather than hostile, to the cultivation of our human capacities."
