# [[developmental-perfectionism]]

## Definition
Developmental Perfectionism is a neo-Aristotelian approach to human flourishing where flourishing is understood as a complex, unified, and well-rounded life, providing the philosophical foundation for understanding why capacity-deskilling is harmful as established in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]].

## Key Characteristics
According to Ferdman's analysis in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]], Developmental Perfectionism has several critical features:

1. **Complex Unity**: Human flourishing is understood as a complex, unified, and well-rounded life rather than the maximization of isolated goods.

2. **Intrinsic Value**: Humans exercise their capacities in activities that yield outcomes with intrinsic value like knowledge, beauty, morality, and friendship.

3. **Constitutive Relationship**: The competent exercise of human capacities is the manifestation of the achievement of intrinsically valuable goods, making the capacities intrinsically valuable too.

4. **Developmental Process**: Flourishing involves a developmental process where capacities are cultivated and exercised over time.

## Core Human Capacities
Developmental Perfectionism identifies several core human capacities that constitute flourishing:

1. **Epistemic Capacities**: Include theoretical rationality (thinking, considering reasons to believe) and practical rationality (phronesis or practical wisdom).

2. **Social Capacities**: Include the moral capacity as well as social and caring capacities for developing meaningful relationships of friendship and love.

3. **Creative Capacities**: Involve making unfamiliar combinations of familiar ideas, transforming conceptual spaces without relying merely on luck or mechanical procedures.

4. **Volitional Capacity**: The ability to exert effort, overcome difficulty, persevere, and grow to achieve intrinsically valuable goals. This is treated as a meta-capacity necessary for developing all other capacities.

## Why Deskilling is Harmful
Developmental Perfectionism provides the philosophical foundation for understanding why capacity-deskilling is harmful:

1. **Constitutive Relationship**: "Capacity-deskilling is bad because our human capacities are constitutive of our flourishing. Impoverished capacities (i.e., capacity-deskilling), therefore, lead to impoverished lives."

2. **Value of Process**: The value of achievements is gained from the competent exercise of capacities involved, not merely from the outcome (e.g., the value of being on Mt. Everest comes from the climb, not from simply being there).

3. **Holistic Nature**: Human capacities rarely operate in isolation; they typically involve the exercise of multiple capacities simultaneously.

## Contrast with Skill Obsolescence
Developmental Perfectionism distinguishes between innocuous skill obsolescence and harmful capacity-deskilling:

- **Skill Obsolescence**: "Skill obsolescence has always been part of human history, where technological developments free up time and resources for the cultivation of other skills." Deskilling of activities like hunting or basket-weaving may be innocuous.

- **Capacity-Deskilling**: "The deskilling of our core human capacities—to know, create, form meaningful relationships, and use our willpower—is a different matter." Capacity-deskilling should always be a cause for worry.

## Implications for AI Ethics
Developmental Perfectionism provides a framework for evaluating AI systems:

1. **Structural Evaluation**: Rather than focusing only on individual responsibility, we should evaluate what kind of socio-technical environments would be best for the cultivation of our personhood.

2. **Capacity-Conducive Design**: AI systems should be designed to create environments conducive to capacity cultivation rather than hostile to it.

3. **Societal Obligation**: There is a societal obligation to foster capacity-conducive environments in the age of AI, recognizing that deskilling is a structural problem requiring societal solutions.

## Implications for Market Research
In the market research industry, Developmental Perfectionism suggests that:
- AI tools should be evaluated based on their impact on researchers' core human capacities
- Organizations have an obligation to create research environments that foster capacity development
- The value of market research lies not just in the outputs but in the competent exercise of capacities involved in the research process

Organizations should evaluate AI implementations based on whether they support or undermine the cultivation of researchers' human capacities, as outlined in [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]].
