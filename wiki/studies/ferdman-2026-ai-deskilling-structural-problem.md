# [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]]

## Key Findings
- AI systems create **capacity-hostile environments** through systemic conditions rather than individual failings
- Introduces distinction between **agential control** (skill) vs habituation (habit)
- Demonstrates how **Artificial Personal Assistants (APAs)** undermine capacity cultivation by:
  - Replacing plan-forming with plan-following
  - Reducing embodied, intersubjective interactions
  - Creating automation bias in decision-making
- Proposes **Developmental Perfectionism** framework for evaluating AI tools

## Methodology
Philosophical analysis combining:
- Social epistemology
- Skill acquisition theory
- Case studies of LLM usage patterns

## Implications
- Market research must consider structural impacts of AI tools
- Highlights risks of deskilling in epistemic, social, and volitional capacities
- Calls for capacity-conducive environment design principles
