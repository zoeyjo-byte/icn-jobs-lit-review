# [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]]

## Study Overview

Avigail Ferdman's 2026 paper in [[ai-society]], *AI deskilling is a structural problem*, presents a groundbreaking structural analysis of how AI systems undermine human capacities through systemic environmental conditions rather than individual failings. Published in AI & SOCIETY (2026) 41:3001–3013, this research moves beyond framing deskilling as an individual responsibility issue to examine how AI creates environments hostile to capacity cultivation.

## Key Contributions

- Introduces the critical distinction between **capacity-hostile environments** and **capacity-conducive environments**
- Develops a capacity-skill framework showing how human capacities function as skills requiring **agential control** and **habituation**
- Demonstrates how AI's influence creates systemic conditions that inhibit capacity development by undermining habituation processes
- Analyzes **Artificial Personal Assistants (APAs)** as a case study of potentially capacity-hostile technology
- Establishes deskilling as a structural problem requiring societal solutions rather than individual willpower

## Methodology

Ferdman draws on philosophy of skill, social epistemology, and neo-Aristotelian perfectionism to construct a framework for understanding human capacities as skills. The analysis examines how AI mediation affects the conditions necessary for capacity cultivation, particularly focusing on:

- The role of **embodiment** in social cognition and moral attention
- The importance of **intersubjective** learning in skill development
- How automation creates **narrow fields of affordances** that restrict capacity development

## Findings

The paper establishes that AI deskilling occurs through three primary mechanisms:

1. **AI automation narrowing affordance fields**: By automating routine tasks essential for capacity development, AI systems deprive users of "the routine opportunities to practice their judgment and strengthen their cognitive musculature"

2. **Undermining embodied habituation**: Disembodied AI interactions make it "harder to form meaningful friendship" and recognize others as moral agents, as "the spontaneity of recognizing the other as moral agent is inherent in the embodied interaction"

3. **Replacing plan-forming with plan-following**: APAs encourage users to respond to AI-generated plans rather than developing their own planning capacities, undermining "the two functions of planning: settling (deciding between options) and coordinating"

## Implications for Market Research

This research has significant implications for understanding the **human operating model gap** in AI adoption. As market research firms implement AI tools, they risk creating capacity-hostile environments that undermine the very human capacities (critical thinking, moral judgment, creativity) needed to effectively leverage AI. The paper warns against environments that systematically reduce opportunities for developing the [[epistemic-capacities]], [[social-capacities]], and [[creative-capacities]] essential to quality market research.
