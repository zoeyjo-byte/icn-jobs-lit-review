# [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]]

## Study Overview

Avigail Ferdman's 2026 paper in [[ai-society]], "AI deskilling is a structural problem," presents a philosophical analysis of how AI systems undermine human capacities through systemic environmental conditions rather than individual failings. Published in AI & Society (2026) 41:3001–3013, this landmark study challenges the individual responsibility framework for addressing AI-induced deskilling.

## Methodology

Ferdman employs a conceptual analysis drawing on:
- Philosophy of skill (agential control, habituation processes)
- Social epistemology (intersubjective dimensions of learning)
- Developmental Perfectionism (neo-Aristotelian approach to human flourishing)
- Affordance theory (Gibson, 1979) applied to digital environments

The analysis synthesizes insights from Plato's Phaedrus (writing deskilling memory), contemporary studies on AI dependence in healthcare (NHS England 2023), and professional settings (Rinta-Kahila et al. 2023).

## Key Findings

1. **Structural perspective on deskilling**: Capacity-deskilling results from systemic conditions in socio-technical environments, not merely individual choices.

2. **Capacity-hostile environments**: AI systems can create environments that restrict opportunities for developing human capacities through:
   - Narrow fields of affordances
   - Reduced embodied interaction
   - Diminished intersubjective habituation

3. **Critical distinction**: Between **plan-forming** (requiring agential control) and **plan-following** (habitual response to environmental triggers), with AI systems often encouraging the latter.

4. **APA analysis**: Artificial Personal Assistants (APAs) risk creating capacity-hostile environments by:
   - Offloading life-planning activities
   - Reducing opportunities for practical wisdom development
   - Undermining intersubjective habituation of capacities
   - Creating dependency that diminishes volitional capacity

## Core Human Capacities Framework

Ferdman identifies four core human capacities essential for flourishing:

| Capacity Type | Components | AI Vulnerability |
|---------------|-------------|------------------|
| [[epistemic-capacities]] | Theoretical rationality (thinking, considering reasons), Practical rationality (phronesis/wisdom) | Erosion of critical thinking, decision-making, analytical reasoning |
| [[social-capacities]] | Moral capacity, relational capacity (friendship, love) | Diminished moral judgment, reduced ethical sensitivity, weakened capacity for meaningful relationships |
| [[creative-capacities]] | Novel idea generation, transforming conceptual spaces | Reduced innovation capacity, reliance on AI for creative tasks |
| [[volitional-capacity]] | Exerting effort, overcoming difficulty, perseverance | Diminished willpower when AI handles challenging tasks |

## Implications for Market Research

This study provides critical theoretical grounding for understanding the [[human-operating-model-gap]] in AI adoption. It explains why the [[entry-level-squeeze]] occurs: when AI automates foundational tasks that traditionally served as training grounds, it creates a narrow field of affordances for developing professional expertise. The research validates the structural nature of the [[ai-deskilling-structural-problem]], showing why solutions must address environmental conditions rather than individual upskilling alone.
