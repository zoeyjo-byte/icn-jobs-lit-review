# Avigail Ferdman's 2026 Paper: AI Deskilling as a Structural Problem

## Overview
Published in [[ai-society]] (2026) 41:3001–3013, this landmark theoretical paper presents a structural analysis of how AI systems undermine human capacities through systemic environmental conditions rather than individual failings. Ferdman argues that deskilling should be understood as a societal problem requiring structural solutions, not merely an individual responsibility issue.

## Key Contributions

### Capacity-Hostile vs. Capacity-Conducive Environments
Ferdman introduces the critical distinction between:
- **Capacity-hostile environments**: Systems that restrict, limit, or create a narrow field of affordances for capacity development, affording only shallow skilling while restricting fuller development of capacities
- **Capacity-conducive environments**: Systems that actively encourage capacity development through rich opportunities for embodied interaction and shared valuing

### Philosophical Framework
The paper constructs a capacity-skill framework showing that:
- Being capacity-skilled requires **agential control** over human capacities
- Capacity cultivation occurs through **habituation** - a gradual process of inculcation through attunement to individual cases
- Habituation depends on **intersubjectivity** (learning from others who share the value of the skill) and **embodiment** (physical presence in social interaction)

### AI's Structural Impact
Ferdman demonstrates how AI systems can create capacity-hostile environments by:
- Automating routine tasks that deprive users of opportunities to practice judgment and strengthen 'cognitive musculature'
- Encouraging **plan-following** rather than **plan-forming**, reducing users' agential control over life decisions
- Creating disembodied interactions that undermine moral attention and relationship formation
- Offloading critical activities to non-human agents, diminishing opportunities for capacity exercise

## Artificial Personal Assistants Case Study
The paper analyzes how AI-powered personal assistants (like ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini) can become capacity-hostile when users over-rely on them for life-planning. While APAs offer convenience, they risk:
- Replacing self-deliberation with automated recommendations
- Diminishing practical wisdom development by removing real-world experience
- Narrowing the field of affordances for independent planning
- Undermining intersubjective habituation through reduced human interaction

## Implications for Market Research
This structural perspective explains why the [[entry-level-squeeze]] occurs - AI automation of foundational research tasks creates environments hostile to the habituation process required for developing professional expertise. The paper provides the philosophical foundation for understanding the [[human-operating-model-gap]] as a systemic issue rather than individual skill deficiency.
