# Ferdman 2026: AI Deskilling is a Structural Problem

**First Observed:** 2026-07-10
**Last Updated:** 2026-07-18

## Citation

Ferdman, A. (2026). AI deskilling is a structural problem. *AI & Society*, 41, 3001-3013. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00146-025-02686-z

## Overview

This landmark paper presents a structural analysis of how AI systems can undermine human capacities through systemic environmental conditions rather than individual failings. Rather than framing deskilling as a matter of individual responsibility, Ferdman argues for applying a structural perspective that identifies how AI's influence creates systemic conditions inhibiting the development and exercise of human capacities.

## Key Concepts Introduced

### Capacity-Hostile Environments

Environments that restrict, limit, or create a narrow field of affordances for the development and exercise of core human capacities. These environments afford opportunities for shallow skilling while restricting fuller development of capacities.

### Capacity-Conducive Environments

Environments that actively encourage the development and exercise of human capacities through rich opportunities for embodied interaction and shared valuing.

### Capacity-Skill Framework

A framework conceptualizing capacity cultivation as skilling, where being capacity-skilled means having agential control over human capacities obtained through intersubjective, embodied habituation.

## Core Argument

The paper makes these key arguments:

1. **Beyond individual responsibility**: Framing deskilling as an individual ethics problem misses the structural dimensions of the problem

2. **Environmental dependence**: We develop and exercise capacities in response to our social and material environment; deficient environments lead to capacity impoverishment

3. **Structural evaluation**: AI applications should be evaluated based on their conduciveness or hostility to capacity cultivation

4. **Systemic conditions**: AI's influence creates systemic conditions that inhibit capacity development through mechanisms like narrow fields of affordances and reduced embodied interaction

## Methodology

The paper employs philosophical analysis drawing on:

- Philosophy of skill (agential control, habituation process)
- Social epistemology
- Developmental Perfectionism (neo-Aristotelian approach to human flourishing)
- Analysis of AI's structural impact on human activity

## Key Findings

1. **Agential control** is critical for skill development and is undermined when AI systems shift users from plan-formers to plan-followers

2. **Habituation process** requires gradual, embodied, intersubjective learning that AI systems can disrupt

3. **Disembodied environments** created by AI mediation limit opportunities for developing social and moral capacities

4. **Cumulative automation** across multiple spheres creates narrow fields of affordances that discourage users from finding compensatory pathways

5. **Capacity-hostile environments** systematically undermine the development of epistemic, social, creative, and volitional capacities

## Implications for Market Research

The paper has significant implications for the market research industry:

- The [[human-operating-model-gap]] reflects a structural problem of capacity-hostile environments rather than merely an individual skills gap
- The [[entry-level-squeeze]] phenomenon represents how AI automation creates environments hostile to developing foundational research capacities
- Organizations must design capacity-conducive environments that preserve opportunities for developing [[human-intensive-skills]]
- The industry's value proposition increasingly depends on human capacities that AI cannot replicate
- AI adoption strategies should be evaluated based on their impact on capacity cultivation
