# [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]]

## Publication Details
**Title:** AI deskilling is a structural problem  
**Author:** Avigail Ferdman (Technion – Israel Institute of Technology)  
**Journal:** AI & SOCIETY (2026) 41:3001–3013  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s00146-025-02686-z  
**Published:** November 5, 2025  

## Abstract
This paper argues for applying a structural perspective to AI-induced deskilling. It introduces the concept of 'capacity-hostile environments' to identify instances where AI mediation impedes human capacity cultivation. The analysis moves beyond individual responsibility, demonstrating how AI creates systemic conditions that inhibit the development and exercise of human capacities by undermining the habituation process. Drawing on philosophy of skill and social epistemology, the paper argues capacity cultivation requires acquiring agential control through gradual habituation, which depends on learning from others and shared understanding of skill value. AI mediation risks undermining these conditions, leading to capacity impoverishment.

## Key Arguments

### Capacity-Deskilling as Structural Harm
The paper distinguishes between skill obsolescence (which can free time for new skills) and capacity-deskilling (which harms human flourishing). Capacity-deskilling refers to deterioration of core human capacities—to know, create, form meaningful relationships, and use willpower. Inadequate development leads to "capacity impoverishment," diminishing us as humans. Unlike hunting or coding skills, core human capacities are analogous to virtues—their deskilling should always be a cause for concern.

### Framework for Human Capacities as Skills
Two key properties define capacity-skilling:
1. **Agential control**: Ability to adjust execution during performance. Skills require control over initiating tasks, whereas habits respond to environmental triggers.
2. **Habituation**: Gradual process requiring practice, encountering failure, and learning from mistakes. Cannot be learned through testimony alone.

### Structural Dimension of Capacity Impoverishment
Introduces "capacity-hostile environments" that restrict, limit, or narrow affordances for capacity development. These environments afford shallow skilling while restricting well-rounded development. In contrast, "capacity-conducive environments" encourage full capacity cultivation through:
- Embodied interaction
- Intersubjective learning
- Opportunities for agential control

### Artificial Personal Assistants Case Study
Analyzes APAs as fast-growing AI applications that risk creating capacity-hostile environments when users become overly dependent. APAs may:
- Automate routine planning tasks, depriving users of habituation opportunities
- Encourage plan-following rather than plan-forming behavior
- Reduce embodied, intersubjective interactions necessary for capacity development
- Narrow affordance fields through constant availability and helpful design

## Methodology
Draws on philosophy of skill, social epistemology, and neo-Aristotelian human flourishing (Developmental Perfectionism). Analyzes AI through affordance theory, examining how design creates environments supporting or undermining capacity development.

## Implications for Market Research
This research critically impacts market research, which relies on human capacities for:
- [[epistemic-capacities]] in data interpretation
- [[social-capacities]] in client relationships
- [[creative-capacities]] in research design
- [[volitional-capacity]] in overcoming research challenges

As AI integrates into research workflows, firms must create [[capacity-conducive-environments]] preserving opportunities for researchers to develop these capacities. The paper argues deskilling is a structural problem requiring societal solutions, not individual responsibility.
