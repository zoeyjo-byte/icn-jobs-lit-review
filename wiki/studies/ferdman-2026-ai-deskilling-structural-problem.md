# [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]]

## Overview
Avigail Ferdman's 2026 paper in [[ai-society]], "AI deskilling is a structural problem," presents a structural analysis of how AI systems can undermine human capacities through systemic environmental conditions rather than individual failings. Published in AI & Society (2026) 41:3001–3013, this philosophical work challenges the framing of AI-induced deskilling as merely an individual responsibility issue.

## Key Contributions
Ferdman introduces the critical distinction between [[capacity-hostile-environments]] and [[capacity-conducive-environments]], arguing that AI's impact on human capacities depends on the structural conditions it creates. The paper analyzes how the process of capacity development requires [[agential-control]], [[habituation-process]], and [[intersubjectivity-in-skilling]], all of which can be undermined by AI systems.

A central insight is that deskilling occurs not merely through the replacement of human activity, but through the creation of environments with a [[narrow-field-of-affordances]] that restrict opportunities for developing and exercising human capacities. Ferdman particularly examines how [[artificial-personal-assistants]] may create capacity-hostile environments by encouraging users to become [[plan-followers]] rather than [[plan-formers]].

## Philosophical Framework
The paper employs a [[developmental-perfectionism]] framework to argue that human capacities are constitutive of flourishing. Ferdman identifies four core [[human-capacities]] that are essential to being human:
- [[epistemic-capacities]] (theoretical and practical rationality)
- [[social-capacities]] (moral and relational capacities)
- [[creative-capacities]] 
- [[volitional-capacity]] (the meta-capacity required for developing all others)

## Implications
Ferdman argues that addressing AI deskilling requires societal solutions rather than placing the burden solely on individuals to resist technological influences. The paper calls for evaluating AI applications based on their conduciveness or hostility to capacity cultivation and emphasizes the societal obligation to foster capacity-conducive environments in the age of AI.
