# [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]]

**Published in:** AI & Society (2026) 41:3001-3013  
**Author:** Avigail Ferdman  
**Institution:** Technion - Israel Institute of Technology  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s00146-025-02686-z

## Abstract

This paper argues for applying a structural perspective to AI-induced deskilling. It introduces the concept of 'capacity-hostile environments' to identify instances where AI mediation impedes human capacity cultivation. The analysis moves beyond individual responsibility, demonstrating how AI's influence creates systemic conditions that inhibit the development and exercise of human capacities by undermining the process of capacity cultivation. Drawing on the philosophy of skill and social epistemology, the paper argues that capacity cultivation includes acquiring agential control over capacities, inculcated through a gradual process of habituation. Habituation depends on learning from others: the 'know how' of the skill, as well as a shared understanding of the value of the skill. AI mediation risks undermining the quality of conditions for capacity habituation, leading to capacity impoverishment.

## Key Contributions

### 1. Core Human Capacities Framework

Ferdman identifies four core human capacities essential for flourishing:

- **Epistemic capacities**: Theoretical rationality (thinking, considering reasons, forming beliefs) and practical rationality (phronesis/wisdom)
- **Social capacities**: Moral capacity and capacities for developing meaningful relationships
- **Creative capacities**: Making unfamiliar combinations of familiar ideas, transforming conceptual spaces
- **Volitional capacity**: The meta-capacity to exert effort, overcome difficulty, persevere and grow to achieve intrinsically valuable goals

### 2. Capacity-Skill Framework

The paper conceptualizes human capacities as skills with two key properties:

- **Agential control**: The ability to adjust execution of an action as it unfolds, including control over initiating the task (distinguishing skills from habits)
- **Habituation**: The gradual process of becoming skilled through practice, involving inculcation through continued attunement to demands of individual cases

### 3. Structural Dimension of Deskilling

Ferdman argues deskilling should be understood as a structural problem, not merely an individual responsibility issue. Key concepts include:

- **Capacity-hostile environments**: Restrict, limit, or create narrow fields of affordances for capacity development
- **Capacity-conducive environments**: Actively encourage capacity development through rich opportunities for embodied interaction
- **Narrow field of affordances**: Environments providing limited action possibilities that restrict capacity development

### 4. Artificial Personal Assistants Analysis

The paper applies the framework to Artificial Personal Assistants (APAs), arguing they may create capacity-hostile environments by:

- Encouraging plan-following rather than plan-forming
- Reducing opportunities for embodied, intersubjective habituation
- Offloading small-scale decisions crucial for developing practical wisdom
- Creating dependency that undermines the volitional capacity

## Methodology

The paper employs philosophical analysis drawing on:
- Neo-Aristotelian perfectionism ([[developmental-perfectionism]])
- Philosophy of skill and habituation
- Social epistemology
- Affordance theory

## Implications for Market Research

This research has significant implications for the market research industry:

1. **Research Design**: Studies on AI adoption must consider structural environmental factors, not just individual behaviors
2. **Skills Assessment**: Traditional skills frameworks may need to incorporate capacity-based metrics
3. **Workforce Development**: Training programs should focus on creating capacity-conducive environments
4. **Ethical Guidelines**: Research ethics frameworks should address capacity-hostile environments
5. **Client Advisory**: Insights professionals can help clients evaluate AI tools based on their conduciveness to capacity cultivation
