# Ferdman 2026: AI Deskilling as a Structural Problem

Avigail Ferdman's 2026 paper in AI & Society presents a structural analysis of how AI systems can undermine human capacities through systemic environmental conditions rather than individual failings. The study introduces the critical distinction between [[capacity-hostile-environments]] and [[capacity-conducive-environments]], arguing that AI's impact on human skills is determined by organizational design choices rather than inherent technological properties. Ferdman demonstrates how AI systems create environments that restrict opportunities for developing core human capacities like [[agential-control]] and [[intersubjectivity-in-skilling]], particularly in knowledge work settings. The research challenges individualistic explanations of skill degradation, showing how workplace structures shaped by AI adoption systematically erode the conditions necessary for cultivating human expertise through [[habituation-process]]. This framework connects to the broader [[ai-deskilling-structural-problem]] concept, highlighting how organizational choices around AI implementation can create self-reinforcing cycles of capacity erosion.
