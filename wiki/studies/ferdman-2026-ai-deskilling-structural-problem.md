# [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]]

## Study Overview
Avigail Ferdman's 2026 paper in AI & Society presents a structural analysis of AI-induced deskilling. The study argues that AI systems create "capacity-hostile environments" that systematically undermine human capacity development through environmental constraints rather than individual failings.

## Key Contributions
- Introduces **capacity-hostile environments** as systemic conditions where AI mediation impedes human capacity cultivation
- Develops **Developmental Perfectionism** framework linking human flourishing to epistemic, social, creative, and volitional capacities
- Distinguishes between **plan-forming** (agential control) and **plan-following** (habitual action) in AI interaction
- Analyzes Artificial Personal Assistants (APAs) as paradigmatic capacity-hostile systems

## Methodology
Philosophical analysis combining:
- Neo-Aristotelian virtue ethics
- Social epistemology
- Enactive cognition theory
- Affordance theory

## Findings
1. AI systems create "narrow fields of affordances" that limit opportunities for capacity development
2. Automation erodes **agential control** through habituation processes
3. **Intersubjective habituation** is compromised when AI replaces human mentorship
4. **Embodiment** is critical for social and moral capacity development

## Implications
- Calls for societal responsibility in creating **capacity-conducive environments**
- Proposes evaluation frameworks for AI systems based on their impact on human capacities
- Highlights risks of over-reliance on APAs for life planning and decision-making
