## Update (2026-08-20)

Added detailed analysis of Ferdman's structural approach to AI deskilling:

- Published in [[ai-society]] (2026) 41:3001-3013
- Methodology: Philosophical analysis drawing on skill philosophy, social epistemology, and Aristotelian virtue ethics
- Key findings:
  - Introduced the concept of "capacity-hostile environments" where AI mediation impedes human capacity cultivation
  - Distinguished between "plan-forming" (requiring [[agential-control]]) and "plan-following" (more habitual)
  - Analyzed how [[artificial-personal-assistants]] may create capacity-hostile environments by encouraging plan-following
  - Identified four core human capacities at risk: [[epistemic-capacities]], [[social-capacities]], [[creative-capacities]], and [[volitional-capacity]]
  - Argued that capacity cultivation requires [[intersubjectivity-in-skilling]] and [[embodiment-in-skilling]]
- Implications: AI applications should be evaluated based on their conduciveness to capacity cultivation; societal obligation to foster capacity-conducive environments
- Notable evidence: Studies show smartphone users struggle with "everyday but essential" skills like empathy and critical thinking (Halliday 2025), with LLMs potentially exacerbating this deskilling
