# Ferdman (2026): AI Deskilling is a Structural Problem

**Published in**: AI & Society (2026) 41:3001–3013
**Author**: Avigail Ferdman (Technion – Israel Institute of Technology)
**DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1007/s00146-025-02686-z

## Research Methodology

Ferdman's study employs a philosophical analysis framework drawing on the philosophy of skill, social epistemology, and neo-Aristotelian ethics. The research synthesizes insights from multiple disciplines to develop a structural perspective on AI-induced deskilling, moving beyond individual responsibility frameworks. The methodology involves:

1. Conceptual analysis of "skill" and "capacity" as they relate to human flourishing
2. Examination of the habituation process in skill development
3. Analysis of environmental affordances and their impact on capacity cultivation
4. Case study of Artificial Personal Assistants (APAs) as a growing AI application

## Key Findings

The paper introduces the critical distinction between **capacity-hostile environments** and **capacity-conducive environments**, arguing that AI systems can create environments that systematically undermine human capacity development. Key findings include:

- Deskilling of core human capacities ([[epistemic-capacities]], [[social-capacities]], [[creative-capacities]], [[volitional-capacity]]) is harmful because these capacities are constitutive of human flourishing
- Capacity development requires **agential control**, **embodied interaction**, and **intersubjective habituation** - elements that AI mediation can undermine
- The process of becoming skilled involves a gradual habituation that cannot be learned through testimony alone but requires practice, encountering and overcoming disappointment and failure
- Artificial Personal Assistants (APAs) risk creating capacity-hostile environments by encouraging users to become **plan-followers** rather than **plan-formers**
- AI automation can create a "narrow field of affordances" that restricts opportunities for developing and exercising human capacities

## Market Research Implications

This research has profound implications for the market research industry:

1. **Research Design**: Market researchers must consider how AI tools might be creating capacity-hostile environments for both researchers and research participants. Traditional qualitative methods relying on embodied interaction may be particularly vulnerable.

2. **Methodology Evaluation**: Research firms should evaluate AI tools not just for efficiency but for their impact on researchers' capacity development. Tools that automate the habituation process necessary for developing research skills could be detrimental long-term.

3. **Participant Interaction**: The shift toward digital and AI-mediated research methods may be creating disembodied environments that undermine the development of social capacities necessary for effective research.

4. **Capacity-Conducive Alternatives**: Market research organizations should design research workflows that maintain opportunities for embodied, intersubjective habituation of research capacities, even when using AI tools.
