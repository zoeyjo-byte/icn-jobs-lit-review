# [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]]

## Publication Details
- **Journal**: AI & Society (2026) 41:3001–3013
- **Author**: Avigail Ferdman, Technion – Israel Institute of Technology
- **DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1007/s00146-025-02686-z
- **Published**: November 5, 2025

## Key Findings

This landmark study presents a structural analysis of how AI systems can undermine human capacities through systemic environmental conditions rather than individual failings. Ferdman introduces the critical distinction between [[capacity-hostile-environments]] and [[capacity-conducive-environments]], arguing that AI's influence creates systemic conditions that inhibit the development and exercise of human capacities by undermining the process of capacity cultivation.

The paper draws on philosophy of skill and social epistemology to argue that capacity cultivation (skilling) includes acquiring [[agential-control]] over capacities, inculcated through a long, gradual process of [[habituation-process]]. Habituation depends on learning from others, including both the "know how" of the skill and a shared understanding of the value of the skill.

Ferdman applies this framework to [[artificial-personal-assistants]], analyzing how these rapidly growing AI applications may create capacity-hostile environments by encouraging users to become plan-followers rather than [[plan-forming-vs-plan-following]]. The study demonstrates how AI mediation risks undermining the quality of conditions for capacity habituation, leading to capacity impoverishment.

## Methodology

The study employs philosophical analysis grounded in neo-Aristotelian [[developmental-perfectionism]], which understands human flourishing as a complex, unified, and well-rounded life. Ferdman examines how the competent exercise of human capacities yields outcomes with intrinsic value, making the capacities themselves intrinsically valuable.

## Implications for Market Research

This research has significant implications for understanding the [[human-operating-model-gap]] in market research organizations adopting AI. It provides a theoretical foundation for why the [[entry-level-squeeze]] phenomenon occurs, as AI systems automate foundational tasks that historically served as training grounds for developing professional expertise.

The study suggests that market research firms must carefully evaluate AI applications based on their conduciveness or hostility to capacity cultivation, particularly for developing the [[epistemic-capacities]] and [[social-capacities]] essential to quality insights work.
