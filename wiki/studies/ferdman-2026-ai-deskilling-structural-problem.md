# [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]]

## Study Overview

Avigail Ferdman's 2026 paper in [[ai-society]] presents a structural analysis of how AI systems undermine human capacities through systemic environmental conditions rather than individual failings. Published in AI & Society (2026) 41:3001–3013, this research challenges the individual responsibility framework for addressing AI-induced deskilling, arguing instead that deskilling should be understood as a structural problem requiring societal solutions.

## Key Findings

The paper introduces the critical distinction between **capacity-hostile environments** and **capacity-conducive environments**, demonstrating how AI mediation creates systemic conditions that inhibit human capacity development. Ferdman identifies that AI deskilling occurs through three primary mechanisms:

1. **Narrowing of affordance fields**: AI automation restricts action possibilities, particularly through offloading routine tasks that are essential for habituation of capacities
2. **Reduction of embodied interaction**: Digital mediation diminishes opportunities for embodied sense-making critical to social and moral capacities
3. **Undermining of intersubjectivity**: AI systems replace human-to-human guidance necessary for shared valuing of capacities

The research specifically examines how **artificial personal assistants (APAs)** create capacity-hostile environments by encouraging **plan-following** rather than **plan-forming**. When users outsource life-planning to AI systems like ChatGPT or Sunsama, they lose opportunities to develop **agential control** over their decision-making processes. As Ferdman states: "When much of the planning process is offloaded to the APA, it arguably requires a shallower exercise of the capacities that are ordinarily involved in making coherent plans."

## Methodology

Ferdman employs a philosophical analysis drawing on:
- Skill theory (particularly the works of Sherman, Dreyfus, and Pavese)
- Social epistemology
- Neo-Aristotelian perfectionism
- Affordance theory

The analysis constructs a capacity-skill framework where human capacities are understood as skills requiring **agential control** and developed through **habituation**. This framework is then applied structurally to examine how AI systems shape environments for capacity development.

## Implications for Market Research

This research has profound implications for the market research industry:
- AI tools that automate data interpretation may create **capacity-hostile environments** for developing **epistemic capacities**
- Reduced face-to-face interaction in remote research settings may undermine **embodiment in skilling** necessary for nuanced qualitative analysis
- Overreliance on AI for research design could erode **creative capacities** needed for innovative methodology
- The shift toward automated reporting threatens **intersubjectivity in skilling** between junior and senior researchers

The paper concludes that addressing AI deskilling requires societal solutions focused on designing **capacity-conducive environments** rather than expecting individuals to be "virtuous superheroes" who resist deskilling through willpower alone. This structural perspective is critical for the market research industry as it navigates AI adoption while preserving the human capacities essential to quality insights.
