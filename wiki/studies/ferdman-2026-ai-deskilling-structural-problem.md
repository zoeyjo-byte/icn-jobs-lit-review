# [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]]

## Overview
Avigail Ferdman's 2026 paper in [[ai-society]] presents a structural analysis of how AI systems can undermine human capacities through systemic environmental conditions rather than individual failings. The paper introduces the critical distinction between [[capacity-hostile-environments]] and [[capacity-conducive-environments]], analyzes the role of [[agential-control]] and [[habituation-process]] in capacity development, and examines how [[artificial-personal-assistants]] may create capacity-hostile environments by encouraging [[plan-following]] rather than [[plan-forming-vs-plan-following]].

## Key Arguments

### Capacity-Hostile Environments
Ferdman defines capacity-hostile environments as those that "restrict, limit or create a narrow field of affordances for the development and exercise of core human capacities." These environments afford opportunities for shallow skilling while restricting fuller development of capacities, with significant implications for AI's impact on human flourishing.

### Capacity-Conducive Environments
In contrast, capacity-conducive environments "actively encourage the development and exercise of human capacities through rich opportunities for embodied interaction and shared valuing." These environments afford structured and unstructured embodied interactions necessary for intersubjective habituation of capacities.

### Agential Control in Skill Development
The paper emphasizes that "to be skilled in something is to have agential control over it, the ability to adjust the execution of the action as the performance unfolds." This distinguishes skills from habits, as skills require control over initiating the task, whereas habits are merely triggered by environmental cues.

### Habituation Process
Ferdman details the "gradual process of becoming skilled through practice, involving inculcation through continued attunement to demands of individual cases." The paper stresses that skill development "cannot be learned through testimony alone but requires practice, encountering and overcoming disappointment and failure."

### Intersubjective Dimension of Skilling
The paper identifies that "skill habituation has a 'shared understanding' dimension" where "becoming skilled typically requires a mentor to instill not only mechanical aspects but also the value of the skill." This creates "shared valuing" of the capacity between mentor and mentee.

### Embodiment in Capacity Development
Ferdman argues that "being embodied is critical for becoming skilled" as "the process of 'shared valuing' of the skill requires some degree of doing something with the mentor(s), creating a shared history." Without embodied interaction, it becomes "much more difficult to achieve" meaningful skill development.

### AI Personal Assistants as Capacity-Hostile
The paper provides a detailed analysis of how [[artificial-personal-assistants]] can create capacity-hostile environments by:
- Encouraging users to become "plan-followers rather than plan-formers"
- Reducing opportunities for embodied interaction
- Limiting intersubjective habituation of capacities
- Creating narrow fields of affordances for capacity development

For example, Ferdman examines scheduling apps like Sunsama that "display tasks synced from various software" and "intelligently reschedule them," arguing that such tools can undermine users' development of practical wisdom by offloading small-scale decision-making.

## Methodological Approach
Ferdman employs a philosophical framework drawing on:
- Neo-Aristotelian "Developmental Perfectionism" to define human flourishing
- Philosophy of skill to analyze capacity development
- Social epistemology to examine intersubjective dimensions of learning
- Affordance theory to analyze environmental influences on capacity cultivation

## Implications for Market Research
This structural perspective on AI deskilling has significant implications for the market research industry, particularly regarding:
- The potential erosion of [[epistemic-capacities]] and analytical reasoning skills among researchers
- The risk of [[entry-level-squeeze]] as AI automates foundational tasks that historically served as training grounds
- The importance of maintaining capacity-conducive environments for developing research expertise
- The need to evaluate AI tools based on their conduciveness to capacity cultivation rather than just efficiency metrics
