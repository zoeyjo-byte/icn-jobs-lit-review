# Ferdman 2026: AI Deskilling is a Structural Problem

## Publication Details
- **Title**: AI Deskilling is a Structural Problem
- **Author**: Avigail Ferdman ([ADDRESS] – Israel Institute of Technology)
- **Journal**: AI & SOCIETY (2026) 41:3001–3013
- **DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1007/s00146-025-02686-z
- **Received**: 24 June 2025
- **Accepted**: 6 October 2025
- **Published online**: 5 November 2025

## Research Question
How does AI mediation create systemic conditions that inhibit the development and exercise of human capacities, rather than merely representing an individual responsibility problem?

## Methodology
Philosophical analysis drawing on:
- Philosophy of skill
- Social epistemology
- Developmental Perfectionism (neo-Aristotelian approach to human flourishing)
- Affordance theory from ecological psychology

## Key Concepts Introduced
- **Capacity-hostile environments**: Environments that restrict, limit, or create a narrow field of affordances for human capacity development
- **Capacity-conducive environments**: Environments that actively encourage capacity development through embodied interaction and intersubjective learning
- **Agential control**: The ability to adjust action execution as performance unfolds, distinguishing skills from habits
- **Habituation process**: The gradual development of skills through practice, with intersubjective and embodied components

## Core Argument
The paper argues that AI deskilling is not merely an individual problem but a structural one:

> "Many artificial intelligence tools replace or stand to replace human activity, via automated decision-making, recommender systems and content generation. The more artificial intelligence (AI) replaces valuable human activity, the more it risks deskilling humans of their human capacities. This paper argues for applying a structural perspective to this phenomenon."

Ferdman moves beyond framing deskilling as individual responsibility to demonstrate how "AI's influence creates systemic conditions that could inhibit the development and exercise of human capacities by undermining the process of capacity cultivation."

## Four Core Human Capacities
The paper identifies four categories of capacities constitutive of human flourishing:

1. **Epistemic capacities**: Theoretical and practical rationality
2. **Social capacities**: Moral capacity and social/caring capacities
3. **Creative capacity**: Making unfamiliar combinations of ideas
4. **Volitional capacity**: Ability to exert effort and overcome difficulty

## Mechanisms of Structural Deskilling
The paper identifies how AI systems undermine capacity development through:

1. **Creating narrow fields of affordances**:
   - Automation of routine tasks deprives users of opportunities to practice judgment
   - Disembodied interfaces fracture users' affordance space
   - Cumulative effect across multiple domains discourages compensation

2. **Undermining habituation**:
   - Reducing opportunities for embodied interaction
   - Diminishing intersubjective learning with mentors
   - Shifting users from plan-forming to plan-following

3. **Devaluing capacities**:
   - When persons develop capacities shallowly, they cannot learn to value mastery
   - Societal devaluation occurs when enough persons don't value capacity excellence

## Artificial Personal Assistants Case Study
The paper analyzes APAs as a fast-growing use case that risks creating capacity-hostile environments:

- APAs afford replacing "many of the activities involved in both planning and executing one's plans"
- They encourage plan-following rather than plan-forming
- They reduce embodied, intersubjective interaction
- They may acclimate users to "merely responding to triggers in the environment"

## Key Findings
1. Deskilling of core human capacities is harmful because it leads to "capacity impoverishment" that diminishes us as humans

2. We cannot expect persons to be "virtuous superheroes" who resist deskilling through willpower alone

3. The structural perspective reveals that capacity development depends on environmental conditions

4. Socio-technical environments should be evaluated based on their conduciveness to capacity cultivation

## Implications for Market Research
- AI-augmented research tools must preserve opportunities for researchers to exercise agential control
- Habituation processes for research skills require embodied interaction and intersubjective learning
- Capacity-hostile research environments could undermine researchers' practical wisdom
- The structural perspective explains why individual training may be insufficient to address AI's impact on research capacities
