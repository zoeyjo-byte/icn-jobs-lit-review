## Update 2026-07-25

Expanded analysis of how AI systems undermine human capacities through structural conditions rather than individual failings. The paper introduces the critical distinction between [[capacity-hostile-environments]] and [[capacity-conducive-environments]], analyzing how AI mediation can create environments with narrow fields of affordances that restrict opportunities for capacity development.

Key findings:
- Deskilling is not merely an individual responsibility issue but a structural problem requiring societal solutions
- Capacity cultivation (skilling) includes acquiring [[agential-control]] over capacities through a gradual process of [[habituation-process]]
- Habituation depends on learning from others: both the "know how" of the skill and a shared understanding of the value of the skill
- AI mediation risks undermining the quality of conditions for capacity habituation, leading to capacity impoverishment
- [[artificial-personal-assistants]] may create capacity-hostile environments by encouraging plan-following rather than plan-forming

The paper argues for evaluating AI applications based on their conduciveness or hostility to capacity cultivation, and calls for societal obligation to foster capacity-conducive environments in the age of AI.
