## Key Findings

This seminal paper establishes AI deskilling as a structural problem rather than merely an individual responsibility issue. Ferdman introduces the critical distinction between 'capacity-hostile environments' and 'capacity-conducive environments' to analyze how AI systems undermine human capacities through systemic conditions.

The research identifies four core human capacities at risk from AI deskilling:
- **Epistemic capacities**: Theoretical rationality (thinking, considering reasons) and practical rationality/phronesis (doing the right thing in the right way)
- **Social capacities**: Moral capacity and relational capacities for meaningful connections
- **Creative capacities**: Generating novel ideas and transforming conceptual spaces
- **Volitional capacity**: Exerting effort, overcoming difficulty, and persevering toward intrinsically valuable goals

Ferdman demonstrates that capacity cultivation (skilling) requires:
1. **Agential control**: Adjusting actions during performance
2. **Habituation**: Gradual skill development through practice
3. **Intersubjectivity**: Learning from others and shared valuation
4. **Embodiment**: Physical presence in skill development

The paper examines how Artificial Personal Assistants (APAs) exemplify capacity-hostile environments by encouraging 'plan-following' rather than 'plan-forming,' thereby undermining practical wisdom development.

## Methodology

Ferdman employs philosophical analysis integrating:
- Philosophy of skill (agential control, habituation)
- Social epistemology
- Developmental Perfectionism (neo-Aristotelian flourishing framework)
- Affordance theory (environmental action possibilities)

The analysis moves beyond individual responsibility to demonstrate how AI creates systemic conditions that inhibit capacity development, using case studies of AI mediation in life-planning activities.

## Market Research Implications

This research has profound implications for market research:
- AI tools for automated insight generation risk deskilling researchers' epistemic capacities
- Over-reliance on AI for data interpretation undermines development of practical wisdom
- Automated reporting systems may create narrow fields of affordances for researchers
- Research organizations must design capacity-conducive environments preserving intersubjective learning
- Market researchers should evaluate AI tools based on their conduciveness to capacity cultivation
