# [[ferdman-2026-ai-deskilling-structural-problem]]

## Publication Details
**Journal**: AI & Society (2026) 41:3001-3013  
**DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1007/s00146-025-02686-z  
**Author**: Avigail Ferdman (Technion - Israel Institute of Technology)  
**Published**: November 5, 2025  

## Abstract
This paper argues for applying a structural perspective to AI-induced deskilling. It introduces the concept of 'capacity-hostile environments' to identify instances where AI mediation impedes human capacity cultivation. The analysis moves beyond individual responsibility, demonstrating how AI's influence creates systemic conditions that inhibit the development and exercise of human capacities by undermining the process of capacity cultivation. Drawing on the philosophy of skill and social epistemology, the paper argues that capacity cultivation includes acquiring agential control over capacities, inculcated through a long, gradual process of habituation that depends on learning from others. AI mediation risks undermining the quality of conditions for capacity habituation, leading to capacity impoverishment.

## Key Arguments

### Core Human Capacities Framework
Ferdman identifies four core human capacities that constitute flourishing:
- **Epistemic capacities**: Theoretical rationality (thinking, considering reasons) and practical rationality/phronesis (doing the right thing in the right way)
- **Social capacities**: Moral capacity and capacities for developing meaningful relationships
- **Creative capacities**: Making unfamiliar combinations of familiar ideas
- **Volitional capacity**: The meta-capacity to exert effort, overcome difficulty, and persevere to achieve intrinsically valuable goals

### Capacity-Skilling Framework
The paper proposes that being capacity-skilled involves:
- Having **agential control** over human capacities
- Obtained through **intersubjective, embodied habituation**
- Manifested in competent exercise of capacities
- Supported by societal **shared valuing** of capacities

### Structural Dimension of Deskilling
Ferdman argues deskilling should be viewed as a structural issue, not individual responsibility:
- Environments can be **capacity-hostile** (restricting capacity development) or **capacity-conducive**
- AI systems create **narrow fields of affordances** that limit capacity exercise opportunities
- **Automation** of routine tasks deprives users of habituation necessary for capacity development
- **Disembodied interaction** limits intersubjective habituation of social/moral capacities

### Artificial Personal Assistants Case Study
The paper analyzes how AI personal assistants (APAs) create capacity-hostile environments:
- APAs encourage **plan-following** rather than **plan-forming**, reducing agential control
- Over-reliance undermines development of **practical wisdom** through real-world experience
- Constant availability discourages seeking alternative growth avenues
- Reduces **embodied, intersubjective interaction** necessary for capacity development

## Methodology
Philosophical analysis drawing on:
- Philosophy of skill (agential control, habituation process)
- Social epistemology (intersubjective learning dimension)
- Enactive cognition approach (embodiment in social cognition)
- Developmental Perfectionism (neo-Aristotelian flourishing framework)

## Implications for Market Research
This research has significant implications:
- AI tools in research (data analysis, report generation) may create capacity-hostile environments
- Over-automation could undermine critical thinking and practical wisdom among researchers
- Organizations should evaluate AI based on conduciveness to capacity cultivation
- Training programs must emphasize agential control when using AI tools
- Research designs should preserve opportunities for embodied learning
