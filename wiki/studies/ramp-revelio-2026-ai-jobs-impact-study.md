# Ramp-Revelio 2026 AI Jobs Impact Study

Published: June 30, 2026

## Overview

The Ramp-Revelio 2026 AI Jobs Impact Study is a comprehensive analysis of how AI adoption affects employment patterns across 21,559 U.S. firms. Unlike previous research that relied on occupational exposure measures or surveys, this study uses observed AI spending data from Ramp's financial platform linked to Revelio Labs workforce records, providing direct evidence of firm-level AI adoption and its workforce consequences.

## Methodology

The study defines AI adoption as the first month of the earliest three-consecutive-month spell where AI spend is at least $100 per month. Adoption intensity is measured as monthly AI spend per employee over the first three months after adoption begins, with high-intensity adopters in the top tercile ($33.67/month) and low-intensity adopters in the bottom two terciles ($2.78/month).

The researchers employed the Callaway-Sant'Anna difference-in-differences framework to compare adopters to later adopters in the same intensity group, controlling for sector fixed effects to address selection bias.

## Key Findings

- **High-intensity AI adopters grew total headcount by 10.2%** over the first 24 months after adoption, while low-intensity adopters showed no statistically significant change
- **Entry-level headcount rose 12.0%** among high-intensity adopters, countering predictions that AI would reduce junior employment
- Employment gains emerged gradually, following a learning curve, with effects compounding over time
- Gains were broad across roles: 10.3% higher sales headcount, 7.8% higher administrative headcount, 7.3% higher engineering headcount
- Sector-level gains were concentrated in the Information sector (13.4% headcount growth), with minimal effects in other sectors

## Implications for Market Research

This study provides concrete evidence that AI adoption, particularly at high intensity, is associated with employment growth rather than job loss. For market research professionals, this suggests AI is currently augmenting human capabilities rather than replacing them, with high-impact use cases concentrated in information-intensive sectors.

The findings challenge simplistic narratives about AI's labor market effects and highlight the importance of distinguishing between low- and high-intensity adoption patterns. For market research firms, this suggests strategic AI investment could support growth rather than necessitate workforce reduction.
