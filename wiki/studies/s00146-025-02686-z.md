# AI Deskilling is a Structural Problem

**Study ID:** s00146-025-02686-z
**Author:** Avigail Ferdman
**Journal:** AI & SOCIETY (2026) 41:3001–3013
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s00146-025-02686-z
**Published:** 5 November 2025

## Abstract
This paper argues for applying a structural perspective to [[concepts/ai-deskilling|AI deskilling]]. It introduces the concept of '[[concepts/capacity-hostile-environments|capacity-hostile environments]]' to identify instances where AI mediation impedes human capacity cultivation. The analysis moves beyond individual responsibility, demonstrating how AI’s influence creates systemic conditions that inhibit the development and exercise of human capacities by undermining the process of capacity cultivation. Drawing on the philosophy of skill and [[concepts/social-epistemology|social epistemology]], it argues that capacity cultivation (skilling) includes acquiring agential control over capacities, inculcated through a long, gradual process of habituation.

## Key Findings
- AI dependence leads to the erosion of activity awareness, competence maintenance, and output assessment.
- Deskilling of core human capacities (to know, create, form relationships, use willpower) leads to "capacity impoverishment," diminishing human flourishing.
- Framing deskilling as an individual ethics problem misses the structural dimensions; a well-ordered socio-technical environment is necessary for flourishing.
- [[entities/artificial-personal-assistants|Artificial Personal Assistants (APAs)]] can create capacity-hostile environments by narrowing the field of affordances and discouraging embodied, intersubjective habituation.
- Life-planning is a self-constituting activity requiring epistemic, social, moral, creative, and volitional capacities; outsourcing it to APAs risks deskilling.

## Methodology
The study employs a philosophical analysis grounded in [[concepts/developmental-perfectionism|Developmental Perfectionism]], the philosophy of skill, and the [[concepts/enactive-approach|Enactive Approach]] in cognition. It analyzes AI tools through the lens of affordances, examining how socio-technical environments either support or hinder the cultivation of human capacities.
