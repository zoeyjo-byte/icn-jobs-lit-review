# Enactive Approach

The **enactive approach** in cognitive science posits that cognition is not merely a computational process occurring inside the brain, but emerges through dynamic interactions between an agent and its environment. A key tenet of this approach is **participatory sense-making**, which emphasizes that social cognition and meaning are generated through the interplay of unfolding interactions and the individuals engaged in them.

## Embodied Interaction
Central to the enactive approach is the necessity of **embodiment** in cognitive and moral development. Recognizing others as moral agents relies on spontaneous, embodied interactions involving gaze, voice, and physical presence. These interactions create an "embodied understanding" where meaning is co-constructed. Disembodied interactions, such as those mediated by screens or AI chatbots, require significantly more mental effort to attribute moral agency and form meaningful relationships.

## Relevance to AI and Deskilling
When applied to AI-mediated environments, the enactive approach highlights the risks of **[[affordance-shrinkage]]** and **[[capacity-hostile-environments]]**. If human interaction is primarily displaced by digital or AI-mediated substitutes, the opportunities for embodied, intersubjective **[[embodied-habituation]]** are diminished. This can lead to a shallow development of **[[social-capacities]]** and moral attention, as the rich, emergent dynamics of face-to-face engagement are replaced by predictable, algorithmically filtered exchanges. The enactive framework thus provides a methodological basis for arguing that AI systems should be designed to encourage, rather than replace, embodied social practices to preserve human flourishing.
